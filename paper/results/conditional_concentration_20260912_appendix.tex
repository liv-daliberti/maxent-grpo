\subsection{Conditional concentration and sampling dependence}
\label{app:conditional-concentration}

Conditional concentration measures repetition of solution keys among correct
outputs. For a fixed prompt with correctness probability $P>0$, write
$q_c=\Pr[V(Y,s_x)=c\mid V(Y,s_x)\ne\bot]$ and define
\begin{equation}
 C(q)=\sum_c q_c^2,\qquad \bar D_2=2P-P^2C(q).
 \label{eq:collision-breadth-bridge}
\end{equation}
Here $C$ is the probability that two independent correct outputs share a
key, and $\bar D_2$ is the expected number of distinct verified keys in two
outputs. The identity follows from
$\bar D_2=\sum_c[1-(1-Pq_c)^2]$. At $P=0$, $C$ is undefined and
$\bar D_2=0$. Expected distinct keys, $\bar D_K$, differ from extra verified
modes, $B=\texttt{distinct@}K-\texttt{pass@}K$. Sampled diversity can change
through either correctness or conditional concentration. Lower $C$ alone
does not imply a more uniform distribution in the majorization ordering
or larger total support. Collisions also do not determine whether any
particular unobserved key has zero probability.

\paragraph{Connection to the conditional theory.}
Under the assumptions of Theorem~\ref{thm:grpo-collapse}, the time change in
its proof gives $dq_c/d\tau=q_c(q_c-C)$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dC}{d\tau}
 =2\left(\sum_c q_c^3-C^2\right)
 =2\operatorname{Var}_{c\sim q}(q_c)\ge0.
 \label{eq:collision-flow}
\end{equation}
The variance vanishes exactly when $q$ is uniform on its positive support.
Concentration therefore increases strictly along this ideal categorical
flow whenever the positive coordinates are unequal. The conclusion is
specific to this flow: it does not establish a monotonic increase during
neural-network training or a monotonic decrease under finite-buffer replay.

\paragraph{Estimator and comparison population.}
For a prompt with $R\ge2$ correct samples, the collision estimator is
$\widehat C=1-\widehat{\pmd}$, using the pair statistic in
App.~\ref{app:metric-sampling}. It is unbiased for promptwise $C$ under
independent sampling from a fixed policy. Estimates are undefined at $R<2$;
joint selection of eligible prompts across conditions imposes additional
assumptions, as detailed in App.~\ref{app:metric-sampling}.

For paired training seed $s$, let $E_s$ contain the prompts with defined
estimates in both conditions. When $E_s$ is nonempty, the seed contrast is
$|E_s|^{-1}\sum_{x\in E_s}(\widehat C_{B,x}-\widehat C_{A,x})$.
Defined seed estimates receive equal weight. Initial-to-final contrasts
compare each method's own initial evaluation with step 3,072; replay
contrasts pair terminal evaluations. Nominal Student-$t$ intervals require
five defined seeds; smaller samples are descriptive. Intervals describe
variation among seed means, without simultaneous coverage or uncertainty
over unseen prompts. Tables report defined seeds and eligible prompts.

The analysis of hosted models instead pools correct pairs across prompts. Under
independent sampling at a common budget, its ratio of expected colliding
to correct pair counts is
$\sum_x P_x^2 C(q_x)/\sum_x P_x^2$; the finite-sample ratio is not generally
unbiased for this target. Training comparisons weight eligible prompts
equally. Changes in correctness can alter both eligibility and pooled pair
weights, so these aggregates estimate different quantities.

\paragraph{Sampling dependence and sensitivity analyses.}
The following comparisons use four groups of eight responses per prompt,
separate from the 64-response evaluation in App.~\ref{app:fresh-concentration}.
The groups have overlapping sampling streams: under the vLLM V0 seed
convention, they contain eleven distinct nominal stream identifiers.
The concentration estimator retains one representative per identifier,
chosen independently of correctness and key identity. Treating these
identifiers as distinct streams is an assumption of the analysis. Equal
identifiers can produce different outputs, and different identifiers do
not guarantee independent draws.

Two sensitivity comparisons assign disjoint sets of five and six stream
identifiers to the two conditions. Split~0 assigns the lower five to~A and
the upper six to~B; split~1 reverses them. Eligibility is determined
separately for each split. Streams also repeat across prompts, so the
splits do not establish independence across prompts or conditions.
Comparisons remain descriptive where the estimator's independent-sampling
or joint-selection assumptions do not hold.

The reported \texttt{mean@8}, \texttt{pass@8}, and \texttt{distinct@8}
average over the four intact groups. Overlapping streams make these group
estimates dependent, but do not change the expectation of their average.

\paragraph{Concentration before and after training.}
At Level~1, all twelve \texttt{Graph}/\texttt{PantryPlan} Dr.GRPO/GRPO comparisons across the
three scales show increased concentration, with nominal intervals above
zero. Per-sample correctness also increases on their jointly eligible
prompts. \texttt{Graph}'s increases range from $.098$ to $.274$ and \texttt{PantryPlan}'s
from $.447$ to $.886$. Both disjoint splits agree in direction in eleven
of these twelve comparisons; Falcon3-1B \texttt{Graph} with GRPO is the
exception. The direction of the estimated effect can therefore depend on
the sampling split. These results do not establish exact mode disappearance
or increased concentration in every RLVR run.

\paragraph{Effect of replay on concentration.}
At Level~1, Re:Dr has lower concentration than Dr.GRPO in all six \texttt{Graph}
and \texttt{PantryPlan} comparisons. Changes range from $-.756$ to $-.273$ in
\texttt{Graph} and $-.433$ to $-.300$ in \texttt{PantryPlan}; all six nominal intervals lie
below zero, and both disjoint splits agree in direction. These
comparisons also have lower per-sample correctness under replay on their
jointly eligible prompts. The collision contrast therefore describes a
change in the conditional correct-key distribution on that population;
it neither fixes correctness nor implies improvement in every accuracy
measure. The \texttt{pass@8} and sampled-diversity metrics over the full evaluation
set measure different outcomes.

Fewer prompts are eligible in the other domains. Level-1
Dr.GRPO/GRPO \texttt{Python} initial evaluations yield no jointly eligible prompts.
Some \texttt{Countdown} initial-to-final comparisons contain only one or two eligible
prompts per seed. Several \texttt{MathIR} conditions already have observed
collision probability one, leaving little room for a measured increase. A diversity increase
alone therefore does not imply reduced conditional concentration.

For Qwen2.5-3B MaxRL at Level~1, replay lowers concentration in \texttt{Countdown},
\texttt{Python}, and \texttt{PantryPlan}, with nominal intervals below zero; \texttt{MathIR}'s
contrast is zero. \texttt{Graph}'s eleven-stream contrast is negative, but its two
disjoint splits disagree in direction, as they do for Falcon3-1B \texttt{Graph} with
MaxRL. At Level~2, both \texttt{Graph} replay contrasts are negative and both
splits agree. \texttt{PantryPlan} has few jointly eligible prompts: Re:Dr has three defined seeds with 2--16 eligible prompts
and mean $+.495$; Re:Max has four defined seeds with 1--19 eligible
prompts and mean $+.439$. Both splits also give positive means, with
fewer defined seeds. Neither contrast has a five-seed interval.
Diversity gains over the full evaluation set can therefore coexist with
greater conditional concentration on these small jointly eligible subsets.

\paragraph{Table conventions.}
Positive $\Delta\widehat C$ indicates greater concentration. Eligible
prompts have $R\ge2$ correct representatives in both conditions, with
eligibility determined separately for each contrast and split. The range
$|E_s|$ counts eligible prompts out of 128 across available seed pairs,
including zero; $n$ counts seeds with a defined estimate, out of five.
Within each seed, eligible prompts receive equal weight; defined seeds
then receive equal weight. The nominal 95\% intervals are unadjusted,
Student-$t$ intervals, are not truncated to the parameter range, and appear
only at $n=5$. Dashes indicate that a contrast or interval cannot be
estimated under these criteria. Replay comparisons give Re:Dr minus
Dr.GRPO or Re:Max minus MaxRL at step 3,072, whereas initial-to-final
comparisons measure the change within each method.
