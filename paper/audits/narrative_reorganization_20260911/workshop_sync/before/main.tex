\begin{abstract}
Correctness alone cannot reveal whether a model keeps producing the same
successful solution. \mb{} makes verified output identity executable across
five domains. Saved training outputs reveal increased conditional concentration
in Graph and PantryPlan under GRPO and Dr.GRPO across three model scales.
Canonical replay reduces this concentration and improves domain-average
held-out correctness and sampled breadth in controlled comparisons.
A categorical analysis explains a concentration pressure and a conditional
replay guarantee; a key-weighting experiment tests the proposed intervention.
Hosted evaluations establish broader relevance, while harder tasks and
sampling sensitivities delimit the findings.
\end{abstract}

\section{\mb{} separates success from concentration}
\label{sec:introduction}\label{sec:modebench}
Mathematical agents need alternative successful computations for search and
cross-checking \citep{brown2024monkeys,snell2024testtime}. Binary rewards in
reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) distinguish success
from failure, but give familiar and newly discovered successes the same reward
\citep{guo2025deepseekr1}. We therefore measure both whether a model succeeds
and which successful outputs it proposes.

\href{https://huggingface.co/datasets/od2961/ModeBench}{\mb{}} assigns accepted
outputs canonical keys: computation routes in Countdown and MathIR, tested
behaviors in Python, and configurations in Graph and PantryPlan.
Cosmetic aliases merge; different routes may share an answer
(Appendix~\ref{app:domain-overview}). These task-defined modes do not identify
latent reasoning or total policy support.

For a fixed prompt, let $P$ be correct-output probability and $q_c$ the
conditional probability of correct key $c$, defined when $P>0$.
\texttt{pass@8} estimates the chance of any success; \texttt{distinct@8}
estimates the number of different verified modes found in eight attempts.
Their difference counts \emph{extra modes} beyond the first success and
remains coupled to correctness. Correct-pair collision separates the
conditional distribution conceptually:
\begin{equation}
 C(q)=\sum_c q_c^2,\qquad B_2=2P-P^2C(q),
 \label{eq:workshop-collision-bridge}
\end{equation}
where $B_2$ is expected breadth in two independent attempts.
At fixed $P$, lower collision means higher $B_2$; observed collision one
only means the sampled correct outputs share a key
(Appendix~\ref{app:conditional-concentration}).

\section{Observed concentration and a replay response}
\label{sec:collapse}\label{sec:method}\label{sec:replay}\label{sec:maxrl-method}
\textbf{Concentration rises in the clearest measurable domains.}
Across Graph and PantryPlan, all twelve GRPO/Dr.GRPO before/after comparisons
show increased collision, with nominal intervals above zero
(Figure~\ref{fig:concentration-story}). Per-sample correctness also rises on
these same eligible prompts. Both disjoint-stream orientations agree in
eleven comparisons; Falcon Graph with GRPO is the exception. Python's initial
outputs provide no jointly eligible prompts, some Countdown comparisons have
only one or two per seed, and several MathIR conditions already have observed
collision one. The evidence supports concentration in the stated tasks,
without identifying literal disappearance of modes.

\textbf{The diagnostic requires an explicit sampling population.}
With $R\ge2$ correct observations and key counts $n_c$, we use
$\widehat C=\sum_c n_c(n_c-1)/[R(R-1)]$. Under a fixed prompt and iid sampling,
$\E[\widehat C\mid R]=C$. Saved sampling groups reuse nominal random streams:
four groups of eight yield eleven distinct identifiers under the audited
sampler mapping. We select the earliest output per identifier without
consulting its key. Paired means weight jointly eligible prompts equally,
then training seeds equally. Disjoint 5/6-stream orientations and fixed-prompt
sensitivities remain descriptive because streams also recur across prompts.
The retrospective analysis and a source-census extension made after its first
results are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:conditional-concentration}.

\textbf{Conditional theory explains a pressure that replay can oppose.}
Fix one isolated prompt with finitely many categories, at least two correct
and one incorrect, finite initial logits ($0<P<1$), and groups of size $G\ge2$.
With independent canonical-outcome logits, Euclidean infinitesimal on-policy
updates, common length normalization, and no KL penalty, the categorical
mean updates for Dr.GRPO and the implemented MaxRL estimator induce the
same conditional flow up to positive time scaling:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{dq_c}{d\tau}=q_c(q_c-C),\qquad
 \frac{dC}{d\tau}=2\operatorname{Var}_{c\sim q}(q_c)\ge0.
 \label{eq:workshop-collision-flow}
\end{equation}
Unequal positive masses therefore become more concentrated; a unique leading
correct mode attracts the conditional mass asymptotically. This is a theorem
about the specified geometry, rather than a guarantee for neural optimization
(Appendices~\ref{app:theory-grpo-mean}--\ref{app:theory-collapse}).

We retain one policy-generated exemplar $e_c$ for each admitted key in a
prompt-specific bank $\mathcal B_x$. For a nonempty bank, replay averages
their token-normalized log likelihoods:
\begin{equation}
 L_{\mathrm{replay}}(x)=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal B_x|}
 \sum_{c\in\mathcal B_x}\frac{\log\pi_\theta(e_c\mid x)}{|e_c|}.
 \label{eq:lm-verified-replay}
\end{equation}
ReplayDr.GRPO uses Dr.GRPO for fresh updates \citep{liu2025understanding};
ReplayMaxRL uses MaxRL \citep{tajwar2026maxrl}. Equal key weights revisit modes
regardless of discovery frequency. In the categorical geometry, positive
fixed-bank replay weights bound admitted probabilities away from zero.
The neural loss and growing bank do not inherit that unconditional guarantee;
undiscovered modes receive no replay
(Appendices~\ref{app:theory-replay},~\ref{app:algorithm}).

\textbf{Replay changes the observed conditional distribution.}
ReplayDr.GRPO lowers collision in all six Graph/PantryPlan comparisons, with
both disjoint orientations agreeing. MaxRL replay also lowers primary
collision in these tasks, but Falcon and 3B Graph have split-direction
exceptions. Eligible-prompt per-sample correctness falls in these replay
comparisons. Lower collision therefore does not mean correctness was held
fixed, nor that the initial distribution was recovered; the full-test
correctness and breadth outcomes below answer a separate question.

\section{Controlled tests of canonical replay}
\label{sec:experiments}\label{sec:results}
Five registered seeds train for eight passes. Each domain has 128 held-out
prompts with four intact eight-output groups; held-out outputs never enter
the bank. No-replay controls run the same bank code with zero replay gradient.
We cross the fresh objective (Dr.GRPO or MaxRL) with replay at
Qwen2.5-0.5B, Falcon3-1B, and Qwen2.5-3B. Thus replay effects compare the
same fresh objective, and estimator effects compare the same replay condition.
Direct GRPO, UCPO \citep{lochab2026ucpo}, success-replay, and semantic-entropy
comparisons remain in Appendix~\ref{app:supporting}.

\textbf{Replay complements both fresh objectives on average.}
\label{sec:results-retention}\label{sec:results-maxrl}
Replay increases domain-average \texttt{pass@8} and \texttt{distinct@8} under
both objectives at every scale (Figure~\ref{fig:replay-factorial-effects}).
All fifteen MaxRL domain-by-scale comparisons have five paired seeds.
At 3B, its five-domain means rise from $.547$ to $.701$ for \texttt{pass@8}
and $.744$ to $1.107$ for \texttt{distinct@8} with replay.
The factorial also exposes exceptions: Falcon Graph has lower sampled
breadth under ReplayMaxRL despite slightly higher \texttt{pass@8}.
Neither the averages nor higher raw breadth establish uniform conditional
deconcentration across domains.

\textbf{Weighting keys equally changes what replay preserves.}
A matched Qwen2.5-0.5B experiment fixes the fresh objective and compares
uniform key weights with weights proportional to fresh discovery frequency.
Uniform weighting adds $.318$ extra modes on the five-domain mean, with paired
bootstrap interval $[.272,.358]$; its \texttt{pass@8} contrast is $.010$ with
interval $[-.119,.139]$ (Figure~\ref{fig:replay-key-weighting}).
This supports balancing discovered keys rather than repeatedly following
their observed frequency. The wide correctness interval does not establish
accuracy equivalence, and extra modes remain correctness-coupled
(Appendix~\ref{app:frequency-replay-progress}).

\section{Hosted relevance and harder-task limits}
\label{sec:hosted-concentration}\label{sec:results-levels}
Seven hosted deployments evaluated across five domains and three levels
also produce accurate but concentrated outputs, particularly in Graph and
MathIR (Appendix~\ref{app:hosted-comparison},
Figure~\ref{fig:hosted-verified-breadth}). A separate no-reasoning GPT
temperature sweep improves aggregate success and breadth through $T=1.5$
(Appendix~\ref{app:gpt56-temperature}). These observations establish the
relevance of verified alternatives beyond the training suite. Different
provider and prompt settings prevent a common-prompt ranking or an inference
about which training procedure caused a deployment's concentration.

Level 2 keeps validators and canonical keys while using harder task
instances. Four complete five-seed factorials show positive mean replay
effects on success and sampled breadth within that level; PantryPlan remains
partial (Appendix~\ref{app:level2-interim}). Its sparse concentration
contrasts include increases under replay. Level construction and evaluation
populations differ, so the within-level comparisons do not identify a causal
effect of increasing difficulty.

\textbf{Conclusion and limits.}
\label{sec:conclusion}\label{sec:discussion}\label{sec:limitations}
\mb{} makes success and verified-output concentration separately observable.
The training suite supplies evidence of concentration and a controlled
replay response; the conditional theory explains why preserving discovered
keys can matter. Replay improves average full-test success and sampled
breadth, but benefits vary by task, metric, and observable population.
Finite outputs cannot establish total support, recover every rare mode, or
validate the categorical guarantee for neural training.
