                                                                                              Abstract
 1                Correctness alone does not reveal how many successful solutions a model can
 2                produce. We introduce M ODE B ENCH, five executable domains that identify task-
 3                defined verified modes. Matched replay comparisons improve average held-out
 4                correctness and sampled breadth across three model scales, with five-seed MaxRL
 5                comparisons complete in every domain; harder-task coverage remains partial.
 6                Hosted evaluations also find accurate but concentrated outputs. Categorical analysis
 7                explains how binary rewards can amplify common modes and how fixed-bank
 8                replay can oppose that pressure under stated assumptions.

 9   1       Preserving alternatives for mathematical agents
10   Mathematical agents can check alternative computations only if the model proposes them [Brown
11   et al., 2024, Snell et al., 2024]. In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), binary
12   correctness feedback rewards familiar and new successes equally [Guo et al., 2025]. Accuracy can
13   therefore improve while sampled verified outputs narrow (Figure 1).
14   We store verified outputs by identity and revisit them during training.

46   Replay gains extend to the harder-level average. Level 2 retains validators and keys. Its test
47   populations differ from Level 1, precluding an isolated difficulty effect. Four complete five-seed
48   factorials (80/80) show positive mean replay effects on correctness and sampled breadth within Level
49   2 (Figure 6); PantryPlan has 10/20 endpoints. Benefits vary: Countdown adds .017 extra modes under
50   ReplayMaxRL, but its correctness interval includes zero (Figure 10).
51   Accurate hosted outputs can remain concentrated. Seven deployments vary in breadth across five
52   domains and three levels; accurate Graph and MathIR outputs often revisit few modes (Figure 7).
53   A no-reasoning GPT sweep raises aggregate pass@8 and breadth through T = 1.5 (Appendix J.3).
54   Prompt and provider settings differ; these observations identify neither training causes nor model-size
55   effects.
56   Conclusion and limits. Replay can improve correctness and sampled breadth; only admitted
57   exemplars receive replay. Fixed-bank scores still have a declining tail (Appendix H.4). Exact mode
58   probabilities, causal accuracy mechanisms and benefits under changed constraints remain unverified.

481   C.6   Scope relative to entropy, admission, and neural optimization

482   Exact entropy is a different comparator.       For a known full correct support and uniform u, the
483   elementary identities
                     H(q) = log m − KL(qku),           Ru (p) = log m − log P + KL(ukq)
484   show that exact conditional entropy and categorical replay both favor a uniform conditional target.
485   With strictly increasing correctness potential Ψ, both Ψ(P ) + βH(q) and Ψ(P ) − ρRu (p) have the
486   unique global optimum P = 1, q = u in probability space on the simplex with P > 0, for positive
487   coefficients. This is a boundary limit for finite-logit softmax policies. The optimum follows from
488   nonnegativity of KL and increasing Ψ; it does not assert convergence of either neural update. The KL
489   direction distinguishes their loss level sets [Pereyra et al., 2017, Agarwal et al., 2021]: replay loss
490   diverges when a protected coordinate vanishes, whereas conditional entropy can remain log(m − 1)
491   with one missing mode.
492   Exact full-output entropy is a third objective and also regularizes incorrect mass. Exact tabular
493   entropy-regularized policy gradient has its own positive probability and convergence guarantees under
494   specified assumptions [Mei et al., 2020]; our collapse theorem contains no entropy term. The sampled
495   semantic scores in the experiments are not exact gradients of full-output or conditional verified-key
496   entropy. Neither their empirical comparison nor the bounded-score lemma is an impossibility result
497   for entropy.

498   Discovery and optimization remain separate requirements. Retention starts after a verified key
499   enters active memory. Actual insertion depends on generation, validation, capacity, and eligibility;
500   a positive sampling probability alone does not establish eventual admission. Once a non-evicting
501   capacity-16 bank fills, an unbanked key can remain ineligible. Changing membership or exemplar
502   weights changes the loss being bounded, so fixed-bank descent cannot simply be carried across those
503   changes. In particular, the fixed-weight argument does not cover a target whose weights continue to
504   change with fresh observation counts.

613   F.3   Admission, capacity, and deterministic scheduling

614   A bank is keyed by the SHA-256 digest of the exact prompt-token sequence. Only loss-active rows
615   with positive reward and a nonempty validator key qualify. The full group is validated against the
616   pre-group bank before any new key is committed. For each new key, the lexicographically smallest
617   normalized response-token tuple is retained; new keys are admitted in sorted-key order. Admission is
618   therefore invariant to rollout-row order. The bank keeps one exemplar per key, up to M = 16, with
619   no eviction or replacement. Later keys may enter diagnostics but not replay support when capacity is
620   full. Prompt-hash collisions or changed tokens under an existing hash are hard errors.
621   At each update, the scheduler sorts nonempty prompt-bank hashes and selects one by a fixed
622   round-robin cursor; singleton banks are eligible. The cursor is independent of reward, evaluation,
623   desired support, or entropy, and is serialized with the bank. Stored prompt and response tokens are
624   materialized unchanged into a right-padded teacher-forcing batch. Prompt and padding positions
625   are masked. Each exemplar contributes its mean response-token log probability, averaged uniformly
626   across keys in the selected bank before applying λreplay and the exact (G − 1)/G2 rollout scaling in
627   Algorithm 1. Neither exhaustive support nor evaluation outputs enter this loss.

628   F.4   Matched controls and recoverable state

629   Controls share validation, bank updates, scheduling, materialization, and the score pass, with the
630   replay score-gradient vector replaced by exact zeros. Treatment activates only that derivative. Policies
631   can subsequently differ in samples, bank contents, selected banks, exemplar lengths, and realized
632   computation. Logs retain bank/key counts, replayed token counts, raw and applied losses, score-
633   gradient summaries, cursor phase, and the exact-zero switch. Model and optimizer state, bank counts

1304   L.2   Observed sampling-budget effects

1305   The completed local panel contains 110,592 fresh responses from all 25 registered checkpoints. Each
1306   trained checkpoint is evaluated in its own domain at both levels; the initial checkpoint covers all six
1307   cells. Serial regrading corrects one original Python grade from failure to success, confirmed once in
1308   an independent warmed worker using the unchanged verifier and timeouts. The saved receipt does not
1309   identify the cause of the original discrepancy. No response is rescued by formatting normalization,
1310   so strict and normalized analyses coincide throughout this panel. The original responses and grades
1311   remain available alongside the correction audit and source identities.

1312   MathIR: concentration largely persists through 64 draws. At Level 2, both trained methods have
1313   Bk = 0 at every measured budget under both wordings. Replay DrGRPO has original P8 = .9774
1314   and P64 = .9875; neutral wording gives .9767 and .9875. Its Dk equals Pk throughout, so the
1315   small late gain consists entirely of first successes. At exactly eight correct draws, both wordings
1316   have R8 = 1 on the same 76 eligible checkpoint–problem groups, versus the certified-support
1317   uniform lower reference 5[1 − (4/5)8 ] = 4.1611. These groups reuse the same 16 problems across
1318   five checkpoints. At Level 3, all neutral trained groups and all original Replay DrGRPO groups
1319   also contain at most one observed correct key. There is one exception in original DrGRPO: a
1320   single problem at seed 46 contains two keys with counts 28 and 9, giving mean B64 = .0125 and
1321   B64 − B8 = .0035. Its equal-seed correct-pair collision is .9803; the other trained MathIR cells have
1322   collision 1.0, against the uniform upper reference .2. Thus the larger budget reveals a rare additional
1323   mode without supporting an exact single-mode claim for every trained condition.

1324   Python: prompt dependence and late discovery coexist. Original DrGRPO has unchanged
1325   Pk = Dk = .8125 at Level 2 and 1.0 at Level 3 from eight through 64 draws. Neutral DrGRPO
1326   has zero correct responses out of 5,120 at Level 2 and three out of 5,120 at Level 3. Each of those
1327   three successes occurs alone in its checkpoint–problem group, so Level 3 mean P64 = D64 = .0375
1328   while correct-pair collision remains undefined. Replay DrGRPO behaves differently. Under original
1329   wording its pass probability is already unchanged from eight to 64, but Dk gains .0582 at Level 2
1330   and .1529 at Level 3. Under neutral wording, Pk rises from .5186 to .7500 and from .5109 to
1331   .7500, respectively; Dk rises from .6618 to 1.1250 and from .6561 to 1.1875. These final mode
1332   counts remain below the original 1.2750 and 1.5750. The corresponding additional-mode gains are
1333   .2318 [.0574, .4565] and .2923 [.0659, .5445], with the registered seed-and-prompt intervals. Larger
1334   budgets therefore recover both first successes and additional verified alternatives in the neutral replay
1335   condition. These unconditional gains do not identify a change in the conditional distribution of
1336   correct solutions by themselves.

1337   Pantry: eight draws understate observed discovery. Across the trained Pantry cells and both
1338   wordings, D64 − D8 ranges from .4151 to 1.0247, with additional-mode gains ranging from .2739
1339   to .7664. The initial checkpoint also gains .8565–1.0778 modes. In the two observed training
1340   seeds, Level 3 point estimates even change method ordering: original DrGRPO/Replay DrGRPO
1341   have D8 = .2075/.3381 but D64 = 1.1563/.9063; neutral wording gives .2253/.3515 at eight and

1344   larger m often have few or no jointly eligible problems. The eligibility table and undefined means
1345   remain essential to interpreting the conditional curves.
1346   These observations support persistent concentration in several trained conditions and substantial late
1347   discovery in others. A flat finite-sample curve cannot exclude rarer unseen modes, and the result
1348   does not license a single saturation claim across domains. The new k = 8 points rarefy the fresh
1349   64-draw pools on 16 problems per cell; they are not differences against the previous experiment’s
1350   32-problem scores. The separately registered frontier panel, including GPT-5.6 Sol, is now collecting
1351   responses and supplies no completed result in this local-panel report. Cross-scale robustness of these
1352   sampling-budget patterns therefore remains an open empirical question for that panel.

1353   M     Related Work
1354   Repeated sampling and verifier-guided selection depend on the proposal distribution [Brown et al.,
1355   2024, Snell et al., 2024]. Prior work studies diversity loss in alignment and reasoning [Kirk et al.,
1356   2024, Dang et al., 2025], entropy dynamics [Cui et al., 2025], and high-budget coverage [Yue et al.,
1357   2025]. Semantic clustering [Farquhar et al., 2024] addresses open-ended outputs; M ODE B ENCH
1358   instead uses executable identity keys. Sampling-aware objectives promote useful proposal sets [Song
1359   et al., 2025, Chen et al., 2025, Li et al., 2025, 2026a,b, Lochab et al., 2026]. MaxRL optimizes
1360   best-of-k success [Tajwar et al., 2026], which equals pass@k for binary rewards. Replay has a
1361   long history in reinforcement and continual learning [Lin, 1992, Rolnick et al., 2019]; RLEP reuses
1362   successful reasoning traces [Zhang et al., 2025]. Our bank retains one policy exemplar per verified key
1363   and weights keys uniformly, connecting persistent success memory to novelty and quality-diversity
1364   search [Lehman and Stanley, 2011, Mouret and Clune, 2015].

1366   N.1   Admissible sources and missing checkpoints

1367   Falcon3-1B Countdown ReplayDr.GRPO seed 59 contains two complete four-draw terminal evalu-
1368   ation sequences with identical request identities but conflicting metrics. The integrity amendment
1369   excludes that source at every checkpoint and from every efficacy endpoint, paired contrast, and
1370   aggregate that reuses it; neither sequence is selected or averaged. The independently valid Dr.GRPO
1371   and GRPO seed-59 sources remain admissible. The core replay comparison therefore has 74 pairs:
1372   14 complete five-seed blocks and Falcon Countdown at n = 4 (seeds 55–58). Primary partial
1373   intersections are descriptive and receive no confidence interval.
1374   Trajectory records use registered sources and approved continuations or replacements, with superseded
1375   attempts excluded. Identical duplicates are deduplicated; conflicting records invalidate the affected
1376   checkpoint rather than being selected by metric value. Missing or ambiguous checkpoints remain
1377   gaps; curves do not interpolate or carry values forward across them. An ambiguous nonterminal
1378   checkpoint does not invalidate an independently admissible terminal endpoint. Each curve declares
1379   its fixed source-admissible seed cohort; paired core curves use the admitted intersection. Accuracy
1380   and mode-count views use the same populations and gap rules. Supporting methods retain their
1381   explicit method-specific cohorts; available checkpoint counts and gaps are recorded alongside the
1382   curves. Machine-readable records identify the sources, exclusions, exact seed sets, checkpoint rule,
1383   and source hashes.

1400   N.3   Exact continuation and validation

1401   Exact training continuation requires the atomic optimizer, bank, token, cursor, and request-stream
1402   state in Appendix F.4, in addition to model weights. State mismatch, non-finite values, missing
1403   completion evidence, changed dataset identities, and source or aggregate hash drift are hard failures.

1405   Figure reproduction uses frozen records; missing results are never replaced by shallower checkpoints
1406   or silently incorporated from a newer archive.

1407   O     Limitations and Disclosures
1408   The categorical theory depends on its stated geometry, energy, and coverage assumptions; it does not
1409   certify the implemented neural optimizer. Uniform correct mass requires full verified coverage, which
1410   Python’s capacity-16 bank generally precludes. The benchmark measures task-defined executable
1411   support on synthetic tasks, not free-form reasoning diversity or downstream utility. Experiments
1412   fix G = 16, zero reference KL, no token-entropy bonus, and one decoding protocol. The current
1413   results state every partial seed intersection and its uncertainty limits; intervals condition on the frozen
1414   prompts and evaluation requests.
