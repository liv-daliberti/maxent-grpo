## Scientific clarifications

1. **Intervals.** The 20,000 replicates are paired problem bootstraps with percentile 95% intervals. Hosted aggregate resampling is stratified by domain and level. Intervals condition on observed response pools and fixed training seeds; they do not estimate variation across new response pools or new training seeds. The 21 model pairs are dependent and intervals have no multiplicity adjustment.
2. **Pantry population.** There are 192 candidate ingredient outages, 181 feasible outages, and 32 held-out Level-2 problems. All five feasible dietary restrictions arise from five problem clusters. Figure 35 averages perturbations within problem, then problems equally, and averages the two trained seeds for the displayed trained curves.
3. **Pantry interfaces.** Ordinary sampling and the controls use a 192-token cap; ordinary sampling uses temperature 1.0. Tuned temperature is selected from 0.7/1.0/1.3 using 384 responses on 16 development problems. All strategies generate eight initial plans, but diversity prompting includes earlier responses in its context. Recovery stops at the first valid plan, or at eight calls, and cumulative success includes zero-call survivors.
4. **Pantry conditional comparison.** The 1.05-point survival difference is a descriptive equal-observed-correct-count comparison. For each problem it averages only eligible seed comparisons, then averages the 19 eligible problems. Reconstructed contributions: 11 problems have one eligible seed comparison and 8 have both. Consequently this changes the problem and seed composition and does not establish equal underlying accuracy or identify diversity as the cause of adaptation.
5. **Pantry costs.** The first table charges an initial portfolio once per changed task, plus recovery, then averages perturbations within problem and problems equally. The cost table counts each generated response once across calibration, initial portfolios for the two controls, and recovery for all three strategies. It excludes ordinary initial-portfolio generation, whose timing is unavailable. Input tokens include reused prefixes; timings are not hardware-normalized compute and exclude training. No complete compute-matched comparison is claimed.
6. **Mixture estimand.** A 4+4 mixture selects four responses without replacement from each constituent's eight-response pool. Its expectation is exact over the 4,900 possible subset pairs. It is compared with each constituent's eight responses and their average. The 'correctness matched' heading was replaced by 'Four correct draws': this conditional analysis requires at least four correct responses from each constituent, then compares two correct draws from each against four from either. It fixes the correct-draw count, not accuracy or attempts to obtain correct draws.
7. **Collision.** Cross/within collision comparisons use jointly eligible prompts with at least two correct draws from each model and average prompts equally. They cannot identify disjoint full supports from an absence of shared observed keys.
8. **Coarser keys.** Graph renames colors only when all anchored colors are fixed; Python groups opposite divisors into an unordered factor pair, preserving case order; Countdown preserves operands, multiplicity, subtraction/division boundaries while flattening/sorting associative addition/multiplication; MathIR/Pantry keys are unchanged. Merging correct keys preserves correctness, cannot increase distinct counts, and cannot decrease collision. Exact Graph/Python group enumeration distinguishes uniform fine-key mass from uniform coarse-group mass. Countdown's full verifier support is unknown.
9. **Local scope.** The local table concerns Qwen2.5-0.5B Level-2 trained endpoints evaluated at Levels 2 and 3 on 32 paired problems, with the displayed fixed seeds. It does not establish results for every training trajectory. The adverse original-wording Python Level-3 result (fine gain .419, coarse gain zero) and the Level-2 reduction (.431 to .075) remain explicit.
