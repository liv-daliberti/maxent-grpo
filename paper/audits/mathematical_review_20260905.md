Mathematical and evidential review, September 5, 2026

The edited categorical results are correct under their stated assumptions. Independent derivation confirms the finite-group mean updates, time change, unique-winner and tied-winner limits, fixed-bank replay barrier, and the deterministic finite-step extension. The substantive limitations concern translating these results into claims about neural training, discovery, and observed breadth. This review covers the current [main manuscript](../main.tex), corresponding [MathAI appendix](../mathai2026/appendix.tex), and frozen audit artifacts; live campaign progress does not change the frozen empirical evidence below.

The categorical model assumes finitely many independently trained Euclidean logits, binary rewards, independent groups with G≥2, finite positive mixed-group weights, a common fixed response-length normalizer, zero reference KL, and exact continuous mean updates. The added replay lemma also covers deterministic exact gradient descent under an explicit step-size bound. The revised text now states the gradient metric locally, calls the convergence result a property of the mean flows, defines all-equal updates as zero, and distinguishes finite-group sampling effects from an unproved claim that finite groups necessarily worsen collapse. Sample-standard-deviation normalization with epsilon changes the scalar coefficient without invalidating the result. Response-dependent row normalizers would require a different derivation.

For correctness mass P and success count M, conditioning on group rewards gives

\[
\mathbb E\widehat g=c_G(P)\nabla P,\qquad
c_G(P)=\frac{\mathbb E[w(M)M(G-M)]}{G^2P(1-P)}.
\]

Equivalently, c_G(P)=(G−1)E[w(B+1)]/G for B distributed as Binomial(G−2,P). This establishes finite positive endpoint extensions; Dr.GRPO has c_G=(G−1)/G. Exhaustive enumeration across four categorical distributions, G=2,…,6, and four estimator variants gives 80 passing checks, with maximum discrepancy 9.33×10⁻¹⁵. The [verifier](verify_categorical_group_expectations_20260905.py), [results](verify_categorical_group_expectations_20260905.json), and [independent derivation](categorical_mean_update_independent_audit_20260905.md) are reproducible arithmetic and analytic evidence, respectively. Simulation is not substituted for proof.

The MaxRL distinction is exact. With advantages Gr_i/M−1 for M>0 and an entirely zero update for M=0, the coefficient is [1−(1−P)^(G−1)]/P. Its potential is Σ from k=1 to G−1 of [1−(1−P)^k]/k. The unconditional success-only estimator, or the centered estimator retaining the negative score-average term on all-failure groups, instead targets order G. The [primary MaxRL paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02710v1) separates these choices in Sections 4.1–4.3: its theoretical unconditional control variate is zero-mean, whereas Algorithm 1 drops both terms on all-failure groups. The revised manuscript correctly identifies the implemented estimator with order G−1; it should not interchange its potential with the order-G objective.

For collapse, the effective time satisfies dτ=c_G(P)P(1−P)dt. Monotonicity and the gradient lower bound imply P→1; finite total τ would force finite limiting logits, a contradiction. Thus τ→∞. The conditional dynamics are dq_c/dτ=q_c(q_c−Σq_d²). If Q is the initially largest coordinate and r_j=q_j/Q<1, then Q≥1/m and

\[
\frac{d\log r_j}{d\tau}=-Q(1-r_j)
\le-\frac{1-r_j(0)}m.
\]

Consequently every losing ratio decays exponentially in τ. Symmetry gives equal limiting mass 1/k for k tied leaders. This direct argument closes the earlier abbreviated limit-point justification. Exact uniform initialization remains uniform; G=1 and boundary P∈{0,1} are excluded. Extinction is asymptotic, not finite-time loss of softmax positivity.

Replay changes the potential to F=ρR−Ψ(P). For a fixed bank and fixed positive weights, energy descent bounds R and therefore every positively weighted banked probability away from zero. Full coverage with independent logits gives P→1 and q→w; uniformity requires uniform target weights. The corrected convergence argument uses bounded derivatives and vanishing gradient, rather than inferring convergence merely from a unique minimizer. Smooth parameterization preserves energy descent and the qualitative retention barrier, but can stall and does not establish uniform convergence in neural models.

The new finite-step lemma strengthens the idealized result. For F=ρR_w−Ψ(P), a global smoothness bound is L=ρ/2+M₁/2+M₂/8, with M₁=sup|Ψ′| and M₂=sup|Ψ″| on [0,1]. Constant deterministic gradient steps with 0<η<2/L preserve the same all-iterate retention floor and converge in probability to the bank target weights. The [independent proof](finite_step_categorical_replay_independent_review_20260905.md) checks the Hessian and boundary-stationarity arguments; the [verifier](verify_discrete_replay_20260905.py) and [results](verify_discrete_replay_20260905.json) check 720,000 steps across 36 trajectories. All sampled descent and retention inequalities pass, with gradient finite-difference error at most 3.42×10⁻¹⁰. These checks supplement the proof; finite-horizon target errors are not an asymptotic proof. The same probability limit holds for the mean flow. A substantive limitation follows: a fixed incomplete categorical bank asymptotically excludes even correct modes outside it. Dynamic discovery and bank coverage are therefore essential to stronger breadth claims; deterministic finite steps do not establish an Adam, stochastic, clipped, or shared-neural guarantee.

Length normalization matters. Even the simplified one-exemplar-per-category loss with lengths L_b equals A R_w, where A=k⁻¹ΣL_b⁻¹ and w_b=L_b⁻¹/ΣL_j⁻¹. Thus ρ_w=ρ_LM A and the full-coverage target favors shorter exemplars unless lengths coincide. An actual exemplar probability is only part of its execution-key probability. Equal key sampling and mean-token scoring therefore do not imply uniform learned key probabilities. The [weighted-replay verifier](verify_weighted_replay_20260905.py) and [results](verify_weighted_replay_20260905.json) check this identity, gradients, target distributions, and relative-frequency scaling.

Effective dose is ρ=ρ_raw μ_x/ν_x for replay frequency μ_x and fresh frequency ν_x. Equal frequencies cancel; round-robin replay alone does not imply dilution. At G=16 the illustrative coefficient .10×15/256 is approximately .00586, and the stated worst-case Dr.GRPO bound can be as weak as exp(−2560), before the initial replay-loss contribution. It is a qualitative barrier, not an experimentally meaningful probability floor. Changing bank membership, eviction, lengths, weights, or relative frequency can invalidate the fixed-potential argument. Capacity 16 cannot cover Python-factors supports of 16–3600 modes as a domain-wide guarantee.

The shared-correctness geometry theorem likewise remains conditional. For the specified aligned update geometry and identical initial logits, greater shared learning reduces effective collapse time at matched correctness and increases expected sampled breadth. Every finite geometry coefficient still permits eventual unique-winner collapse. Parameter replication can accelerate, preserve, or slow the same trajectory depending on scaling; parameter count alone proves nothing. The [geometry verifier](verify_model_size_theorem_20260904.py) and [results](verify_model_size_theorem_20260904.json) support the calculation, not a causal explanation of cross-family or cross-scale experiments.

The [paired interaction audit](audit_factorial_interactions_20260905.py) and [JSON](audit_factorial_interactions_20260905.json) use ten complete, common-five-seed, four-arm blocks at step 3072: nine E118 blocks and E119 Level-2 Graph. For each seed, interaction is (ReplayMaxRL−MaxRL)−(ReplayDr.GRPO−Dr.GRPO). Eight of ten mean interactions are negative on both endpoints. For distinct@8, Falcon Graph is −.776 with unadjusted 95% interval [−.995,−.557]; Qwen0.5B Graph is −.483 [−.872,−.093]; Level-2 Graph is −.351 [−.446,−.255]. These describe diminished incremental replay effects under the stronger fresh objective, not failure of all replay benefits. Positive simple effects do not establish positive interaction or synergy, and bounded endpoints can produce ceiling effects. Falcon Countdown has only four admissible common seeds and is excluded from this five-seed audit; incomplete blocks are not imputed.

Uncertainty is paired across training seeds, after averaging 128 frozen prompts and four fixed draw streams. With n=5, t intervals have four degrees of freedom and are approximate, unadjusted descriptions. They do not resample new prompts or decoding seeds, provide simultaneous coverage, or justify selecting favorable domains. Reader safeguards should retain separate co-primary pass@8 and distinct@8 endpoints, fixed terminal horizons, admissibility rules, per-seed effects, and the distinction between prespecified comparisons and exploratory interactions.

The most valuable remaining reviewer requests are:

1. **Test fixed identities.** Occupancy and fingerprints do not measure survival. The [E121 registration](../preregistration/e121_fixed_bank_survival_telemetry_20260903.md) freezes Graph banks at step 384 and records aligned per-exemplar mean-token and sequence scores. Report missing visits and changing fingerprints as failures. E121 is planned mechanism evidence here, not an available empirical confirmation or a measurement of the tiny theoretical bound.
2. **Measure the neural, finite-step gap.** Check whether actual alternating stochastic updates preserve individual exemplars under shared-prompt interference, optimizer preconditioning, and clipping. Neither the ideal energy argument nor finite-time score positivity proves this practical claim.
3. **Separate length, capacity, and dose.** Report length distributions, capacity hits, admission/eviction, realized replay tokens, and replay/fresh frequencies. Shared code and matched hyperparameters do not force policies to develop identical banks or consume identical replay work.
4. **Report paired interactions without synergy language.** Show simple effects alongside within-seed differences of differences and unadjusted uncertainty; do not subtract separately computed confidence limits or pool incomplete blocks.
5. **Compare breadth at matched success.** Per prompt, distinct@K=Σ_c[1−(1−μ_c)^K] and pass@K=1−(1−P)^K. Higher distinct@K can reflect more successes, redistribution, or both. Even distinct@K−pass@K remains success-dependent; it is not a correctness-adjusted support estimator. Nonlinear identities must precede prompt averaging.
6. **Bound inference and key-granularity claims.** Finite-K coverage cannot identify total positive-probability support or semantic entropy. Keep decoding budgets and settings matched; evaluate key refinements/coarsenings and validator behavior. Execution keys capture the registered behavior equivalence, not necessarily distinct reasoning strategies, and open-ended outputs have no canonical uniform generator.
