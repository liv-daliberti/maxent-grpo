                                                                                              Abstract
 1                A language model can become more accurate on mathematical problems while los-
 2                ing alternative correct solutions. To measure this loss, we introduce M ODE B ENCH,
 3                a benchmark of five executable domains that distinguishes different correct out-
 4                puts for the same problem. We then add replay of previously verified outputs to
 5                Dr.GRPO and MaxRL, yielding ReplayDr.GRPO and ReplayMaxRL. Both variants
 6                improve held-out correctness while preserving a broader repertoire, across three
 7                and two model scales, respectively, and on a harder graph-coloring task. These
 8                results distinguish discovering a successful behavior from keeping it available.

 9   1       Preserving alternatives for mathematical agents
10   Training a language model to solve mathematical problems more accurately may also change which
11   correct solutions it can still produce. That distinction matters for a mathematical agent: a different
12   computation or proof idea may offer a way forward when a familiar route fails or constraints change.
13   Tools and verifiers can guide search and revision [Brown et al., 2024, Snell et al., 2024], but a verifier
14   cannot select an alternative the model never proposes.
15   Correctness feedback can hide the loss of these options. Reinforcement learning with verifiable
16   rewards (RLVR) reinforces correct outputs [Guo et al., 2025]. A familiar solution earns the same
17   correctness reward as a newly discovered one. Accuracy can therefore improve even as the range of
18   verified outputs shrinks. In our runs, these two trends occur together (Figure 1).

21   99.4% at Qwen2.5-0.5B, 71.6% at Falcon3-1B, and 41.5% at Qwen2.5-3B (Appendix Figure 10).
22   We therefore ask how training can keep discovered alternatives available.

23   2        M ODE B ENCH makes output identity executable
24   M ODE B ENCH assigns each accepted output an exact, executable identity: a canonical key whose
25   distinct values define verified modes (Figure 2). Keys capture computation routes in Countdown
26   and MathIR, tested program behaviors in Python, and valid configurations in Graph and PantryPlan.
27   Cosmetic aliases merge; distinct routes can share an answer (key rules: Appendix B.1). This measures
28   task-defined output support; it does not establish diversity of latent reasoning strategies.

34   Each retained key receives equal replay weight, independent of its frequency in fresh rollouts. A
35   solution seen once gets the same share of replay as one seen repeatedly. This keeps common
36   solutions from absorbing the replay budget and gives rare successes a continuing training signal.
37   Replacing MaxRL with Dr.GRPO [Liu et al., 2025], which debiases GRPO [Shao et al., 2024],
38   yields ReplayDr.GRPO. Appendix K gives the full update and Appendix E.8 the idealized retention
39   guarantee.
40   Related work. We connect studies of collapse [Cui et al., 2025, Dang et al., 2025] and success replay
41   [Zhang et al., 2025] through executable identity (Appendix D).

42   4     Does memory preserve verified output support?
43   pass@8 is the chance that eight samples contain any correct response; distinct@8 is their average
44   number of distinct verified modes. Extra modes, distinct@8−pass@8, counts alternatives beyond
45   the first correct output. Both support measures remain coupled to correctness. For a fixed prompt, our
46   idealized categorical analysis proves that stronger updates shared across correct outputs can preserve
47   more sampled modes at matched correctness, with identical pass@8 (Theorem E.5).
48   We train for eight passes with five registered seeds per comparison. Each domain has 128 held-out
49   prompts with four eight-sample draws; none enter the bank. Dr.GRPO/MaxRL controls run the same
50   bank code with zero replay gradient. Figure 4 also compares reward normalization, UCPO [Lochab
51   et al., 2026], RLEP-Dr success replay, and semantic entropy (protocols: Appendix B).

67   5     Conclusion and Limits
68   M ODE B ENCH makes a missing part of evaluation measurable: the range of distinct, verified out-
69   comes a model can produce for one problem. This matters because different approaches can have
70   different strengths. One may succeed where another fails, or remain useful when constraints change.
71   Understanding diversity therefore gives a fuller picture of a model’s capabilities than accuracy alone.
72   Our results show that replay can preserve alternatives while improving held-out correctness. These
73   controlled tasks provide a starting point for studying diversity in broader mathematical reasoning.
74   Replay retains only discovered, stored modes; individual-mode survival remains unmeasured (Ap-
75   pendix L). Whether broader support causes accuracy gains or helps on later tasks remains open.
76   Adding an edge can invalidate one graph coloring while leaving another valid. A natural next step is
77   to test whether retained alternatives help a model respond to such changes.

257   correct mode appears in more updates, while an absent mode supplies no direct sampled signal.
258   A categorical reduction
                       P       makes this feedback loop explicit. Let pc denote the probability of correct
259   mode c and P = c∈C pc the total correct mass. Under Euclidean gradient ascent in independently
260   trained softmax logits on expected binary reward, two correct modes c and d satisfy

283   B     Benchmark Construction and Experimental Design
284   B.1   Executable domains and matched levels

285   A domain is mode-verifiable when the same execution both accepts a response and produces its
286   canonical identity. Cosmetic aliases must merge; different executed outcomes must remain distinct.
287   M ODE B ENCH instantiates this contract in five domain families. Each family is generated rather
288   than fixed: we release two disjoint levels with the same validator and canonical-key definition, each
289   containing 384 training prompts and a fixed 128-prompt evaluation split.
290   Figure 2 illustrates the five executable domains at Level 1:

291    • Graph coloring completes a partial three-color assignment. Execution checks every fixed color
292      and edge; the full color vector is the key, yielding 6.27 valid modes per evaluation prompt on
293      average across the fixed evaluation split of 128 prompts.
294    • Countdown builds an exact arithmetic expression from a fixed multiset of numbers. The key is
295      its normalized executed syntax tree: commutative reorderings merge, while different computations
296      remain distinct (4.52 modes per evaluation prompt).


                                                         9
297    • Python factors writes a restricted one-line function that is executed on frozen inputs. Its return
298      vector is the key, so different source programs with identical behavior merge (229.44 modes).
299    • MathIR solves an equation by selecting executable algebra actions. The normalized state
300      trajectory is the key, preserving different valid derivations of the same answer (5.00 modes).
301    • PantryPlan selects ingredients for a constrained plan; a trusted environment finds and validates
302      exact feasible quantities. The selected ingredient support is the key, abstracting away quantity and
303      order aliases (18.19 modes per evaluation prompt).
304   M ODE B ENCH targets task-defined executable output support. ReplayMaxRL stores one verified
305   exemplar per observed task-defined key and samples uniformly over those keys. Appendices I, H,
306   and J give the complete execution, alias, and split contracts.
307   Matched levels. Level 2 preserves each validator, key, and valid-mode-count histogram while adding
308   structural difficulty: more hidden Graph vertices and Countdown operands, Python inputs above the
309   base range, variable-on-both-sides MathIR equations, and tighter PantryPlan constraints. Frozen
310   evaluation admits a level only when it is harder but solvable; Section B.6 gives the design.

376   ∆pass@8 among the displayed alternatives in every domain.
377   On Countdown, replay raises correctness while preserving distinct routes to the same answer.

423   D     Related Work
424   Verifiable learning and output support. Repeated sampling and verifier-guided selection depend on
425   the proposal distribution [Brown et al., 2024, Snell et al., 2024]. Prior work reports reduced sampled
426   output diversity under RLHF or reasoning RL [Kirk et al., 2024, Dang et al., 2025], declining policy
427   entropy [Cui et al., 2025], and reduced high-budget problem coverage in tested RLVR models [Yue
428   et al., 2025]. These measures differ from within-prompt execution-key breadth. Semantic clustering
429   infers when generations express the same answer [Farquhar et al., 2024]. M ODE B ENCH instead lets
430   accepting execution define the equivalence relation, yielding an exact behavioral key.
431   Sampling-aware exploration. Outcome bonuses, entropy control, set objectives, distribution match-
432   ing, and uniform-correct objectives encourage broader verified-output support [Song et al., 2025,
433   Chen et al., 2025, Li et al., 2025, 2026a,b, Lochab et al., 2026]. MaxRL combines best@k objectives
434   across sampling budgets [Tajwar et al., 2026]. For our binary rewards, best@k equals pass@k;
435   ReplayMaxRL tests whether explicit mode memory adds support beyond this fresh-rollout objective.
436   Replay and persistent memory. Experience replay reuses past experience and can mitigate forgetting
437   [Lin, 1992, Rolnick et al., 2019]; RLEP reuses successful reasoning trajectories [Zhang et al., 2025].
438   Our replay component instead stores one exemplar per canonical executed key and samples uniformly
439   over keys, making equal retention an explicit target rather than depending on how often each success
440   occurs. Novelty search rewards behavioral novelty [Lehman and Stanley, 2011], while MAP-Elites
441   retains one elite per behavior-descriptor cell [Mouret and Clune, 2015]. Our replay bank uses the
442   execution key as the descriptor inside a policy-gradient update.

445   A binary verifier assigns the same reward to every correct mode, but an update can still amplify the
446   most common one. We first explain that mechanism in a finite categorical model, then ask what
447   changes when we alter the update geometry, add entropy, or replay earlier successes. Replay directly
448   protects represented modes; discovery, admission, and reliable neural optimization remain separate
449   requirements.

456   a persistent bank signal, then turn bounded replay loss into protected probabilities. The remaining

458   bank admission. Finally, Section E.12 shows how much those bounds can certify and what finite

1092   E.11    Discovery, admission, and retention through bank changes

1093   Discovery and retention require two separate budgets: enough opportunities to admit a missing key,
1094   and enough persistent loss weight to protect it after admission. Bank growth connects the two because
1095   admitting a key can also change the objective used to protect earlier keys.
1096   Retention begins only after a verified key enters active memory. Archive exploration separates
1097   discovery from reliable return to a discovered state [Ecoffet et al., 2021]; replay with behavioral
1098   cloning supplies a related empirical mechanism for reducing forgetting [Rolnick et al., 2019]. Neither
1099   result is a theorem that every execution key enters a finite bank or maintains a positive probability
1100   thereafter. We state the additional admission and energy conditions explicitly.

1275   actual key probability in this constructed example is .20 + .20 = .40. Grouping complete responses
1276   by their shared execution key therefore strengthens the certificate without treating either response as
1277   the whole key.

1291   E.12.4   Finite observations and repeated checkpoint measurement
1292   At a frozen checkpoint, for a key specified before sampling, N independent draws under the same
1293   decoding law give X ∼ Binomial(N, pb ). Exact binomial-tail inversion gives one-sided confidence
1294   bounds [Clopper and Pearson, 1934]. If X = 0, the one-sided (1 − α) upper bound and lower bound
1295   are respectively
                                            U0 = 1 − α1/N ,       0.
1296   Indeed, Prpb (X = 0) = (1 − pb )N < α whenever pb > U0 ; for nonzero counts, the full confidence
1297   procedure uses the corresponding binomial-tail inversion.

1298   Worked example: unseen is not the same as absent. For one key selected before sampling, zero
1299   sightings in 32 draws give a one-sided 95% upper confidence bound of about 8.94%, with lower
1300   bound zero. The sample therefore does not establish exact extinction. If the claim must cover 16
1301   prespecified keys simultaneously, Bonferroni allocates .05/16 error to each key; the zero-count
1302   upper bound becomes about 16.50%. Requiring simultaneous coverage makes the certificate weaker.
1303   Conversely, two sightings in 32 draws have empirical frequency 6.25%, but their one-sided 95%
1304   lower confidence bound is only about 1.12%. These hypothetical upper and lower examples are
1305   separate one-sided limits; a joint interval requires allocation to both tails.
1306   For repeated inspection of draws from a fixed policy, a confidence sequence can provide validity
1307   under optional stopping [Howard et al., 2021]. For fixed beta-mixture parameters a0 , b0 > 0, let
1308   St be the number of successes in t independent Bernoulli draws and let B denote the beta function.
1309   Define
                              B(St + a0 , t − St + b0 )

                              B(a0 , b0 )pSt (1 − p)t−St
1310   For each true p ∈ (0, 1), the numerator integrates the likelihood under a fixed beta distribution. Hence
1311   Mt (p) is a mixture of likelihood-ratio martingales, is nonnegative, and has initial value one. The
1312   nonnegative martingale maximal inequality therefore gives Prp (∃t : p ∈    / It ) ≤ α. Error allocations
1313   across a fixed roster of keys make coverage simultaneous by a union bound; independence across
1314   keys is unnecessary.
1315   This construction requires a fixed Bernoulli probability. Counts from different prompts, seeds, or
1316   changing checkpoints cannot be pooled to certify the current checkpoint’s probability. Separate frozen-
1317   checkpoint measurements can instead allocate error across prespecified prompt–key–checkpoint
1318   statements. Repeated deterministic teacher-forced scores are not independent Bernoulli observations,
1319   and choosing identities after inspecting the same samples also needs selection-aware inference. A
1320   probability bound at observed checkpoints alone makes no claim about unobserved intervening or
1321   subsequent training states.

1322   Worked example: repeated checkpoints spend an error budget. Suppose the plan specifies
1323   16 keys at each of ten frozen checkpoints, with 32 new draws per checkpoint. There are 160 key–
1324   checkpoint statements. Allocating .05/160 to each gives simultaneous 95% coverage by a union
1325   bound, without requiring the key counts to be independent. A zero count then has an upper bound
1326   of about 22.29%. Pooling all 320 draws would not fix this weakness: the ten checkpoints can have
1327   different key probabilities. The confidence sequence above instead permits repeated inspection and a
1328   data-dependent stopping time while sampling one fixed checkpoint.
1329   For an unknown catalogue, missing mass is the total probability of categories not yet observed under
1330   a fixed iid law. Concentration results address this quantity [McAllester and Ortiz, 2003, Berend
1331   and Kontorovich, 2013]; it is neither the number of missing modes nor proof of their extinction.
1332   Such bounds do not certify each named key, and stationary missing-mass formulas do not directly
1333   apply to a history generated by changing policies. Named-key binomial counts provide the direct
1334   measurement of per-mode visibility. For example, a hypothetical missing mass of .10 could consist


                                                             35
1335   of one unseen mode with probability .10 or one hundred unseen modes of probability .001 each; the
1336   same aggregate mass does not identify how many keys are missing.
1337   Remark E.24 (Exact scope of the ReplayMaxRL guarantee). The categorical replay theorem protects
1338   represented bank modes after discovery; it is not an unconditional end-to-end guarantee for a language
1339   model. Without full coverage it protects only B, and Lemma E.14 shows that a frozen incomplete
1340   bank can exclude unbanked correct modes in the categorical limit. Capacity 16 can therefore truncate
1341   protected support. The admission results above identify sufficient coverage and switching conditions;
1342   they do not establish those conditions for the implemented proposal and insertion machinery.
1343   The categorical proof uses log pb , whereas the implementation uses length-normalized teacher-forced
1344   exemplar scores. The complete-exemplar bridge and stochastic extension permit shared parameters
1345   under explicit joint-energy and update-error assumptions. They do not prove uniform neural key
1346   probabilities or verify those assumptions for the current clipped, alternating AdamW updates. The
1347   sharper certificates require correctly normalized complete likelihoods under the decoding law being
1348   bounded. Finally, exact entropy and exact categorical natural gradient have their own protection
1349   guarantees; collapse is a property of the stated update model, not of every procedure that optimizes
1350   correctness or entropy. These results motivate direct per-mode survival measurements.

1472   reward contributes exactly zero fresh task gradient. Only a mixed group — at least one correct and at

1474   therefore measures how often a group can supply fresh reward information. A zero current task
1475   gradient does not imply an unchanged policy: AdamW momentum and weight decay can still move
1476   parameters.

1545   G    Semantic MaxEnt
1546   Policy entropy can collapse during RL for reasoning, motivating explicit exploration control [Cui
1547   et al., 2025]. Outcome-based exploration uses answer frequencies to encourage rarely sampled
1548   outputs [Song et al., 2025]. Semantic MaxEnt applies this motivation to validator-positive execution
1549   keys: an entropy-inspired bonus favors underrepresented verified outcomes in fresh rollouts.
1550   The distinction between fresh exploration and retention motivates our main method. The Semantic
1551   MaxEnt bonus acts only on outcomes present in the current rollouts; it supplies no direct reinforcement
1552   for a remembered mode that is absent. ReplayMaxRL assigns these roles to separate mechanisms:
1553   MaxRL targets success across sampling budgets [Tajwar et al., 2026], while uniform replay revisits
1554   verified exemplars independently of their current sampling frequency. The Dr.GRPO/MaxRL ×


                                                               44
1555   replay factorial tests whether this persistent memory adds value beyond the fresh-rollout objective.
1556   Semantic MaxEnt remains a direct comparison with entropy-inspired exploration.

1599   H     External Python Verification Contract
1600   Main-body link. This appendix expands the Python Factors contract in Section 2 and its limitation in
1601   Section 5, where observed behavior rather than source-code identity defines a Python mode.

1602   H.1   Accepted language and static bounds

1603   Every candidate is stripped, limited to 240 characters, and parsed by ast.parse in expression mode.
1604   It must be exactly one lambda n: EXPR: positional-only arguments, defaults, keyword arguments,
1605   variadic arguments, additional parameters, and any parameter name other than n are rejected. The
1606   complete AST is capped at 64 nodes. Task specifications contain two to eight distinct integer inputs in
1607   [4, 1000] and are rejected unless their exact behavior space contains at least two valid output vectors.
1608   The expression grammar admits integer literals with absolute value at most 10,000; the input variable
1609   n; +, -, *, floor division, and remainder; integer comparisons; Boolean and/or; unary plus, minus,
1610   and not; and conditional expressions. Boolean and non-integer literals are rejected. Calls, imports,
1611   attributes, comprehensions, containers, subscripts, assignment, loops, and every identifier except n
1612   have no accepted AST node and therefore cannot reach execution. The grammar is defined once in
1613   parent-importable contract code, and the external worker applies it immediately before compilation.


                                                                                               46
1614   H.2   External process and fail-closed semantics

1615   The trainer never evaluates model-written code. A locked parent client starts the same Python
1616   executable with isolated mode -I, a fresh process session, and a persistent JSON-lines worker. Each
1617   request contains only the restricted source and trusted task JSON. The worker reparses the candidate,
1618   compiles it in expression mode, evaluates it with __builtins__={}, and runs every frozen input
1619   under a 0.25-second real-time alarm. The parent imposes a separate one-second response deadline.
1620   The worker returns one JSON object containing either valid=true, the integer output vector, and its
1621   canonical key, or valid=false and an exception class. A worker timeout, parent timeout, empty or
1622   malformed output, broken pipe, non-integer output, or failed divisor check yields ⊥ and reward zero.
1623   Broken pipes, operating-system errors, and malformed responses terminate the worker and permit
1624   one clean restart; a timed out request is failed immediately. Worker stdout is never interpreted as a
1625   candidate answer, and no exception path manufactures a key.

1626   H.3   Correctness and behavioral identity

1627   For each frozen input nj , the returned value dj must be an integer but not a Boolean and must satisfy
1628   1 < dj < nj and nj mod dj = 0. Correctness is conjunctive across the complete input list. The

1705   K.3    Bank admission, determinism, and capacity

1706   A bank is keyed by the SHA-256 digest of the exact prompt-token sequence. Only loss-active rows
1707   with positive task reward and a nonempty validator key are eligible. The full rollout group is validated
1708   before new keys are committed, so every candidate is considered against the same pre-group bank
1709   state.
1710   For each newly observed key, candidates are normalized to token tuples and the lexicographically
1711   smallest eligible response is stored. New keys are processed in sorted-key order, making admission
1712   invariant to rollout-row order. The bank holds at most one exemplar per canonical key and at most
1713   M = 16 exemplars per prompt. Existing exemplars are never evicted or overwritten by a later surface
1714   form; once capacity is full, later keys may remain in diagnostic counts but cannot enter the replay
1715   support. A prompt-hash collision or a changed token sequence under an existing prompt hash is a
1716   hard error.


                                                               52
1717   K.4   Recurrent scheduling and verified-likelihood loss



1718   At each optimizer update the global scheduler sorts the nonempty prompt-bank hashes and selects one
1719   by a round-robin cursor. Singleton banks are eligible: verified likelihood can preserve a discovered
1720   success before a second mode is found. The cursor advances independently of reward, evaluation,
1721   desired support, or bank entropy and is serialized with the bank, so an interruption cannot silently
1722   resample the schedule.
1723   For the selected bank, stored prompt and response tokens are materialized unchanged into a right-
1724   padded teacher-forcing batch. Prompt and padding tokens are masked; each response receives its
1725   mean token log probability sc . Uniform verified likelihood averages −sc first across modes within
1726   the selected prompt, then applies the registered replay coefficient and the per-rollout scaling shown in
1727   Algorithm 1. Thus each stored mode has equal target weight even if its on-policy frequency was high
1728   when discovered. Exhaustive support and evaluation outputs never enter the loss.




1729   K.5   Matched control, telemetry, and resume state



1730   The matched control uses the same validation, bank-update, scheduling, replay-materialization, and
1731   score-pass code, but replaces the replay score-gradient vector by exact zeros. The treatment changes
1732   that derivative from zero to live. Policy-dependent samples, bank contents, selected banks, exemplar
1733   lengths, and realized computation can subsequently differ; sharing the code does not hold those
1734   quantities fixed. Both methods log available and replayed banks, retained modes, realized token
1735   counts, raw and applied replay losses, score-gradient sums and norms, scheduler phase, and whether
1736   the exact-zero switch was active.
1737   Model, optimizer, bank counts, exemplar tokens, replay cursor, data cursor, and request-stream state
1738   are checkpointed as one resume contract. The loader checks the schema, replay capacity, scheduler
1739   settings, estimator constants, token types, prompt hashes, exemplar/support consistency, and cursor
1740   bounds before accepting a checkpoint.




1741   L     Replay Mechanism Checks




1742   The intervention changes whether the replay derivative is applied. Treatment and control share
1743   verifier, bank-admission, round-robin scheduling, replay- materialization, and score-pass code. The
1744   control zeros the replay derivative; the treatment applies it. The resulting policies can build different
1745   banks and incur different replay workloads. ReplayMaxRL and ReplayDr.GRPO differ only in the
1746   fresh-rollout advantage. No semantic-entropy term, proposal model, novelty reward, adaptive replay
1747   coefficient, reference KL, or gold support is active.

1895   O     Limitations and Disclosures
1896   Appendix E gives categorical collapse and retention results, plus conditional extensions to stochastic
1897   updates and bank admission. These require the stated geometry, energy, and coverage assumptions;
1898   they do not certify the implemented neural optimizer. Convergence to uniform correct mass in the
1899   categorical uniform-replay model requires full verified coverage, which Python’s bank capacity
1900   precludes. M ODE B ENCH measures task-defined executable support on small synthetic tasks rather
1901   than free-form reasoning diversity or downstream utility. Experiments fix G = 16, zero reference
1902   KL, no token-entropy bonus, and a single decoding protocol; dynamic sampling, larger groups,
1903   regularization, and decoding sweeps remain open. The uniform-versus-frequency ablation has five
1904   of nine complete blocks, all at Qwen2.5-0.5B, in the frozen September 4, 15:40:26 UTC E120
1905   snapshot. The across-scale MaxRL pair has 11/15 complete blocks; requiring all four arms leaves
1906   ten complete blocks and one four-seed block. Core replay evidence contains 14 complete blocks
1907   and one four-seed block. Level-2 transfer remains complete only on Graph. Sign tests are nominal
1908   exploratory summaries assuming independent block signs; individual intervals quantify estimation
1909   uncertainty.
