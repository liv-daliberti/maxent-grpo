                                                                                              Abstract
 1                Mathematical agents need alternatives for search, verification, and recovery.
 2                Yet RLVR can improve correctness while collapsing sampled output support.
 3                M ODE B ENCH gives verified outputs exact executable identities across five do-
 4                mains. Replay revisits stored modes independently of fresh frequency, augmenting
 5                Dr.GRPO and MaxRL. It improves held-out correctness and support at three and
 6                two model scales, respectively, with one complete harder graph-coloring demon-
 7                stration. These measurements concern verified outputs; diversity of latent reasoning
 8                strategies remains untested.


 9   1       Preserving alternatives for mathematical agents
10   Mathematical agents propose candidates, check them with tools, and revise after feedback [Brown
11   et al., 2024, Snell et al., 2024]. Alternative proof or computation routes could aid search and self-
12   correction when attempts fail or constraints change. A verifier can only select from what the policy
13   still proposes.
14   Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR; Guo et al., 2025) can raise accuracy while
15   collapsing sampled verified output support in our runs (Figure 1).

20   2       ModeBench makes output identity executable
21   M ODE B ENCH assigns each accepted output an exact, executable identity: a canonical key whose
22   distinct values define verified modes (Figure 2). Keys capture computation routes in Countdown
23   and MathIR, tested program behaviors in Python, and valid configurations in Graph and PantryPlan.
24   Cosmetic aliases merge; distinct routes can share an answer (key rules: Appendix B.1). This measures
25   task-defined output support; it does not establish diversity of latent reasoning strategies.

67   5     Conclusion and Limits
68   ModeBench makes a missing part of evaluation measurable: the range of distinct, verified outcomes
69   a model can produce for one problem. This matters because different approaches can have differ-
70   ent strengths. One may succeed where another fails, or remain useful when constraints change.
71   Understanding diversity therefore gives a fuller picture of a model’s capabilities than accuracy alone.
72   Our results show that replay can preserve alternatives while improving held-out correctness. These
73   controlled tasks provide a starting point for studying diversity in broader mathematical reason-
74   ing. Replay retains only discovered, stored modes; individual-mode survival remains unmeasured
75   (Appendix L). We have not shown that broader support causes accuracy gains or helps solve later
76   problems. The next step is to test which alternatives help on richer mathematical tasks, and when.
