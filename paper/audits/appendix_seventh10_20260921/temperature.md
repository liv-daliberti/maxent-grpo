# GPT-5.6 Sol temperature sweep: prose, caption, and figure review

## Scope and changes

Reviewed Appendix O.3, incoming PDF pages 80–82: `paper/results/gpt56_temperature_curve_20260911_appendix.tex`, its active expanded-cohort emitter in `ops/plot_paper_gpt56_temperature_curve.py`, and Figure 38 (`paper/figures/gpt56_temperature_curve.{pdf,png,json}`). The proposed main.tex caption is supplied separately in `figure_caption.tex`; root integrates it. No changes to main.tex were made by this agent.

- Removed manuscript and analysis history: “expanded cohort,” “original/additional” chronology, “frozen,” the historical medium-reference discussion, “retained reports,” and the report-inventory paragraph.
- Named the sensitivity populations by their scientific selection rule: ranks 1–8 per domain–level cell (120 prompts and 4,800 responses) and ranks 9–32 (360 prompts and 14,400 responses). Outcome-independent selection remains explicit.
- Preserved the timing limitation. The 360-prompt requests interleave all five temperatures within each prompt/draw slot. For the 120-prompt population, the T=0 and nonzero-temperature measurements use separate collection periods. Differences in prompt composition and service behavior can affect subset and temperature comparisons. Matching exposed settings and the served-model name does not rule out internal service changes.
- Stated the common request/output configuration: reasoning `none`, 8,192 maximum output tokens, returned `top_p=0.98`, and served model `gpt-5.6-sol-2026-07-09`. Medium reasoning does not support the evaluated nondefault temperatures, and T=2.5 is unsupported in this evaluation. This sweep cannot establish medium-reasoning temperature robustness or deterministic behavior at T=0.
- Preserved stateless calls without claiming independent provider randomness. Whole-prompt bootstrap pairing matches tasks across temperatures; it does not pair individual model responses.
- Defined empirical pass@8 directly from intact groups, including unsolved prompts. Raw distinct@8 depends on both success and variation among correct modes. The old Figure 38 assertion that it tracks pass@8 “by construction” is false: between T=1.5 and T=2, aggregate normalized pass@8 falls while distinct@8 rises. The replacement caption and prose state this dependence correctly.
- Linked normalized grading to the actual typography rules in Appendix N. Strict successes and their canonical keys are preserved, and the same executable verifiers evaluate normalized answers. The figure uses normalized grading; the table includes both gradings.
- Recast the table caption as a supported observed finding with grading marks, the complete population, equal-cell/equal-domain weights, and uncertainty. All nonzero settings have higher observed pass@8 than T=0 in each displayed level and both gradings. Added `tab:gpt56-temperature` so the figure can point directly to its intervals.
- Figure caption defines both panels, level colors/markers, temperature labels, eight-response estimands and unsolved-prompt zeroes, and pointwise intervals. Removed its inaccurate promise of per-domain estimates in this subsection: the displayed table contains level and aggregate values.
- Preserved all endpoint, high-temperature, and subset contrasts and intervals. The highest observed pass@8 is at T=1.5; the highest observed distinct@8 is at T=2. Neither is established as a population optimum or continuous Pareto boundary. Pointwise intervals use 20,000 whole-prompt percentile resamples, stratified by domain and level, without a multiplicity adjustment.
- Retained all unsuccessful model responses and the zero provider-refusal/truncation counts. Failed API attempts are excluded from the 19,200-response sampling budget and retried without adding draws to any group. Existing completion records identify four HTTP 500 attempts, with 19,200 HTTP 200 model responses across all arms. No generated responses were added or excluded by this edit.
- Changed the figure footer from “Frozen formatting normalization” to “Formatting normalization,” and “matched prompts” to “common prompts.” These are common tasks and fixed eight-response budgets, not matched correctness or computation. Plot coordinates, label positions, dimensions, axes geometry, colors and markers are unchanged.
