                                                                                              Abstract

 1                A mathematical reasoning model can become more accurate while losing alternative
 2                correct solutions. To measure this loss, we introduce M ODE B ENCH, a benchmark
 3                of five executable domains that distinguishes different correct outputs for the same
 4                problem. We then show that ReplayMaxRL, which combines sampling-aware
 5                reinforcement learning with a memory of previously verified outputs, improves
 6                held-out correctness while preserving a broader repertoire. We find gains from
 7                replay with both Dr.GRPO and MaxRL, across three and two model scales, respec-
 8                tively, and on a harder graph-coloring task. These results distinguish discovering a
 9                successful behavior from keeping it available.

10   1       Preserving alternatives for mathematical agents

11   When we train a mathematical reasoning model to be correct more often, we may also change which
12   correct solutions it can still produce. That distinction matters for a mathematical agent: a different
13   computation or proof idea may offer a way forward when a familiar route fails or constraints change.
14   Tools and verifiers can guide search and revision [Brown et al., 2024, Snell et al., 2024], but a verifier
15   cannot select an alternative the model never proposes.
16   Correctness feedback can hide the loss of these options. Reinforcement learning with verifiable
17   rewards (RLVR) reinforces correct outputs [Guo et al., 2025]. A familiar solution earns the same

     Submitted to 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026). Do not distribute.
18   correctness reward as a newly discovered one. Accuracy can therefore improve even as the range of
19   verified outputs shrinks. In our runs, these two trends occur together (Figure 1).
20   The loss also appears across our datasets. Averaged over five domains, eight passes of Dr.GRPO
21   reduce the initial number of verified alternatives beyond the first correct output in eight samples by
22   99.4% at Qwen2.5-0.5B, 71.6% at Falcon3-1B, and 41.5% at Qwen2.5-3B (Appendix Figure 10).
23   We therefore ask how training can keep discovered alternatives available.


24   2        M ODE B ENCH makes output identity executable

25   M ODE B ENCH assigns each accepted output an exact, executable identity: a canonical key whose
26   distinct values define verified modes (Figure 2). Keys capture computation routes in Countdown
27   and MathIR, tested program behaviors in Python, and valid configurations in Graph and PantryPlan.
28   Cosmetic aliases merge; distinct routes can share an answer (key rules: Appendix B.1). This measures
29   task-defined output support; it does not establish diversity of latent reasoning strategies.

31   ReplayMaxRL learns from new samples and revisits earlier verified successes (Figure 3).
32   MaxRL trains policy πθ for success within k samples across sampling budgets [Tajwar et al., 2026].
33   Each training prompt x keeps one policy-generated exemplar ec per admitted key in bank Bx . With
34   |ec | its token length, recurrent replay minimizes

35   Each retained key receives equal replay weight, independent of its frequency in fresh rollouts.
36   Replacing MaxRL with Dr.GRPO [Liu et al., 2025], which debiases GRPO [Shao et al., 2024], yields
37   ReplayDr.GRPO. Appendix K gives the full update; Appendix E analyzes collapse and retention in
38   idealized categorical models.
39   Related work. Prior work studies diversity collapse [Cui et al., 2025, Dang et al., 2025] and
40   success replay [Zhang et al., 2025]. We focus on executable identity for measurement and memory
41   (Appendix D).

69   5     Conclusion and Limits
70   M ODE B ENCH makes a missing part of evaluation measurable: the range of distinct, verified out-
71   comes a model can produce for one problem. This matters because different approaches can have
72   different strengths. One may succeed where another fails, or remain useful when constraints change.
73   Understanding diversity therefore gives a fuller picture of a model’s capabilities than accuracy alone.
74   Our results show that replay can preserve alternatives while improving held-out correctness. These
75   controlled tasks provide a starting point for studying diversity in broader mathematical reason-
76   ing. Replay retains only discovered, stored modes; individual-mode survival remains unmeasured
77   (Appendix L). We have not shown that broader support causes accuracy gains or helps solve later
78   problems. The next step is to test which alternatives help on richer mathematical tasks, and when.
