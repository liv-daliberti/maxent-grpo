\begin{abstract}
Repeated sampling helps only when a policy proposes distinct viable solutions.
Binary-reward RL can raise accuracy while concentrating on one correct
execution, so eight attempts buy one answer. We introduce \mb{}, five
executable domains whose validators jointly determine correctness and outcome
identity, and \xdr{}, which rewards and replays rare verified outcomes. On a
preregistered five-seed, 12-epoch surface covering all five domains on one
common design, \xdr{} raises \texttt{pass@8} and \texttt{distinct@8}
everywhere. PantryPlan exposes the trajectory:
Dr.GRPO raises \texttt{pass@1} $.388\to.525$ while \texttt{pass@8} falls
$.902\to.556$ and \texttt{distinct@8} $2.426\to.634$; \xdr{} ends at $.869$
and $2.186$ with a \emph{higher} \texttt{pass@1} of $.605$. Analysis explains
balanced replay, and a six-run MathIR ablation isolates $+.137$
\texttt{distinct@8} from separated support discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Test-time scaling promises to trade inference compute for better reasoning by
drawing multiple candidates and selecting among them. We study a failure that
breaks that trade: binary-verifier RL can raise accuracy while concentrating a
policy on one successful execution. Under group-relative binary-reward RL,
eight nominal attempts can become eight copies of the same answer---the system
looks better by correctness while its useful proposal support quietly
collapses.

Breadth is what test-time scaling consumes. Self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, best-of-$N$ \citep{brown2024monkeys},
verifier-guided search \citep{snell2024testtime}, and self-improvement
\citep{dong2023raft} all spend candidates, and a selector returns only a route
the policy proposes. Breadth
therefore decides whether more inference buys another chance or another copy:
RLVR can win at small $k$ yet lose at large $k$ \citep{yue2025rlvrlimit}, while
RLHF and fine-tuning can trade generalization or \texttt{pass@}$K$ for narrower
outputs \citep{kirk2024understanding,dang2025diversity}. Beyond exact tasks, a
one-bit reward flattens what it means to be right because a distribution over
acceptable answers and a point mass earn the same score.
Exact domains make this loss countable; plurality matters everywhere.
Standard alignment can reduce distributional pluralism
\citep{sorensen2024pluralistic}; related studies report a
``correct-answer effect'' on nuanced questions and flattened portrayals
of demographic groups \citep{park2024diversitythought,wang2025flatten}.

Useful breadth is difficult to define and preserve. String diversity overcounts
aliases, so answers need meaning-level clustering \citep{farquhar2024detecting},
and final answers undercount the programs that reach them
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}. Valid modes are unknown, credit must reach
verified novelty alone where novelty search and count-based bonuses pay for any
unvisited behavior \citep{lehman2011novelty,bellemare2016pseudocount}, and rare
modes can vanish before a prompt returns \citep{rolnick2019replay}. Binary
reward equates all correct responses
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,liu2025understanding}, while finite on-policy
sampling reinforces common modes and leaves absent modes untouched
\citep{cui2025entropy}. Repairing the failure therefore takes a signal bound to
executed mode identity.

Prior work documents collapse and promotes outcome bonuses, batch penalties,
pass@$K$ objectives, and set-level or distributional optimization
\citep{cui2025entropy,dang2025diversity,song2025outcome,divpo2025,
chen2025passktraining,li2025divergence}. But an open-set executable task needs
three properties jointly: semantic identity bound to correctness, credit only
for a genuinely new verified outcome, and memory that preserves a transient
discovery beyond its collection batch. Each supplies one or two; closing the gap
takes all three.

We introduce \mb{}, whose five domains derive correctness and canonical mode
identity from the same execution, and \xdr{}, a Dr.GRPO extension over the
policy's own verified outcomes. \xdr{} rewards rare and newly discovered
verified outcomes, retains them in a prompt-local bank, and schedules global
verified replay with mass and balance control. On the common five-seed
surface, matched Dr.GRPO makes neutral \texttt{pass@1}, neutral
\texttt{pass@8}, and mean \# distinct correct@8 nearly coincide; \xdr{} opens
that band in every domain. PantryPlan makes the loss temporal: Dr.GRPO raises
\texttt{pass@1} $.388\to.525$ while \texttt{pass@8} falls $.902\to.556$ and
\texttt{distinct@8} $2.426\to.634$; \xdr{} retains far more sampled support
at strictly higher accuracy. A completed MathIR ablation isolates $+.137$
\texttt{distinct@8} from separated support discovery. The exact 0.5B
environments are synthetic, and the MathIR actuator comparison supplies the
component-level causal claim.

\section{Mode Collapse under GRPO}
\label{sec:collapse}

The unit of diversity must be semantic. Surface strings overcount formatting
changes, while final answers can erase different programs or procedures. We
therefore identify a mode with a validator-positive executed outcome: the same
execution determines whether a response is correct and which canonical outcome
it realizes. Section~\ref{sec:modebench} gives the exact benchmark contract;
this section formalizes the failure it exposes.

\subsection{Correct mass and correct-mode distribution}

For prompt $x$, response $y$, and reference specification $s_x$, let
\[
  V(y,s_x)\in\{\bot\}\cup\mathcal C_x,
  \qquad r(x,y)=\1[V(y,s_x)\neq\bot].
\]
The policy's total correct mass is
$P_\theta^+(x)=\Pr_{Y\sim\pi_\theta}[V(Y,s_x)\neq\bot]$.
Conditional on correctness, its mode distribution is
\[
  p_\theta^+(c\mid x)
  =\Pr[V(Y,s_x)=c\mid V(Y,s_x)\neq\bot].
\]
We say training exhibits \emph{mode collapse} on $x$ when probability within
$p_\theta^+$ concentrates on a strict subset of $\mathcal C_x$ while
$P_\theta^+$ remains comparable or increases. Mode collapse is therefore a
statement about where correct mass sits: a high-accuracy policy on one correct
mode exhibits it.

Two population quantities describe the failure: conditional verified-mode
entropy
$H_\theta^+(x)=-\sum_c p_\theta^+(c\mid x)\log p_\theta^+(c\mid x)$
and concentration $\max_c p_\theta^+(c\mid x)$. They are unavailable when the
support is unknown. Given $K$ independent samples $Y_{1:K}$, define
\[
  D_K(x)=\left|\{V(Y_i,s_x):V(Y_i,s_x)\neq\bot\}\right|,
  \qquad P_K(x)=\1[D_K(x)>0].
\]
Evaluation reports $\texttt{distinct@}K=\E_x\E[D_K(x)]$ and
$\texttt{pass@}K=\E_x\E[P_K(x)]$. The first counts correct execution modes;
the second asks whether search finds any. We also report \texttt{mean@}$K$,
the fraction of draws that are correct, and greedy \texttt{pass@1}. Each
carries information the others leave out.

Two structural properties make this quartet a single diagnostic. Because $D_K\1[D_K>0]=D_K$, their ratio is an exact conditional
expectation,
$\texttt{modes-per-success}=\texttt{distinct@}K/\texttt{pass@}K
=\E[D_K\mid D_K>0]$,
the number of \emph{different} correct executions a successful $K$-sample set
contains; a value of $1$ means every success is the same success. And
$\texttt{mean@}K\le\texttt{pass@}K\le\texttt{distinct@}K$ always, with the
first inequality strict unless temperature-one sampling is degenerate. A tight
band across all four numbers is therefore a signature of a point-mass policy.

\subsection{Why equal reward concentrates correct modes}

For a group of $G$ responses, both variants center the binary reward on its
group mean, $A_i^{\mathrm{task}}=r_i-G^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^G r_j$; Dr.GRPO applies
one shared maximum-length normalization where GRPO divides by the group reward
standard deviation. Each rescaling is blind to \emph{which} correct mode a
response realizes, so correct responses in the same group receive the same task
advantage. That symmetry equalizes credit across identities and leaves mode
ratios free to follow sampling frequency; Lemma~\ref{lem:grpo-mean} shows the
two normalizations share a direction and differ only in speed.

A categorical toy model makes the feedback explicit. Let $z_c$ be a logit for
correct mode $c$, $p_c=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_c$, and
$P^+=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}p_c$. Gradient ascent on expected binary reward gives
\begingroup
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\[
  \dot z_c=\frac{\partial P^+}{\partial z_c}=p_c(1-P^+),
  \qquad
  \frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{p_c}{p_d}
  =(p_c-p_d)(1-P^+).
\]
\endgroup

While incorrect mass remains, the more common of two equally rewarded correct
modes receives the larger logit update. Finite on-policy groups sharpen the
asymmetry: an absent rare mode receives no update, and an all-correct group has
zero centered task advantage. Thus the first successful mode can become a
self-reinforcing and eventually locally frozen solution. This idealized
calculation isolates the mechanism: under binary reward the only force acting on
mode ratios is sampling frequency. Recent
analyses reach related conclusions through outcome-frequency amplification,
divergence choice, or set-level policy geometry
\citep{sinha2026expected,li2026distribution,li2026setpo}.

Appendix~\ref{app:theory} makes this precise. Under the infinitesimal
on-policy mean dynamics, Theorem~\ref{thm:grpo-collapse} shows that generic
binary-reward GRPO and Dr.GRPO trajectories select one correct mode, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:xdr-balance} with Corollary~\ref{cor:xdr-no-collapse} shows
that once the verified bank covers the support, xGRPO reverses the unstable
mode-ratio dynamics. The coverage assumption is stated explicitly: the theorems
act once the bank covers the support.

\section{\mb: Executable Mode Measurement}
\label{sec:modebench}

\subsection{Benchmark contract}

A benchmark instance is mode-verifiable only when $V$ satisfies two
requirements. First, it executes the object the model actually generated and
judges that execution. Second, the canonical key is derived from the same
execution that established correctness. Cosmetic aliases merge; different
executed outcomes stay distinct. This contract confines diversity credit to
verified responses and confines breadth to solutions the validator has
distinguished.

\noindent\textbf{Graph coloring.} Each prompt gives a graph, a partial
three-coloring, and hidden vertices; the validator reconstructs the completion,
preserves shown colors, and rejects monochromatic edges. The color vector is
the key.

\noindent\textbf{Countdown.} Each prompt gives three distinct integers and a
target, each usable once with $+,-,\times,\div$ and parentheses. The parser
executes in exact rational arithmetic and canonicalization sorts commutative
children, merging syntactic aliases while retaining genuinely different trees.

\noindent\textbf{Python factors.} Each prompt asks for \texttt{lambda n: EXPR}
on four composite inputs; a syntax-restricted external worker
(Appendix~\ref{app:python}) requires every returned $d$ to satisfy $1<d<n$ and
$n\bmod d=0$. The output vector is the key, so different program texts with
identical behavior merge. A prompt with inputs $(n_1,\ldots,n_m)$ has exactly
$\prod_j|D(n_j)|$ modes for $D(n)$ the proper nontrivial divisors; released
rows contain 16--3,600.

\noindent\textbf{MathIR.} Each prompt shows a linear equation and at most eight
lettered transformations, of which the model emits at most four. An interpreter
applies each to both sides and normalizes exactly after every step; the key is
the state trajectory. Each prompt has five exhaustively enumerated valid routes,
withheld from the policy and the bank.

\noindent\textbf{PantryPlan.} Each prompt asks for an exact integer-gram
allocation from six frozen ingredients subject to inventory, serving-step,
mass, nutrient, and dietary-tag constraints. The same decimal-arithmetic
validator accepts the allocation and keys it by the sorted set of ingredients
used: quantity and ordering aliases merge, while different feasible
formulations remain distinct. The registered evaluation split averages $18.19$
exact supports per prompt, all withheld from the policy and bank.

\noindent\textbf{Prompts.} Every domain is a single-turn prompt with a fixed
chat template and no few-shot examples; the exact rendered string the policy
receives in each domain, taken from the first row of that cohort's frozen
evaluation split, is reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\section{\xdr: Online Verified MaxEnt-Dr.GRPO}
\label{sec:method}

\xdr{}---short for \emph{executable-mode GRPO}---is an outcome-level
maximum-entropy surrogate layered on Dr.GRPO: it spreads mass over executed
verified outcomes and learns their support online, coupling rare/new-mode
advantages, verified replay, and self-referenced adaptive coefficients while
leaving the task advantage unchanged. One design rule governs
every component: \emph{a signal may act on an outcome only after that outcome
has been executed and accepted}, so discovery credit, replay, and balance all
read the same validator output.

Each component below was added in response to a prospectively recorded failure
of the preceding version; Appendix~\ref{app:ablations} gives that record and
what each component's evidence licenses, and Algorithm~\ref{alg:xdr-update} in
Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm} fixes the exact update.

\subsection{Online verified support}

For each prompt $x$ the learner maintains a bank $\Bpos$, initially empty, of
keys the current policy generated and $V$ accepted. Every group is scored
against one immutable pre-group snapshot and new keys are committed only after
the whole group, so novelty is invariant to row order. Admission is fail-closed
at the intersection of positive rollout reward and executable canonical
validation; a disagreement receives no key and is recorded. The matched
Dr.GRPO control runs the same validator and the same passive counts with all
auxiliary coefficients set to zero, so discovery telemetry is comparable across
arms without altering the control's objective.

\subsection{Open-set rare-mode and discovery advantages}

For a verified mode $a$, let $c_x(a)$ combine its historical count with its
leave-one-out multiplicity among the other verified responses in the group.
With pseudocount $\lambda=1$, the detached predictive distribution
$\hat q_x$ over the $J_x$ explicitly counted keys includes one structural
unseen bucket $\varnothing$:
\[
  \hat q_x(a)=\frac{c_x(a)+\lambda}
  {\sum_b c_x(b)+\lambda(J_x+1)},\qquad
  \hat q_x(\varnothing)=\frac{\lambda}
  {\sum_b c_x(b)+\lambda(J_x+1)}.
\]
The unseen bucket reserves mass for modes the bank has yet to see. This is an
executable-outcome analogue of count-based exploration
\citep{bellemare2016pseudocount}: only validator-positive visits receive a
count or bonus.

With surprisal clip $S=5$, define
$h_i=\min\{-\log\hat q_x(a_i),S\}$ and
$\bar h=\E_{a\sim\hat q_x}\min\{-\log\hat q_x(a),S\}$, and set
$A_i^{\mathrm{rare}}=\beta_t\,(h_i-\bar h)/S$.
Centering under $\hat q_x$, rather than under the empirical group, keeps a
carrier when the current correct samples all share one mode but the bank
records more. Let $\mathcal D_x$ be the verified keys absent from the pre-group
bank and $m_x(a)$ their current-group multiplicity. A first discovery receives
$A_i^{\mathrm{new}}=\nu\,\1[a_i\in\mathcal D_x]/m_x(a_i)$ with $\nu=0.50$, so
all duplicates of one new key share total credit $\nu$. The actor advantage is
\[
  A_i=A_i^{\mathrm{task}}
  +\1[V(y_i,s_x)\neq\bot]
   \left(A_i^{\mathrm{rare}}+A_i^{\mathrm{new}}\right).
\]
The mode terms enter uncentered, and novelty and entropy pressure reach
validator-positive responses alone.

\subsection{Global verified replay}
\label{sec:replay}

With hundreds of training prompts, a prompt-local discovery would otherwise
wait nearly a pass before acting again. After the first verified bank exists,
every optimizer update therefore schedules exactly one bank in deterministic
global round-robin order, retaining at most one policy-generated exemplar per
observed key up to 16 keys. Replay converts a transient discovery into a
persistent training constraint, paralleling rehearsal's role in resisting
forgetting \citep{rolnick2019replay}. Two losses apply: \emph{verified mass},
uniform exemplar likelihood for every scheduled bank including a singleton,
with coefficient $\mu_t$; and \emph{known-mode balance},
$\mathrm{KL}(U_{\mathrm{bank}}\|q_{\mathrm{model}})$ for banks with at least
two observed modes, with coefficient $\alpha_t$. Selection reads only
accumulated verified banks and a one-bank compute budget---never exhaustive
support, evaluation outcomes, a desired mode count, or reference solutions.
Before the first discovery, replay is inactive.

\subsection{Projection-free self-reference}

The rare-mode, verified-mass, and balance coefficients each warm up for 64
eligible observations and reference only their own warmup mean. Let $\rho$ be
normalized open-set entropy, $s$ verified-exemplar surprisal, and $h$ scheduled
bank entropy. With EMA decay $.9$,
\[
  \beta_t=.10\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{ema}}},\qquad
  \mu_t=.10\frac{s_{\mathrm{ema}}}{s_{\mathrm{ref}}},\qquad
  \alpha_t=.10\frac{h_{\mathrm{ref}}}{h_{\mathrm{ema}}}.
\]
Falling mode entropy increases exploration pressure; rising surprisal on known
correct exemplars increases mass pressure; falling bank entropy increases
balance pressure. No coefficient is projected. This is a stress-test design:
continued collapse remains visible as continued coefficient growth rather than
as silent saturation. Singleton banks are omitted from the balance sensor.

\subsection{Separated-support escape actuator}
\label{sec:actuator}

All balance mechanisms need an alternative to preserve, and a singleton bank
offers none. A registered extension therefore tests one support-only escape,
eligible only after controller warmup and only when the run's own entropy EMA
falls below its own warmup reference: a domain-specific, validator-preserving
transformation may propose at most one new verified key. Proposal rows never
enter PPO, and proposal-only support is isolated from the historical count
table behind the on-policy rare/new advantages---it may enter replay support,
but joins on-policy counts only once the neutral policy independently produces
and validates it. A same-plumbing actuator-off arm is the causal comparator.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Inference-time compute and verifier-based reasoning RL.}
Self-consistency, repeated sampling, and verifier-guided search generate a
candidate set and then aggregate or select from it
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency,brown2024monkeys,snell2024testtime,
lightman2023lets}. GRPO and Dr.GRPO instead train proposal policies from
group-relative outcome rewards
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1,liu2025understanding}. Dr.GRPO
fixes response-length normalization; our upstream question is orthogonal:
whether RL preserves enough probability \emph{within} equally rewarded correct
outcomes for additional inference to remain useful.

\noindent\textbf{Collapse and diversity-aware optimization.}
RLHF trades generalization against diversity \citep{kirk2024understanding},
RLVR can lose token entropy \citep{cui2025entropy} and can narrow the large-$k$
sampling boundary relative to the base model \citep{yue2025rlvrlimit}, and
fine-tuning can raise \texttt{pass@1} while \texttt{pass@}K falls
\citep{dang2025diversity}. Outcome bonuses, batch penalties, pass@$K$
objectives, semantic similarity, mass-covering divergences, set objectives, and
distribution matching target related failures
\citep{song2025outcome,divpo2025,chen2025passktraining,
li2025divergence,li2026setpo,li2026distribution,sinha2026expected}; clipping
asymmetries such as \citet{yu2025dapo} counteract entropy collapse at the token
level. Closest is \citet{song2025outcome}: their UCB counts final answers,
including incorrect ones, their batch method penalizes repeats, and bonuses are
disabled on all-correct batches; their 7B competition-math study is stronger
external evidence for the general problem. \xdr{} addresses a narrower
distinction: only validator-positive \emph{executed} keys receive novelty, and
replay lets a verified discovery act beyond its collection batch.

\noindent\textbf{Mode-covering objectives and archives.}
GFlowNets sample
in proportion to reward rather than maximizing it, and have been used to
fine-tune language models toward diverse high-reward reasoning chains
\citep{bengio2023gflownet,hu2024amortizing,zhu2025flowrl}. Quality-diversity
search maintains an explicit archive indexed by a behavior descriptor and
selects for novelty as well as fitness
\citep{lehman2011novelty,mouret2015illuminating}. \xdr{}'s bank is a
quality-diversity archive whose behavior descriptor is the validator's own
canonical key, and its balance loss targets the uniform-over-verified-support
distribution rather than the reward-proportional one. Execution supplies
identity, so the validator's key replaces the hand-designed descriptor space or
tractable target density both literatures require.

\noindent\textbf{Semantic maximum-entropy control.}
Maximum-entropy RL augments reward with policy entropy and can adapt its
coefficient \citep{ziebart2008maximum,levine2018control,haarnoja18b,
haarnoja2018algorithms}; grammar constraints can prevent invalid generations
\citep{geng2023grammar}. \xdr{} changes the random variable from tokens or a
known action set to open-set verified executions, combining empirical rare-mode
advantages with mass/balance replay into an outcome-level surrogate estimated
from the bank. Where semantic entropy clusters
generations by bidirectional entailment to estimate uncertainty
\citep{farquhar2024detecting}, \mb{} makes the equivalence relation exact and
adversarially safe by construction: Python uses a restricted grammar before
isolated execution and MathIR a finite command language, so the key cannot be
asserted by the model. The contract removes many false distinctions without
claiming every reasoning strategy is executable.

\section{Experimental Design and Evidence Policy}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Matched training and evaluation}

All five domains run one preregistered design: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct at a pinned
revision, seeds 43--47, group size 16, $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}=0$, rollout
temperature 1, and exactly 12 epochs through each fixed pool. Every domain
trains on 384 prompts and evaluates on a fixed 128-prompt split, giving 4,608
optimizer updates per run; earlier smaller-pool cohorts are superseded by that
common size and are not reported. PantryPlan's task-bound interface selects a
six-ingredient binary support mask and materializes one feasible allocation, so
it tests support retention rather than free-form recipe generation. Within every domain, arms share
data, model, optimizer, rollout count, training budget, evaluation seed,
checkpoint cadence, and recovery rules; only the \xdr{} mechanism differs.

Evaluation uses greedy decoding and four deterministic replicates of $K=8$
temperature-one samples, run four times per training pass. We report the five
fixed checkpoints $0,3,6,9,12$; every headline row is the deepest checkpoint
reached by all five seeds of both arms, which is the terminal pass 12 in every
domain.

\noindent\textbf{Is the gain bought with extra compute?} The arms take the same
optimizer steps over the same rollouts, so both perform identical
\emph{generation}---the dominant cost in verifier RL. The extra work is scoring
at most 16 already-generated exemplars per update at weight
$w_{\mathrm{rep}}=(G-1)/G^2\approx.06$ against a 16-rollout group, every one of
them produced and verified by the policy itself earlier in the same run: a
bounded reuse term over the same sample budget.

\subsection{Evidence policy}

All five domains are complete and terminally audited on this one design: the
same seeds, arms, reporting rule, and pass-12 endpoint, with each domain
standing on its own rather than pooled into a single test. The MathIR
same-plumbing actuator comparison supplies the component-level causal claim.
The evidence ledger, state-integrity rules, exclusions, dataset auditing, seed
rows, terminal causal rows, and cohort provenance are bound in
Appendices~\ref{app:reproducibility},~\ref{app:data},~\ref{app:per-seed},

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three distinctions organize the design. \emph{Identity}: accepted execution
defines a mode. \emph{Discovery versus balance}: balance acts on modes already
banked, so the isolated actuator targets coverage. \emph{Entropy versus
support}: token entropy raises per-position uncertainty; support counts verified
keys. The next test is a coverage--budget curve on larger executable tasks.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The common five-seed design and fixed reporting grid remove available-seed
averaging, but each domain still stands on its own rather than in a pooled
test. All domains are synthetic and exact identity is unusually available;
the benchmark and both arms run on two 0.5--1.7B instruction-tuned model
families, but every reported number comes from one
(Appendix~\ref{app:cross-family}). PantryPlan tests substitution within a declared item set and Python
Factors a restricted grammar in isolation, so our claims stop at those
interfaces. Baselines are limited to matched Dr.GRPO; beyond decoding
(Appendix~\ref{app:decoding}), matched token entropy and distribution matching
remain untested, as does whether a selector turns breadth into end-task
accuracy.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
Group-relative binary reward can improve accuracy while flattening ``right'' to
one answer. \mb{} makes that lost option value measurable across five
executable domains, categorical analysis explains it, and \xdr{} implements a
discover--retain--rebalance response. The completed five-seed surface shows the
same support-opening direction in every domain, and the MathIR ablation
isolates separated discovery. Exact verification is our microscope: elsewhere
many answers satisfy the same goal, and preserving one narrows what selection
recovers.

\subsubsection*{Ethics Statement}

The verified world is a microscope: what is mathematical here is our ability
to name and validate modes.
Preserving the diversity of what it means to be right
is therefore option value for reasoning, selection, and collective expression.
Creative-work studies find that AI
assistance can raise average ratings while making stories more similar and
users less semantically distinct
\citep{doshi2024creative,anderson2024homogenization}.
These studies show that quality and a narrower collective space can coexist;
establishing Dr.GRPO as the cause and showing that \xdr{}
transfers to open-ended tasks both remain open.
Where modes are hardest to enumerate, they may be easiest to erase.

This cuts both ways in deployment. Breadth is instrumentally valuable: a policy
proposing more distinct correct executions may also propose riskier ones, and
\texttt{distinct@}$K$ credits an alternative without asking what acting on it
costs. Systems adopting this objective should report correctness, invalidity,
and breadth together, and should map executed modes onto application-valued
distinctions rather than treating mode count as an end in itself. All
experiments here use synthetic, self-generated data with no human subjects and
no personal or scraped content.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility Statement}

Every quantitative claim is bound to a frozen artifact. One matching caveat is
explicit: GPU placement is not matched across arms, and it is not inert, because
the two GPU models in this cohort sample different tokens from identical weights
under identical draw seeds. Twenty-two of the twenty-five paired seeds ran both
arms on one GPU model and three pairs are split; the decoding control of
Appendix~\ref{app:decoding} pins every measurement to the GPU model that trained
its checkpoint.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} give the
matched protocol, the complete hyperparameter set, and the evidence ledger that
fixes what each comparison may claim; Appendix~\ref{app:data} records SHA-256
identities for all training and evaluation splits. Comparison protocols were
registered before results were observed and are included as
\path{paper/preregistration/*.md}. Bank, controller, scheduler, optimizer,
data-cursor, and request-stream state are checkpointed atomically, and
integrity violations---non-finite values, state mismatch, missing terminal
markers, or proposal leakage into PPO---fail closed rather than degrade
silently. Every figure is regenerated from hash-bound artifacts by a named
script listed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, and rendering aborts if a
displayed endpoint does not reproduce its frozen aggregate. Two superseded
cohorts are excluded by machine-readable audits written before their outcomes
were known.

\section{Why GRPO Collapses and xGRPO Does Not after Coverage}
\label{app:theory}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix supplies the formal claims
invoked in Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:results}.

This appendix separates two questions that are easy to conflate: whether a
binary-reward group update preserves ratios among already correct modes, and
whether xGRPO preserves every correct mode that exists. The first admits an
unconditional answer in a finite categorical model. The second necessarily
requires a coverage assumption: no method whose bank contains only executed,
validator-positive objects can protect a mode that is never sampled or
constructed. We therefore prove support preservation first and state full
no-collapse as a coverage corollary.

\subsection{Categorical reduction and the GRPO mean update}

Fix one prompt. Let the finite response categories be
$\mathcal A=\mathcal C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal N$, where the $m\ge2$
categories in $\mathcal C$ are correct execution modes and the $n\ge1$
categories in $\mathcal N$ are incorrect. Let
$p_a=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_a$, $P=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}p_c$, and
$q_c=p_c/P$. Thus $q$ is exactly the conditional correct-mode distribution
$p_\theta^+(\cdot\mid x)$ of Section~\ref{sec:collapse}, and $P$ is the correct
mass $P_\theta^+(x)$; it is not the detached count predictor $\hat q_x$ of
Section~\ref{sec:method}, which the policy never differentiates through.

Draw a group $A_1,\ldots,A_G\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}p$ with $G\ge2$,
write $R_i=\1[A_i\in\mathcal C]$, and let $M=\sum_iR_i$. Both GRPO variants
can be written, at the on-policy point where the PPO ratio equals one, as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat g_G
  =\frac1G\sum_{i=1}^G
   w(M)\left(R_i-\frac{M}{G}\right)\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}.
  \label{eq:group-estimator}
\end{equation}
For Dr.GRPO, $w(M)=1$ after absorbing the shared positive length scale. For
binary-reward GRPO, $w(M)$ is the reciprocal group reward standard deviation
on mixed groups. In either case $w(\ell)>0$ for
$\ell\in\{1,\ldots,G-1\}$; its value on all-equal groups is irrelevant because
the centered advantage is zero. We take explicit reference KL to be zero, as
in the experiments.

\begin{lemma}[A group baseline does not remove frequency amplification]
\label{lem:grpo-mean}
For $0<P<1$, the expected group update is
\begin{equation}
  \E[\widehat g_G]
  =c_G(P)\nabla_z P,
  \qquad
  c_G(P)=
  \frac{\E\!\big[w(M)M(G-M)\big]}{G^2P(1-P)}>0.
  \label{eq:expected-group-gradient}
\end{equation}
For Dr.GRPO, $c_G(P)=(G-1)/G$. Hence reward centering and binary reward
standardization change the speed, but not the expected direction, of the
binary-reward policy gradient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the complete binary reward vector, whose sum is $M$. The score
identities give
\[
  \E[\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}\mid R_i=1]=\nabla_z\log P,
  \qquad
  \E[\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}\mid R_i=0]
  =\nabla_z\log(1-P).
\]
There are $M$ correct rows with centered reward $(G-M)/G$ and $G-M$
incorrect rows with centered reward $-M/G$. Therefore the conditional
expectation of the unaveraged sum in~\eqref{eq:group-estimator} is
\[
  w(M)\frac{M(G-M)}{G}
  \left(\nabla_z\log P-\nabla_z\log(1-P)\right)
  =w(M)\frac{M(G-M)}{GP(1-P)}\nabla_zP.
\]
The outer factor $1/G$ and expectation over
$M\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(G,P)$ yield~\eqref{eq:expected-group-gradient}.
For $w\equiv1$,
$\E[M(G-M)]=G(G-1)P(1-P)$, proving the Dr.GRPO value.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Winner-take-all collapse under GRPO and Dr.GRPO}

Consider the infinitesimal expected-update flow
$\dot z=\E[\widehat g_G]=c_G(P)\nabla_zP$. This is the clean mean-field limit
of fresh on-policy groups and the first-order PPO update. It deliberately
removes sampling noise; the result below therefore identifies a structural
instability rather than blaming finite batches.

\begin{theorem}[Generic binary-reward GRPO mode collapse]
\label{thm:grpo-collapse}
Start from finite logits with $0<P(0)<1$. If the conditional correct-mode
distribution $q(0)$ has a unique largest coordinate $c^\star$, then
\[
  P(t)\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  q_{c^\star}(t)\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  q_c(t)\longrightarrow0\quad(c\ne c^\star).
\]
Thus both GRPO and Dr.GRPO converge to a single correct execution mode for
generic initial conditions, even though every mode in $\mathcal C$ has exactly
the same reward.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a correct mode $c$ and incorrect category $a$,
\[
  \dot z_c=c_G(P)p_c(1-P),
  \qquad
  \dot z_a=-c_G(P)p_aP.
\]
Also $\dot P=c_G(P)\lVert\nabla_zP\rVert_2^2>0$. If $P$ converged to an
interior value, continuity and positivity of $c_G$, together with
\[
 \lVert\nabla_zP\rVert_2^2
 \ge P^2(1-P)^2\left(\frac1m+\frac1n\right),
\]
would keep $\dot P$ bounded away from zero, a contradiction. Hence $P\to1$.

Define the effective optimization time
\[
  \tau(t)=\int_0^t c_G(P(s))P(s)(1-P(s))\,ds.
\]
If $\tau(\infty)<\infty$, every correct and incorrect logit would have finite
total variation, because the absolute velocity of each is at most $\dot\tau$.
All logits would then converge to finite values, contradicting $P\to1$.
Therefore $\tau(t)\to\infty$.

On the correct simplex, changing from $t$ to $\tau$ gives the replicator flow
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dq_c}{d\tau}
  =q_c\left(q_c-\sum_{d\in\mathcal C}q_d^2\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{d}{d\tau}\log\frac{q_c}{q_d}=q_c-q_d.
  \label{eq:grpo-replicator}
\end{equation}
The initially unique maximum remains the unique maximum, and every one of its
log-ratios increases. The Lyapunov quantity $\sum_cq_c^2$ is nondecreasing,
since its derivative is twice the variance of the coordinate values under
$q$. Every limit point of~\eqref{eq:grpo-replicator} is therefore uniform on
its positive support. Because $q_{c^\star}\ge1/m$ and its ratio to every other
coordinate stays strictly greater than one, no multi-coordinate uniform limit
is possible. The only limit point is the vertex $q_{c^\star}=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ties are nongeneric]
\label{cor:grpo-ties}
If exactly $k<m$ correct modes tie for the largest initial probability, symmetry
preserves the tie and the flow converges to the uniform distribution on those
$k$ modes while eliminating the rest. Only the exactly uniform initialization
over all $m$ correct modes avoids collapse. Any arbitrarily small perturbation
that creates a unique maximum reduces to Theorem~\ref{thm:grpo-collapse}.
\end{corollary}

Finite groups make this instability operationally harsher: a mode absent from
a group has no score carrier, all-correct groups have zero task advantage, and
sampling noise almost surely breaks exact ties. These facts are not needed for
the theorem.

\subsection{Why the xGRPO terms reverse the instability}

The open-set actor signal has a direct maximum-entropy interpretation before
clipping and count approximation.

\begin{lemma}[Centered surprisal is the execution-mode entropy gradient]
\label{lem:surprisal-entropy}
Let $C\sim q$ on a known correct support and
$\mathcal H(q)=-\sum_cq_c\log q_c$. Then
\[
  \E_{C\sim q}
  \left[\bigl(-\log q_C-\mathcal H(q)\bigr)
  \nabla_z\log q_C\right]
  =\nabla_z\mathcal H(q).
\]
Thus, when the detached predictor equals the current correct-mode distribution,
the centered rare-mode advantage is an unbiased entropy-gradient estimator.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The baseline vanishes by the score identity. For the remaining term,
\[
  \sum_cq_c(-\log q_c)\nabla_z\log q_c
  =-\sum_c(\log q_c)\nabla_zq_c
  =\nabla_z\mathcal H(q),
\]
because $\sum_c\nabla_zq_c=0$ supplies the missing constant in the derivative
of $-q_c\log q_c$.
\end{proof}

The implemented pseudocount, unseen bucket, and surprisal clip make this a
robust surrogate rather than an exact identity. The one-time new-mode term
adds total credit $\nu$ independent of the number of duplicates once a new key
appears. Neither signal can assign gradient to a never-observed zero-carrier
mode; that is why verified replay and the coverage condition below matter.
The next result needs only the balance term; it does not count any additional
restoring pressure from rare-mode or verified-mass replay. We index time by mean
updates acting on this prompt, absorbing fixed round-robin frequency and replay
scaling into the effective coefficient $a(t)$; the categorical abstraction
excludes cross-prompt parameter interference.

Let a verified bank contain $m\ge2$ modes, let $u_c=1/m$, and let $q$ be the
model distribution normalized over those bank modes. In the categorical
projection, descending the implemented balance loss
$L(q)=\mathrm{KL}(u\|q)$ contributes $a(t)(u-q)$ to the mode logits. Binary
GRPO contributes the amplifying term $b(t)q$, where
$b(t)=c_G(P)P(1-P)$ in the mean flow. Terms shared by every correct-mode logit
are denoted $d(t)\mathbf1$ and do not change $q$.

\begin{theorem}[Verified balance makes the uniform bank the unique attractor]
\label{thm:xdr-balance}
Suppose that after some time $T$ the bank is fixed and scheduled infinitely
often, and its mean conditional logit dynamics are
\begin{equation}
  \dot z=b(t)q+a(t)(u-q)+d(t)\mathbf1.
  \label{eq:xdr-mode-flow}
\end{equation}
If $a(t)>b(t)$ eventually and
$\int_T^\infty(a(t)-b(t))\,dt=\infty$, then
\[
  \mathrm{KL}(u\|q(t))\longrightarrow0,
  \qquad q(t)\longrightarrow u.
\]
Consequently every bank mode retains asymptotic probability $1/m$ and
conditional mode collapse is impossible on that bank.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\lambda(t)=a(t)-b(t)$. Since $u=(1/m)\mathbf1$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:xdr-mode-flow} is, up to a common-logit shift,
\[
  \dot z=-\lambda(t)(q-u)+\widetilde d(t)\mathbf1.
\]
For $L(z)=\mathrm{KL}(u\|\operatorname{softmax}(z))$,
$\nabla_zL=q-u$ and $(q-u)^\top\mathbf1=0$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \dot L=-\lambda(t)\lVert q-u\rVert_2^2\le0.
  \label{eq:kl-decay}
\end{equation}
Use replay time $s=\int_T^t\lambda(r)dr$, which diverges by assumption.
Equation~\eqref{eq:kl-decay} is ordinary gradient flow for $L$ modulo the
irrelevant common-logit direction. Its sublevel sets are compact in the open
simplex because $\mathrm{KL}(u\|q)\to\infty$ at the boundary, and its only
stationary distribution is $q=u$. Therefore $q(t)\to u$ and $L(t)\to0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[xGRPO no-collapse guarantee after verified coverage]
\label{cor:xdr-no-collapse}
Assume the categorical mean-flow model above and:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item every correct execution mode enters and remains in the verified bank by
  a finite time---so the protected support fits within replay capacity;
  \item deterministic global replay visits that bank infinitely often;
  \item the balance controller is active with reference entropy
  $h_{\mathrm{ref}}>0$ and tracks bank entropy through
  $a(t)=a_0h_{\mathrm{ref}}/h_{\mathrm{ema}}(t)$; and
  \item correct mass approaches one, so the GRPO amplification
  $b(t)=c_G(P)P(1-P)$ approaches zero.
\end{enumerate}
Then $q(t)\to u$ on the full correct support. In particular,
\[
  \min_{c\in\mathcal C}q_c(t)\longrightarrow\frac1m,
  \qquad
  \mathcal H(q(t))\longrightarrow\log m,
\]
so mode collapse cannot emerge asymptotically.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Because $0<h_{\mathrm{ema}}(t)\le\log m$ on eligible replay observations,
$a(t)\ge a_0h_{\mathrm{ref}}/\log m=:a_{\min}>0$. Since $b(t)\to0$,
eventually $a(t)-b(t)\ge a_{\min}/2$, whose integral over infinitely many
replay visits diverges. Theorem~\ref{thm:xdr-balance} applies. Moreover, if
entropy attempted to approach zero before that regime,
$h_{\mathrm{ema}}\to0$ would make $a(t)\to\infty$ while $b(t)$ remains
bounded, so the boundary cannot be an attracting limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact scope of the guarantee]
If the verified bank covers only $\mathcal B\subsetneq\mathcal C$---including
when the true support exceeds the implementation's capacity 16---the theorem
guarantees non-collapse and asymptotic balance on $\mathcal B$, not discovery
of $\mathcal C\setminus\mathcal B$. The open-set unseen bucket, first-discovery
credit, and separated-support actuator are mechanisms for satisfying coverage;
they are not a catalogue oracle. Likewise, the proof concerns realizable
categorical mean dynamics with recurrent replay. It does not claim global
convergence for an arbitrary nonconvex language model. This distinction is
what makes the guarantee falsifiable rather than rhetorical.
\end{remark}

\section{External Python Verification Contract}
\label{app:python}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix expands the Python Factors
contract in Section~\ref{sec:modebench} and its limitation in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

A Python response is parsed in expression mode and must be a lambda with one
argument named $n$. Accepted nodes are integer constants, $n$, bounded
arithmetic, comparisons, Boolean operators, unary operators, and conditional
expressions. Calls, imports, attributes, comprehensions, containers,
subscripts, assignment, loops, and every other identifier are rejected before
execution. Candidate length and AST size are bounded.

The parent sends restricted source and trusted JSON task data to a persistent
external interpreter over JSON lines. The worker reparses, removes builtins,
evaluates the lambda, and invokes it on all frozen inputs under a per-request
timer. It returns only validity, the integer vector, and its key. Timeout,
broken pipe, malformed output, non-integer values, or divisor failure returns
no key. The parent can restart a failed worker.

\section{Algorithmic Details}
\label{app:algorithm}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix formalizes the state transition
summarized by Figure~\ref{fig:xdr-mechanism} and Section~\ref{sec:method}.
For each prompt $x$, let $\mathcal T_x$ be the historical on-policy key
\emph{tally} used by the semantic actor (distinct from $\mathcal C_x$, the
set of correct modes that exist), $\mathcal R_x$ the capacity-$M$
replay-exemplar state, $\tau$ the global replay cursor, and $\Gamma$ the three
controller states. The separation $\mathcal T_x\ne\mathcal R_x$ matters only
when the optional support proposal is enabled.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{One \xdr{} optimizer update}
  \label{alg:xdr-update}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Policy $\pi_\theta$; prompt $x$ and verifier specification
      $s_x$; group size $G$; states $(\mathcal T,\mathcal R,\tau,\Gamma)$
    \ENSURE Atomically updated $(\theta',\mathcal T',\mathcal R',
      \tau',\Gamma')$
    \STATE Freeze $\mathcal T_x^-\gets\mathcal T_x$ and
      $\mathcal R_x^-\gets\mathcal R_x$; read coefficients
      $(\beta_t,\mu_t,\alpha_t)$ from $\Gamma$
    \STATE Draw the on-policy group
      $\mathcal G=\{y_i\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\}_{i=1}^G$
    \FOR{$i=1,\ldots,G$}
      \STATE Execute $\widetilde c_i\gets V(y_i,s_x)$ and observe task
        reward $r_i$
      \STATE Set $c_i\gets\widetilde c_i$ iff
        $r_i>0\wedge\widetilde c_i\ne\bot$; otherwise set $c_i\gets\bot$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Compute centered Dr.GRPO task advantages
      $A_i^{\mathrm{task}}$ from $\{r_j\}_{j=1}^G$
    \FOR{$i=1,\ldots,G$ with $c_i\ne\bot$}
      \STATE Form the detached leave-one-out open-set predictor
        $\hat q_{x,-i}$ from $\mathcal T_x^-$ and
        $\{c_j:j\ne i,c_j\ne\bot\}$
      \STATE Compute predictive-centered rarity
        $A_i^{\mathrm{rare}}$ and split one first-seen credit
        $A_i^{\mathrm{new}}$ across equal new keys
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Set $A_i\gets A_i^{\mathrm{task}}+
      \1[c_i\ne\bot](A_i^{\mathrm{rare}}+A_i^{\mathrm{new}})$
    \IF{the registered singleton gate is eligible}
      \STATE Construct at most one domain transformation $\widetilde y$;
        re-execute it with $V$
      \IF{$V(\widetilde y,s_x)$ is a new verified key}
        \STATE Add its exemplar only to $\mathcal R_x$; never add it to
          $\mathcal G$, PPO, or $\mathcal T_x$
      \ENDIF
    \ENDIF
    \STATE After all group scores are fixed, commit each neutral verified
      $c_i$ to $\mathcal T_x$ and its first exemplar to $\mathcal R_x$
    \STATE $(z,\tau')\gets\textsc{NextBank}
      (\{\mathcal R_u\}_u,\tau)$ by deterministic global round robin
    \IF{a replay bank $\mathcal R_z$ exists}
      \STATE For each retained exemplar $e_c$, compute the length-normalized
        score $s_c=|e_c|^{-1}\log\pi_\theta(e_c\mid z)$
      \STATE $L_{\mathrm{mass}}\gets-|\mathcal R_z|^{-1}
        \sum_{c\in\mathcal R_z}s_c$
      \STATE $L_{\mathrm{balance}}\gets
        \1[|\mathcal R_z|>1]\,
        \mathrm{KL}(U(\mathcal R_z)\|\operatorname{softmax}_c s_c)$
    \ELSE
      \STATE $L_{\mathrm{mass}},L_{\mathrm{balance}}\gets0$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $w_{\mathrm{rep}}\gets(G-1)/G^2$ for one replay
      pseudo-group per $G$ on-policy rows
    \STATE $\theta'\gets\textsc{Update}
      (\theta,L_{\mathrm{PPO}}(A)+w_{\mathrm{rep}}
      [\mu_tL_{\mathrm{mass}}+\alpha_tL_{\mathrm{balance}}])$
    \STATE Update only eligible controller EMAs/references to obtain
      $\Gamma'$ for the next update
    \STATE Checkpoint $(\theta',\mathcal T',\mathcal R',\tau',\Gamma')$
      as one atomic state
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The pre-update controller values act throughout the current transition; their
updated values first act on the next one. The base objective omits the optional
singleton block; the actuator variant enables it with separated support. Neither version
uses a gold mode catalogue, direct token entropy, or the historical prompt-local
bank-entropy actor channel (\texttt{bank\_alpha}$=0$). Fixed doses, warmups,
and capacities are experimental constants in Section~\ref{sec:experiments},
not steps of the algorithm.

\section{Decoding Control: Does a Wider Sampler Repair the Collapse?}
\label{app:decoding}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix supports the decoding statement in
Section~\ref{sec:results} and narrows the baseline limitation in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

Every headline cell samples at temperature one. A reader may reasonably ask
whether matched Dr.GRPO's missing modes are merely improbable rather than
absent: if a modestly hotter sampler recovered them, the finding would concern
sharpening at a fixed decoding setting, not loss of support. That is a different
scientific claim, so we test it directly.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol.} We re-measure all $50$ frozen terminal checkpoints
(five domains $\times$ two arms $\times$ seeds 43--47) at six sampling
temperatures $T\in\{0.5,0.7,1.0,1.3,1.6,2.0\}$, giving $300$ measurement cells.
Each cell loads one checkpoint and runs the \emph{same} evaluation path used
during training---the same validator, prompts, split, response budget, $K=8$,
and four fixed draw seeds---while performing no rollout, optimizer step, or
export, so a cell is a pure function of the checkpoint and its decoding setting.
Two controls make the sweep admissible. First, every cell at $T=1$ must
reproduce its run's published terminal value within
$\max(3\,\mathrm{SE},.02)$ for rates and $\max(3\,\mathrm{SE},.05)$ for mode
counts; all $200$ such checks pass, the great majority exactly. Second, each cell
runs on the GPU model that trained its checkpoint, because floating-point
reduction order differs between GPU models and changes which tokens are sampled
from identical weights under identical draw seeds. That effect is visible in the
frozen pass-0 rows, which are identical within each GPU group and differ between
them. It also bears on the headline cohort: twenty-two of the twenty-five paired
seeds ran both arms on one GPU model and three pairs are split, so the sweep
below pins placement where the headline design does not.

\noindent\textbf{Estimand.} For arm $a$ and domain $d$ define the
\emph{temperature-repair index}
\[
  \rho_{a,d}
  =\frac{\max_T\ \texttt{distinct@8}(a,d,T)}
        {\texttt{distinct@8}(\xdr,d,1.0)}.
\]
The numerator maximizes over the whole sweep \emph{with no accuracy
constraint}, so the baseline may spend arbitrary correctness to buy breadth.
A value below one means that no decoding setting recovers the treatment's
temperature-one breadth.

\begin{table}[H]

\noindent\textbf{Result.} In four of the five domains the baseline's breadth is
nearly invariant to temperature. Graph coloring moves $.323\to.327$ across the
entire range, and Python factors is constant at $.172$ through $T=1.3$, while
accuracy degrades sharply---Python \texttt{mean@8} falls $.172\to.029$ at
$T=2$. Heating the sampler produces invalid text, not a second verified
execution. PantryPlan is the one domain where temperature does buy the baseline
breadth at no accuracy cost ($.597\to.719$ as $T$ rises to $2$ with
\texttt{mean@8} steady near $.52$), which its eight-token support-mask interface
makes possible because it has no malformed-output failure mode; even there the
gain closes about a third of the gap. Across all five domains
$\rho\le.73$, and in four of five \xdr{}'s breadth at \emph{every} temperature
exceeds the baseline's breadth at its \emph{own best} temperature; on Python
that holds at every $T<2$, where both arms stop returning valid output.

The same sweep shows the reported operating point is not tuned in the
treatment's favor: \xdr{}'s breadth peaks at $T=1.0$--$1.3$ in four domains, so
temperature one is at or near its own optimum. PantryPlan is again the
exception, with \xdr{} continuing to $2.896$ at $T=2$; reporting temperature one
understates the treatment there.

We therefore read the failure as loss of support rather than sharpening: the
absent modes are not waiting at low probability for a hotter sampler. This
control covers decoding of the trained policies only. It says nothing about the
alternative training objectives listed in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Cross-Family Replication on a Second Base Model}
\label{app:cross-family}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix records the registered design of
the second-base-model replication referenced in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} and Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. \emph{It
reports no outcome.} At submission the cohort is still training and no
evaluation checkpoint of it has been read, so nothing in this paper's claims
rests on it.

\noindent\textbf{Why.} Every headline cell is produced by one base model. A
reader may reasonably ask whether the collapse of matched Dr.GRPO's metric band,
and its reopening under \xdr{}, is a property of the objective or of that one
model family's tokenizer and instruction tuning. The registered replication
answers that directly by re-running the whole five-domain design on
Falcon3-1B-Instruct at a pinned revision: an independent line in tokenizer,
pretraining corpus, and instruction tuning, and roughly three times the
parameter count. The parameter count is deliberately not matched, because no
comparison in the study is between the two base models; every comparison is
within a domain, between the two arms, at a matched seed.

\noindent\textbf{What is held fixed.} The five domains, both arms, seeds
43--47, 12 epochs over 384 training prompts with a fixed 128-prompt evaluation
split, group size 16, $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}=0$, rollout temperature 1, the
evaluation protocol, the reporting rule, and every \xdr{} coefficient at its
value in this paper. No coefficient is retuned for the new model.

\noindent\textbf{What necessarily changes.} Three things, each forced by the
base model rather than chosen, and each applied identically to both arms.
First, the \emph{chat surface}: Falcon3 was instruction-tuned on different role
markers, so each domain uses a twin of its prompt contract that shares the
system instruction and the canonical answer rewrite verbatim and differs only in
those markers; the rendering reproduces the model's own published chat template
byte for byte. Second, \emph{optimizer-state placement}: at 1.67B parameters the
weights, master copy, and Adam moments do not fit a 24\,GB device alongside a
collocated inference engine, so optimizer state is offloaded to host memory,
which changes where state lives and not the update computed. Third, two
\emph{response budgets}. Falcon3 is uniformly more verbose than
Qwen2.5-0.5B, and budgets calibrated to the latter's terseness truncate it:
measured against the paired runs before any outcome was read, the manuscript's
budget bound 44\% of Falcon rollouts on Python factors and 14\% on MathIR while
binding the Qwen cohort on 20 of 4,609 and 0 of 4,609 responses respectively.
Those two budgets were raised until truncation was negligible. Graph coloring
(6\%) and Countdown (2\%) were left at the manuscript values, and PantryPlan is
unaffected because its canonical sampler emits a fixed six-token action.

\noindent\textbf{What transfers without modification.} The canonical action
spaces resolve to the same horizon, sequence count, and maximum sequence
entropy under the new tokenizer as under the original: $3/27$ for Graph
coloring, $3/108$ for Countdown, and $6/64$ for PantryPlan. The objective is
therefore defined over the same action geometry in both families, and the
reward and validator paths are surface-independent.

\noindent\textbf{Registered prediction.} Stated before launch and deliberately
qualitative: matched Dr.GRPO's four metrics will again coincide, with
modes-per-success near one, in at least four of the five domains; \xdr{} will
raise \texttt{distinct@8} in at least four of the five; and \xdr{} will not
reduce \texttt{pass@8} below the matched baseline in any domain. Absolute values
are \emph{not} predicted to reproduce this paper's numbers, and no threshold on
their agreement is registered. A different base model has different task
competence; what is under test is the direction and the presence of the band
collapse, not the magnitude.

\noindent\textbf{Claim boundary.} A negative or mixed result is reportable and
will be reported. If the effect does not reproduce, the correct conclusion is
that the result as stated is contingent on the base model, and this paper's
claim will be narrowed accordingly rather than the cohort excluded. Because two
response budgets differ from the manuscript's, the replication's Python factors
and MathIR rows are not comparable to a run of the same model at the original
budgets, and the reported cohort uses the raised-budget runs throughout.
