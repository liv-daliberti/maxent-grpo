\title{Mode Collapse under GRPO:\\
\mb{} and x-Mode GRPO}

\author{Anonymous Authors}

\begin{abstract}
Repeated sampling helps only if a policy proposes distinct viable solutions.
Binary-reward RL can raise accuracy while \textit{erasing} breadth: equally
rewarded correct answers compete through sampling frequency, so eight attempts
become copies. We introduce \mb{}, six executable domains where execution
determines correctness and canonical identity: five static-response and one
interactive MuJoCo maze. We introduce \xmode{}, which adds verified replay to
Dr.GRPO: one validator-positive exemplar per observed execution mode is retained
and revisited with uniform likelihood. In a
finite categorical mean flow, any positive recurrent dose prevents banked
modes from vanishing; full verified coverage rules out winner-take-all
collapse. A dated interim snapshot is positive in every currently paired
breadth panel, but coverage is incomplete and no final performance claim is
made. Accuracy conceals collapse; \mb{} makes monotony countable and \xmode{}
makes retention testable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Inference-time scaling promises to trade inference compute for better reasoning by drawing multiple candidates and selecting among them.
We study a failure that breaks that trade: binary-verifier Reinforcement Learning (RL) Language Model (LM) post-training can raise accuracy while concentrating a policy on one successful execution.
Under group-relative binary-reward RL, eight nominal attempts can become eight copies of the same answer. As we show in Figure \ref{fig:story}, standard Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)~\citep{shao2024deepseekmath, liu2025understanding} looks better by correctness after training while its useful proposal support quietly collapses.

Inference-time scaling requires genuine solution diversity. Self-consistency
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, best-of-$N$ \citep{brown2024monkeys},
verifier-guided search \citep{snell2024testtime}, and self-improvement
\citep{dong2023raft} all generate candidates, and a selector returns only a route the policy proposes.
Breadth therefore decides whether more inference buys another chance or another copy: RLVR can win at small $k$ yet lose at large $k$ \citep{yue2025rlvrlimit}, while RLHF and fine-tuning can trade generalization or \texttt{pass@}$K$ for narrower outputs \citep{kirk2024understanding,dang2025diversity}.

Useful response breadth is difficult to define and preserve. Raw strings
overcount formatting aliases, while final answers can merge distinct executable
programs or procedures. In exact tasks, validator-produced canonical execution
keys provide task-relevant identity. Valid modes are unknown, but a
validator-positive execution can still be retained after it is observed, and
rare modes can vanish before a prompt returns \citep{rolnick2019replay}. Binary reward equates all correct responses
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,liu2025understanding}, while finite on-policy
sampling reinforces common modes and leaves absent modes untouched
\citep{cui2025entropy}. Repairing the failure therefore takes a signal bound to
executed mode identity.

Prior work documents collapse and promotes outcome bonuses, batch penalties,
pass@$K$ objectives, and set-level or distributional optimization
\citep{cui2025entropy,dang2025diversity,song2025outcome,divpo2025,
chen2025passktraining,li2025divergence}. Existing work nevertheless lacks an
execution-grounded benchmark in which the same validator determines correctness
and outcome identity. \xmode{} adds one intervention to Dr.GRPO: verified replay
retains a validator-positive output beyond its collection batch.

We introduce \mb{}, whose six domains derive correctness and
canonical mode identity from the same execution, and evaluate \xmode{} over the
policy's own accepted outcomes. Five are static-response; PointMaze asks the
same Dr.GRPO-versus-\xmode{} question of an LM acting sequentially in an
interactive MuJoCo environment. The registered evaluation spans aligned
Qwen2.5-0.5B, Falcon3-1B, and Qwen2.5-3B comparisons. Throughout, \emph{\xmode{}} names only Dr.GRPO with verified replay as its
sole auxiliary derivative; \emph{verified replay} names that mechanism. Older

\section{Mode Collapse under GRPO}
\label{sec:collapse}

The unit of diversity must be task-relevant and execution-grounded. Surface
strings overcount formatting changes, while final answers can erase different
programs or procedures. We therefore identify a mode with a validator-positive
executed outcome: the same execution determines whether a response is correct
and which canonical outcome it realizes. This section fixes notation and
formalizes the failure the contract exposes.

\subsection{Prompts, validators, and policy-induced quantities}
\label{sec:collapse-setup}

Let $\mathcal X$ denote prompts and $\mathcal Y$ responses. Every prompt
$x\in\mathcal X$ carries a reference specification $s_x$ and a finite nonempty
set $\mathcal C_x$ of \emph{correct execution modes} (canonical keys). A
\emph{validator} is a map
\[
  V(\cdot\,,s_x):\mathcal Y\to\mathcal C_x\cup\{\bot\}
\]
that executes a response and returns either the canonical key of its verified
outcome or the failure symbol $\bot$; the binary task reward is
$r(x,y)=\1[V(y,s_x)\neq\bot]$. The learner observes only $V$'s output on the
responses it produces: neither $\mathcal C_x$ nor $|\mathcal C_x|$ is
available to it.

A policy $\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ over $\mathcal Y$ induces two objects. The
\emph{correct mass}
\(
  P_\theta^+(x)
  =\Pr_{Y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}\bigl[V(Y,s_x)\neq\bot\bigr]
\)
is the probability that a sample verifies. Whenever $P_\theta^+(x)>0$, the
\emph{conditional correct-mode distribution} on $\mathcal C_x$ is
\[
  p_\theta^+(c\mid x)
  =\Pr_{Y\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)}
   \bigl[V(Y,s_x)=c\;\big|\;V(Y,s_x)\neq\bot\bigr].
\]
Two population summaries of $p_\theta^+$ measure breadth: the verified-mode
entropy
$H_\theta^+(x)=-\sum_{c\in\mathcal C_x}
 p_\theta^+(c\mid x)\log p_\theta^+(c\mid x)$
and the concentration $\max_{c\in\mathcal C_x}p_\theta^+(c\mid x)$.
These are diagnostic population quantities, not training inputs.

\begin{definition}[Mode collapse]
\label{def:collapse}
\textit{A training trajectory $(\theta_t)_{t\ge0}$ exhibits \emph{mode collapse} on
$x$ when $p_{\theta_t}^+(\cdot\mid x)$ concentrates on a strict subset of
$\mathcal C_x$ as $t$ grows while $P_{\theta_t}^+(x)$ remains comparable or
increases.}
\end{definition}

Mode collapse is therefore a statement about where correct mass sits rather than accuracy: a high-accuracy policy concentrated on one correct mode exhibits mode collapse.

\subsection{Sample diagnostics}
\label{sec:collapse-metrics}

$H_\theta^+$ and the concentration are unavailable when the support is
unknown, so evaluation estimates breadth from samples. Fix $K\in\mathbb N$
and draw $Y_1,\ldots,Y_K\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}
\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ at temperature one. Define
\[
  D_K(x)=\bigl|\{V(Y_i,s_x):i\le K\}\setminus\{\bot\}\bigr|,
  \qquad
  S_K(x)=\1[D_K(x)>0],
  \qquad
  F_K(x)=\frac1K\sum_{i=1}^K r(x,Y_i),
\]
the number of distinct verified modes among the $K$ draws, the indicator that
at least one draw verifies, and the fraction of draws that verify. With $K$ draws jointly, evaluation reports
\[
  \texttt{distinct@}K=\E[D_K],
  \qquad
  \texttt{pass@}K=\E[S_K],
  \qquad
  \texttt{mean@}K=\E[F_K],
\]
together with greedy $\texttt{pass@1}=\E_x\bigl[r(x,y^{\mathrm{gr}}(x))\bigr]$
for $y^{\mathrm{gr}}(x)$ the deterministic greedy decode.
$\texttt{distinct@}K$ counts correct execution modes; $\texttt{pass@}K$ asks
whether search finds any. The three joint-sample metrics form an ordered
diagnostic; greedy $\texttt{pass@1}$ is reported separately.

\begin{lemma}[Ordered sample diagnostics]
\label{lem:sample-ordering}
(i) $F_K\le S_K\le D_K$ pointwise, hence
$\texttt{mean@}K\le\texttt{pass@}K\le\texttt{distinct@}K$, and the first
inequality is strict in expectation unless, almost surely, either no draw or
every draw verifies. (ii) Because $D_K\1[D_K>0]=D_K$,
\[
  \frac{\texttt{distinct@}K}{\texttt{pass@}K}
  =\E\bigl[D_K\;\big|\;D_K>0\bigr]
  \eqqcolon\texttt{modes-per-success},
\]
the mean number of \emph{different} correct executions a successful
$K$-sample set contains; a value of $1$ means each successful set contains
only one distinct execution. That execution may differ across prompts or
independent sample sets.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $F_K\in[0,1]$ with $F_K>0\Rightarrow S_K=1$, and
$S_K=1\Rightarrow D_K\ge1$; moreover $F_K=S_K$ exactly when the number of
verifying draws is $0$ or $K$. (ii)
$\E[D_K]/\Pr[D_K>0]=\E[D_K\1[D_K>0]]/\Pr[D_K>0]=\E[D_K\mid D_K>0]$.
\end{proof}

A tight band among $\texttt{mean@}K$, $\texttt{pass@}K$, and
$\texttt{distinct@}K$ is therefore a sample-level signature of collapse:
successful $K$-sample sets reveal almost no additional execution beyond the
first. Agreement with greedy $\texttt{pass@1}$ is a separate empirical
observation rather than part of the ordering.

\subsection{Why equal reward concentrates correct modes}
\label{sec:collapse-mechanism}

\noindent\textbf{Group-relative task advantages.}
Fix a prompt $x$ and draw a group
$Y_1,\ldots,Y_G\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)$ with
$G\ge2$ and rewards $r_i=r(x,Y_i)$. Both variants center the binary reward on
its group mean,
$A_i^{\mathrm{task}}=r_i-G^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^G r_j$; Dr.GRPO then applies one
shared maximum-length normalization where GRPO divides by the group reward
standard deviation. Every quantity in these rescalings is a function of
$(r_1,\ldots,r_G)$ alone, and $r_i$ is blind to \emph{which} key $V$
returned, so correct responses in the same group receive the same task
advantage. That symmetry equalizes credit across identities and leaves mode
ratios free to follow sampling frequency; Lemma~\ref{lem:grpo-mean} shows that
the expected GRPO and Dr.GRPO updates are positive scalar multiples of
$\nabla P$, so they share a direction and differ only in speed.

\noindent\textbf{A categorical toy model.}
Fix $x$ and suppress it from the notation. Partition the response categories
as $\mathcal A=\mathcal C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal N$, with
$m=|\mathcal C|\ge2$ correct modes and $n=|\mathcal N|\ge1$ incorrect
categories; give each category $a\in\mathcal A$ a logit $z_a$ and set
$p_a=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_a$, correct mass
$P=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}p_c$, and conditional mode distribution
$q_c=p_c/P$---the categorical projections of $P_\theta^+(x)$ and
$p_\theta^+(\cdot\mid x)$, in the notation of Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.
It therefore suffices to inspect gradient ascent on the expected binary reward
$\E_{A\sim p}\1[A\in\mathcal C]=P$. For $c,d\in\mathcal C$,
\begingroup
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{4pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\[
  \dot z_c=\frac{\partial P}{\partial z_c}=p_c(1-P),
  \qquad
  \frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{q_c}{q_d}
  =\frac{d}{dt}\log\frac{p_c}{p_d}
  =(p_c-p_d)(1-P).
\]
\endgroup

While incorrect mass remains ($P<1$), the more common of two equally rewarded
correct modes receives the larger logit update---and the conditional ratios
$q_c/q_d$ inherit the same dynamics. Finite on-policy groups sharpen the
asymmetry: an absent rare mode receives no update, and an all-correct group
has zero centered task advantage. Thus an early overrepresented correct mode
can become a self-reinforcing and eventually locally frozen solution. In this
categorical toy model, the binary-reward dynamics amplify sampling-frequency
differences between correct modes. Recent analyses reach related
conclusions through outcome-frequency amplification, divergence choice, or
set-level policy geometry
\citep{sinha2026expected,li2026distribution,li2026setpo}.

Appendix~\ref{app:theory} makes this precise. Theorem~\ref{thm:grpo-collapse}
isolates the winner-take-all mechanism under infinitesimal on-policy mean
dynamics. The positive result, Theorem~\ref{thm:replay-retention} with
Corollary~\ref{cor:replay-no-collapse}, is deliberately post-discovery. Under
the theorem's fixed-bank categorical assumptions, recurrent verified replay
prevents banked modes from vanishing; with full verified coverage, the flow
converges to the uniform correct-mode distribution. It does not prove discovery
or global convergence in a nonconvex language model.

\subsection{Benchmark}

A benchmark domain is mode-verifiable only when $V$ satisfies two
requirements: (1) it executes the object the model actually generated and
judges that execution, (2) the canonical key is derived from the same
execution that established correctness. Additionally, cosmetic aliases merge; different executed outcomes stay distinct.
This restricts diversity credit to verified responses and confines breadth to
solutions the validator has distinguished.


Each domain trains on 384 prompts and evaluates on a fixed 128-prompt split,
and the mean number of correct execution modes per evaluation prompt---exact
over the released multi-answer split---is $6.27$ for Graph coloring, $4.52$ for
Countdown, $229.44$ for Python factors, $5.00$ for MathIR, and $18.19$ for
PantryPlan; each PointMaze map exposes three to five separated corridors.
Table~\ref{tab:tasks} gives each domain's generated object,
executed key, and merged aliases; the exact prompt each policy receives is
reproduced verbatim in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\section{x-Mode GRPO: Verified Replay for Dr.GRPO}
\label{sec:method}

\xmode{} is Dr.GRPO plus one persistent, validator-grounded rehearsal channel:
verified replay. The learner stores the policy's own successful outputs after
execution, then revisits them after their collection batch. This replay
likelihood is the only auxiliary derivative. An output may enter replay only
after the same
executable validator used for task reward has accepted it and produced its
canonical mode key.

Algorithm~\ref{alg:verified-replay-update} in Appendix~\ref{app:algorithm}
specifies the exact update.

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/verified_replay_mechanism.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{x-Mode GRPO.} One executable validator
  supplies both task reward and canonical mode identity (A); the first accepted
  output for each observed key enters a persistent prompt-local bank (B); a
  deterministic recurrent schedule revisits one bank per optimizer update (C);
  and uniform teacher-forced likelihood keeps a gradient on every banked mode,
  including modes absent from the current on-policy group (D). The
  compute-matched control runs the same maintenance, scheduling, and
  teacher-forced traversal with zero applied replay gradient.}
  \label{fig:verified-replay-mechanism}
\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{Verified exemplar bank.}
For each prompt $x$, the learner maintains a capacity-$M$ bank
$\mathcal B_x$, initially empty. A response enters only when its rollout reward
is positive and executable canonical validation returns a key
$c\ne\bot$. The first policy-generated token sequence for each observed key is
retained, up to $M=16$ keys. Admission never reads exhaustive support,
evaluation behavior, a desired mode count, or a reference solution.

\noindent\textbf{Uniform verified likelihood.}\label{sec:replay}
After the first nonempty bank exists, a recurrent replay schedule selects
exactly one prompt-local bank per optimizer update. The implementation uses a
fixed round-robin cursor so every nonempty bank is revisited. For every
retained exemplar $e_c$ in the scheduled bank, the current model computes the
length-normalized teacher-forced score
\[
  s_\theta(e_c\mid x)
  =|e_c|^{-1}\log\pi_\theta(e_c\mid x).
\]
The replay loss is
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{replay}}(x)
  =-\frac{1}{|\mathcal B_x|}
    \sum_{c\in\mathcal B_x}s_\theta(e_c\mid x).
  \label{eq:lm-verified-replay}
\end{equation}
Singleton banks are eligible: replay begins as soon as one verified exemplar
exists, and each newly observed mode adds one equally weighted exemplar. The
categorical reduction in Theorem~\ref{thm:replay-retention} shows that the
corresponding exact category loss simultaneously creates a barrier against
banked-mode extinction and, after full coverage, targets a uniform
correct-mode distribution.

The actor minimizes
\[
  L_{\mathrm{PPO}}(A^{\mathrm{task}})
  +\lambda_{\mathrm{replay}}w_{\mathrm{rep}}L_{\mathrm{replay}},
\]
where $\lambda_{\mathrm{replay}}=.10$ is a fixed implementation dose and
$w_{\mathrm{rep}}=(G-1)/G^2$ is the fixed row normalization for one replay
pseudo-group relative to $G$ on-policy rows. Neither coefficient adapts during
training. The theory requires only a positive recurrent effective dose; it does
not privilege $.10$. The matched Dr.GRPO control maintains the same passive bank,
scheduler, replay batch, score traversal, and backward-compute envelope but
sets the applied replay derivative to exact zero.

\subsection{Semantic MaxEnt: an entropy gradient on verified outcomes}
\label{sec:semantic-maxent}

Verified replay retains what the policy has already found. It supplies no
pressure toward outcomes never produced, because a mode absent from the bank
contributes no term to Equation~\ref{eq:lm-verified-replay}. Semantic MaxEnt
supplies exactly that pressure, and it is derivable rather than heuristic.

Fix a prompt $x$ and let $p_\theta$ be the policy-induced distribution over
canonical verified outcomes. The map from parameters to outcomes runs sample
$\to$ execute $\to$ validate $\to$ canonicalize and is not differentiable, so
its Shannon entropy $H(p_\theta)$ admits no pathwise gradient. The
log-derivative identity supplies one anyway:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\theta H(p_\theta)
  =\E_{a\sim p_\theta}\!\big[(-\log p_\theta(a))\,\nabla_\theta\log p_\theta(a)\big],
  \label{eq:entropy-score-function}
\end{equation}
the $-1$ ordinarily accompanying $-\log p$ vanishing because
$\sum_a\nabla_\theta p_\theta(a)=0$. Entropy ascent \emph{is} surprisal-weighted
policy gradient: no separate entropy objective is needed, only a per-rollout
weight. Writing the surrogate with a \emph{detached} plug-in predictor $\hat p$
gives $\nabla_\theta S=-\sum_a(\nabla_\theta p_\theta(a))\log\hat p(a)$, which
coincides with $\nabla_\theta H$ exactly when $\hat p=p_\theta$; detaching is
what makes the identity hold, not a convenience.

We estimate $\hat p$ from the prompt's own verified history with an open-set
Laplace predictor carrying one reserved \textsc{unseen} bucket, so an outcome
never produced still carries mass and the gradient still points toward it
(Appendix~\ref{app:semantic-maxent}). A row is \emph{eligible} only if it is
active, parseable, and validator-positive; with surprisal
$s_i=-\log\hat p(a_i)$ clipped at $C=5$,
\begin{equation}
  A^{\mathrm{sem}}_i
  =\eta\;\frac{\min(s_i,C)-\E_{\hat p}[\min(s,C)]}{C},
  \qquad \eta=.10 ,
  \label{eq:semantic-advantage}
\end{equation}
added to the task advantage after that advantage's own centering. Every
ineligible row receives exact zero and never enters $\hat p$'s support, so an
all-incorrect group is an exact no-op and no wrong answer is rewarded for being
unusual. Centering under $\hat p$ makes $\E_{\hat p}[A^{\mathrm{sem}}]=0$, and
$|A^{\mathrm{sem}}|\le\eta$, so with the unit reward gap a verified response
keeps at least a $.90$ advantage margin over an incorrect one and the
correctness ordering cannot invert.

The two components are complementary by construction:
Equation~\ref{eq:semantic-advantage} reaches outcomes never produced but has no
memory, while Equation~\ref{eq:lm-verified-replay} has memory but no reach. We
measure them separately in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\noindent\textbf{Inference-time compute and verifier-based reasoning RL.}
Self-consistency, repeated sampling, and verifier-guided search draw a
candidate set and select from it
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency,brown2024monkeys,snell2024testtime,
lightman2023lets}; GRPO and Dr.GRPO train the proposal policy itself from
group-relative outcome rewards
\citep{shao2024deepseekmath,guo2025deepseekr1,liu2025understanding}. Dr.GRPO
fixes length normalization; our question is orthogonal and upstream: whether
RL leaves enough probability \emph{within} equally rewarded correct outcomes
for additional inference to remain useful.

\noindent\textbf{Collapse and diversity-aware optimization.}
RLHF and fine-tuning can trade generalization or \texttt{pass@}$K$ for
narrower outputs \citep{kirk2024understanding,dang2025diversity}, and RLVR
can lose token entropy and narrow the large-$k$ sampling boundary relative to
the base model \citep{cui2025entropy,yue2025rlvrlimit}. Proposed repairs
include outcome bonuses, batch penalties, pass@$K$ objectives, mass-covering
divergences, set-level and distribution-matching objectives, and token-level
clipping asymmetries
\citep{song2025outcome,divpo2025,chen2025passktraining,li2025divergence,
li2026setpo,li2026distribution,sinha2026expected,yu2025dapo}. Closest is
\citet{song2025outcome}, whose 7B competition-math study is stronger external
evidence for the general problem; their UCB counts final answers---including
incorrect ones---and their bonuses are disabled on all-correct batches,
whereas verified replay admits only validator-positive \emph{executed} keys and
replays a verified success beyond its collection batch.

\noindent\textbf{Experience replay, archives, and mode-covering objectives.}
Standard replay buffers retain past transitions so later updates can reuse
experience \citep{lin1992selfimproving,mnih2015human}; replay also supports
retention in continual learning \citep{rolnick2019replay}. Our unit of stored
experience is instead a completed successful sequence, indexed by its verified
execution mode. Replaying it applies supervised sequence likelihood rather
than an off-policy Dr.GRPO task update.

GFlowNets sample in proportion to reward and have fine-tuned LMs toward
diverse reasoning chains \citep{bengio2023gflownet,hu2024amortizing,
zhu2025flowrl}; quality-diversity search maintains an archive indexed by a
hand-designed behavior descriptor \citep{lehman2011novelty,
mouret2015illuminating}; maximum-entropy RL adds entropy regularization
over tokens or a known action set \citep{ziebart2008maximum,
levine2018control,haarnoja18b,haarnoja2018algorithms}; and semantic entropy
clusters generations by estimated entailment \citep{farquhar2024detecting}.
Verified replay instead indexes a persistent bank with the validator's
canonical execution key and applies one uniform exemplar-likelihood objective
to each scheduled bank. Because the validator computes the key from execution
rather than reading a model-asserted label, the equivalence relation is
validator-defined; it does not claim that every reasoning strategy is
observable.


\section{Experimental Design and Evidence Policy}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Matched training and evaluation}

The clean \xmode{} comparison uses \qwenmark{}2.5-0.5B-Instruct at a pinned
revision, seeds 43--47, group size 16, $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}=0$, rollout
temperature 1, and exactly eight passes through each fixed pool. Each static
domain trains on 384 prompts and evaluates on a fixed 128-prompt split, giving
3,072 optimizer updates per run. Evaluation and resumable checkpoints occur
every 192 updates, the fixed half-pass grid $0,.5,\ldots,8$. The original 50
cells are five domains $\times$ two arms $\times$ five paired seeds.

\noindent\textbf{Aligned scale extensions.} The same two-arm, eight-pass,
384/128, group-16 design is running on Falcon3-1B-Instruct with seeds 55--59
and on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with seeds 70--74. The replay mechanism, capacity,
fixed $.10$ weight, half-pass evaluation grid, and exact-zero derivative
control are unchanged. Optimizer recipes are model-specific but fixed before
any replay-arm outcome: Falcon uses a constant $2\times10^{-7}$ learning rate,
whereas Qwen2.5-3B uses a $10^{-7}$ cosine schedule with 10\% warmup and a
10\% minimum-rate floor. Every comparison is within model, domain, and seed;
absolute differences between model families are not treatment effects. These
scale cohorts are shown to expose progress, not pooled with the 0.5B cohort.

Within each pair, the control and treatment share data, model, optimizer,
rollouts, passive verified bank, deterministic recurrent scheduler,
teacher-forced replay traversal, checkpoint cadence, GPU model, and recovery
rules. The sole scientific difference is the applied replay derivative:
exact zero for matched Dr.GRPO and live uniform verified likelihood for
\xmode{}. No other auxiliary derivative is active. The replay weight
$.10$ is a frozen implementation dose, not a selected checkpoint or
theoretical constant.

\noindent\textbf{Compute matching.} Both arms perform identical rollout
generation, verified-bank maintenance, recurrent scheduling, teacher-forced
exemplar scoring, and backward traversal. The control sets the resulting
replay derivative to exact zero; the treatment applies it. The comparison
therefore changes the learning signal without changing its compute envelope.
Relative to ordinary Dr.GRPO without this matched traversal, verified replay
adds teacher-forced scoring of at most 16 previously generated exemplars per
update. Terminal reporting will include measured throughput rather than infer
the overhead from the loss coefficient.

\noindent\textbf{PointMaze stratum.} Qwen2.5-0.5B uses the same paired
eight-pass protocol on 384 training and 128 evaluation maps, with seeds 43--47
and a shared frozen 72-update warm start learned on disjoint maps. A replay
exemplar is one successful route: treatment applies its mean selected-action
log likelihood, while control performs the same traversal with an exact-zero
derivative. All other auxiliary signals are off, so this remains the same
Dr.GRPO-versus-\xmode{} test but a separate estimator from the static domains.
The parallel Falcon3-1B stratum uses seeds 55--59 and a shared Falcon warm
start; four control cells, but no replay cell, had registered evaluation in
the dated snapshot. No Qwen2.5-3B PointMaze stratum is scheduled.

\subsection{Evidence policy}

Each environment is analyzed separately. The primary estimands are the paired
seed differences, \xmode{} minus matched Dr.GRPO, at pass 8 for
\texttt{distinct@8} and \texttt{pass@8}. The secondary trajectory estimand is
trapezoidal AUC over the complete half-pass grid. The final report will include
all five paired seed differences and their mean and range. We do not pool
environments, model families, or interface strata, and never select a best
checkpoint. For the five static domains, descriptive \texttt{pass@1} uses
greedy decoding; sampled \texttt{mean@8} and excess multiplicity are also
reported descriptively.

PointMaze uses the same two primary estimands and trajectory summary but has no
greedy full-program endpoint: its actions are sequential and
evaluated under a common-random-number interactive protocol. The
five-domain 0.5B cohort becomes final only after all 50 pass-8 endpoints pass
the frozen audit; each scale cohort and the PointMaze cohort becomes final only
after every registered cell in it passes its prospective audit.
Until then, the dated values are explicitly interim. The evidence ledger,
state-integrity rules, exclusions, dataset auditing, and cohort provenance are
bound in Appendices~\ref{app:reproducibility} and~\ref{app:data}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}

\noindent\textbf{Two distinctions organize the design.} \emph{Identity}:
accepted execution defines a mode. \emph{Retention versus discovery}: replay
protects an observed, banked mode but does not guarantee that an unobserved
mode will be found.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} The registered program covers five synthetic
static-response domains, one synthetic-map MuJoCo PointMaze environment, and
three model scales or families, with five paired seeds planned per stratum.
Exact mode identity is unusually available. PointMaze delegates low-level force
control to a fixed PD adapter, so it tests language-level waypoint choice
rather than end-to-end motor learning. Capacity-16 banks can omit later
discoveries, the fixed $.10$ replay dose is neither ablated nor claimed
optimal, and the same holds for the $.10$ semantic coefficient. The theoretical guarantee assumes a
recurrently replayed fixed bank in a categorical model: a conditional retention
result, not a discovery guarantee or a convergence proof for a language model.

\noindent\textbf{What it adds up to.} \mb{} provides execution-grounded
measurement of correct-mode collapse across static and interactive domains,
and the categorical analysis gives \xmode{} a conditional
post-discovery retention guarantee through verified replay. Every currently
paired interim breadth panel points toward \xmode{}, and adding an entropy
gradient on verified outcomes adds to it in most panels. These interim point
estimates support no neural performance claim until each registered pass-8
stratum is complete and audited.

\subsubsection*{Ethics Statement}

The benchmark and experiment-specific data are synthetic; this study collects
no human-subject or personal data. More verified modes are not inherently
better: a distinct execution may be risky or costly even when it passes the
validator, and \texttt{distinct@}$K$ does not measure downstream value. Systems
that evaluate or optimize breadth should report correctness, invalidity, and
application-specific costs alongside mode counts.

\subsubsection*{Reproducibility Statement}

Every quantitative claim is bound to a frozen artifact. The clean comparison
pairs each domain and seed within GPU model; physical device identity need not
match. Because GPU model can change sampled tokens, the audit rejects a pair
whose GPU models differ.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} give the
matched protocol, the complete hyperparameter set, and the evidence ledger that
fixes what each comparison may claim; Appendix~\ref{app:data} records SHA-256
identities for all training and evaluation splits. Comparison protocols were
registered before results were observed and are included as
\path{paper/preregistration/*.md}. Bank, scheduler, optimizer,
data-cursor, and request-stream state are checkpointed atomically, and
integrity violations---non-finite values, state mismatch, missing terminal
markers, or proposal leakage into PPO---fail closed rather than degrade
silently. Terminal figures are regenerated from hash-bound artifacts by named
scripts listed in Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}, and rendering aborts if a
displayed endpoint does not reproduce its frozen aggregate. The interim
Figure~\ref{fig:interim-replay} is deliberately frozen together with its full
JSON provenance rather than silently advancing on each build. Two superseded
cohorts are excluded by machine-readable audits written before their outcomes
were known.

\section{Why GRPO Collapses and x-Mode GRPO Retains Modes}
\label{app:theory}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix supplies the formal claims
invoked in Sections~\ref{sec:method} and~\ref{sec:results}.

This appendix separates two questions that are easy to conflate: whether a
binary-reward group update preserves ratios among already correct modes, and
whether \xmode{} preserves modes that the policy has already produced
and the validator has accepted. The first admits an unconditional answer in a
finite categorical model. The second necessarily requires a coverage
assumption: no method whose bank contains only executed, validator-positive
objects can protect a mode that is never sampled or constructed. We therefore
prove support preservation first and state full no-collapse as a coverage
corollary. Accordingly, the positive theorem explains the stability mechanism
after discovery; it does not prove that \xmode{} will establish its own
coverage premise in a language model.

\subsection{Categorical reduction and the GRPO mean update}

Fix one prompt. Let the finite response categories be
$\mathcal A=\mathcal C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal N$, where the $m\ge2$
categories in $\mathcal C$ are correct execution modes and the $n\ge1$
categories in $\mathcal N$ are incorrect. Let
$p_a=\operatorname{softmax}(z)_a$, $P=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}p_c$, and
$q_c=p_c/P$. Thus $q$ is exactly the conditional correct-mode distribution
$p_\theta^+(\cdot\mid x)$ of Section~\ref{sec:collapse}, and $P$ is the correct
mass $P_\theta^+(x)$.

Draw a group $A_1,\ldots,A_G\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}p$ with $G\ge2$,
write $R_i=\1[A_i\in\mathcal C]$, and let $M=\sum_iR_i$. Both GRPO variants
can be written, at the on-policy point where the PPO ratio equals one, as
\begin{equation}
  \widehat g_G
  =\frac1G\sum_{i=1}^G
   w(M)\left(R_i-\frac{M}{G}\right)\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}.
  \label{eq:group-estimator}
\end{equation}
For Dr.GRPO, $w(M)=1$ after absorbing the shared positive length scale. For
binary-reward GRPO, $w(M)$ is the reciprocal group reward standard deviation
on mixed groups. In either case $w(\ell)>0$ for
$\ell\in\{1,\ldots,G-1\}$; its value on all-equal groups is irrelevant because
the centered advantage is zero. We take explicit reference KL to be zero, as
in the experiments.

\begin{lemma}[A group baseline does not remove frequency amplification]
\label{lem:grpo-mean}
For $0<P<1$, the expected group update is
\begin{equation}
  \E[\widehat g_G]
  =c_G(P)\nabla_z P,
  \qquad
  c_G(P)=
  \frac{\E\!\big[w(M)M(G-M)\big]}{G^2P(1-P)}>0.
  \label{eq:expected-group-gradient}
\end{equation}
For Dr.GRPO, $c_G(P)=(G-1)/G$. Hence reward centering and binary reward
standardization change the speed, but not the expected direction, of the
binary-reward policy gradient.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the complete binary reward vector, whose sum is $M$. The score
identities give
\[
  \E[\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}\mid R_i=1]=\nabla_z\log P,
  \qquad
  \E[\nabla_z\log p_{A_i}\mid R_i=0]
  =\nabla_z\log(1-P).
\]
There are $M$ correct rows with centered reward $(G-M)/G$ and $G-M$
incorrect rows with centered reward $-M/G$. Therefore the conditional
expectation of the unaveraged sum in~\eqref{eq:group-estimator} is
\[
  w(M)\frac{M(G-M)}{G}
  \left(\nabla_z\log P-\nabla_z\log(1-P)\right)
  =w(M)\frac{M(G-M)}{GP(1-P)}\nabla_zP.
\]
The outer factor $1/G$ and expectation over
$M\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(G,P)$ yield~\eqref{eq:expected-group-gradient}.
For $w\equiv1$,
$\E[M(G-M)]=G(G-1)P(1-P)$, proving the Dr.GRPO value.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Winner-take-all collapse under GRPO and Dr.GRPO}

Consider the infinitesimal expected-update flow
$\dot z=\E[\widehat g_G]=c_G(P)\nabla_zP$. This is the clean mean-field limit
of fresh on-policy groups and the first-order PPO update. It deliberately
removes sampling noise; the result below therefore identifies a structural
instability rather than blaming finite batches.

\begin{theorem}[Generic binary-reward GRPO mode collapse]
\label{thm:grpo-collapse}
Start from finite logits with $0<P(0)<1$. If the conditional correct-mode
distribution $q(0)$ has a unique largest coordinate $c^\star$, then
\[
  P(t)\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  q_{c^\star}(t)\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad
  q_c(t)\longrightarrow0\quad(c\ne c^\star).
\]
Thus both GRPO and Dr.GRPO converge to a single correct execution mode for
generic initial conditions, even though every mode in $\mathcal C$ has exactly
the same reward.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For a correct mode $c$ and incorrect category $a$,
\[
  \dot z_c=c_G(P)p_c(1-P),
  \qquad
  \dot z_a=-c_G(P)p_aP.
\]
Also $\dot P=c_G(P)\lVert\nabla_zP\rVert_2^2>0$. If $P$ converged to an
interior value, continuity and positivity of $c_G$, together with
\[
 \lVert\nabla_zP\rVert_2^2
 \ge P^2(1-P)^2\left(\frac1m+\frac1n\right),
\]
would keep $\dot P$ bounded away from zero, a contradiction. Hence $P\to1$.

Define the effective optimization time
\[
  \tau(t)=\int_0^t c_G(P(s))P(s)(1-P(s))\,ds.
\]
If $\tau(\infty)<\infty$, every correct and incorrect logit would have finite
total variation, because the absolute velocity of each is at most $\dot\tau$.
All logits would then converge to finite values, contradicting $P\to1$.
Therefore $\tau(t)\to\infty$.

On the correct simplex, changing from $t$ to $\tau$ gives the replicator flow
\begin{equation}
  \frac{dq_c}{d\tau}
  =q_c\left(q_c-\sum_{d\in\mathcal C}q_d^2\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{d}{d\tau}\log\frac{q_c}{q_d}=q_c-q_d.
  \label{eq:grpo-replicator}
\end{equation}
The initially unique maximum remains the unique maximum, and every one of its
log-ratios increases. The Lyapunov quantity $\sum_cq_c^2$ is nondecreasing,
since its derivative is twice the variance of the coordinate values under
$q$. Every limit point of~\eqref{eq:grpo-replicator} is therefore uniform on
its positive support. Because $q_{c^\star}\ge1/m$ and its ratio to every other
coordinate stays strictly greater than one, no multi-coordinate uniform limit
is possible. The only limit point is the vertex $q_{c^\star}=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Ties are nongeneric]
\label{cor:grpo-ties}
If exactly $k<m$ correct modes tie for the largest initial probability, symmetry
preserves the tie and the flow converges to the uniform distribution on those
$k$ modes while eliminating the rest. Only the exactly uniform initialization
over all $m$ correct modes avoids collapse. Any arbitrarily small perturbation
that creates a unique maximum reduces to Theorem~\ref{thm:grpo-collapse}.
\end{corollary}

Finite groups make this instability operationally harsher: a mode absent from
a group has no score carrier, all-correct groups have zero task advantage, and
sampling noise almost surely breaks exact ties. These facts are not needed for
the theorem.

\subsection{Why uniform verified replay prevents extinction}

Let $\mathcal B\subseteq\mathcal C$ be a nonempty fixed bank of $k$ modes that
the policy has produced and the validator has accepted. Uniform verified replay
uses one exemplar per banked mode and, in the categorical reduction, minimizes
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathcal B}(z)
  =-\frac1k\sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\log p_b.
  \label{eq:verified-replay-loss}
\end{equation}
This is ordinary cross-entropy against the distribution that assigns mass
$1/k$ to each banked mode and zero elsewhere. It is not conditioned on the
bank: decreasing~\eqref{eq:verified-replay-loss} can raise total bank mass as
well as redistribute mass within the bank.

Absorb a fixed round-robin replay frequency and loss scale into an effective
dose $\rho>0$. The prompt-isolated categorical mean flow for binary-reward GRPO
plus verified replay is
\begin{equation}
  \dot z
  =c_G(P)\nabla_zP-\rho\nabla_zR_{\mathcal B}(z).
  \label{eq:replay-mean-flow}
\end{equation}
Let $\Psi(P)=\int_0^P c_G(s)\,ds$. For the finite-group estimators in
Lemma~\ref{lem:grpo-mean}, $c_G$ has a finite positive extension to $[0,1]$;
in particular, Dr.GRPO has $c_G=(G-1)/G$. Define
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathcal B}(z)=\rho R_{\mathcal B}(z)-\Psi(P(z)).
  \label{eq:replay-potential}
\end{equation}
Then~\eqref{eq:replay-mean-flow} is exactly gradient flow
$\dot z=-\nabla_zF_{\mathcal B}$.

\begin{theorem}[Verified replay prevents banked-mode extinction]
\label{thm:replay-retention}
Suppose that after time $T$ the nonempty verified bank $\mathcal B$ is fixed,
fits within replay capacity, and receives a fixed positive effective replay
dose $\rho$. Under~\eqref{eq:replay-mean-flow}, define
\[
  C_T
  =R_{\mathcal B}(z(T))
   +\frac{\Psi(1)-\Psi(P(T))}{\rho}.
\]
Then for every $b\in\mathcal B$ and every $t\ge T$,
\begin{equation}
  p_b(t)\ge \exp(-kC_T)>0.
  \label{eq:replay-retention-bound}
\end{equation}
Thus no verified mode in a recurrently replayed bank can vanish, even when the
bank does not cover every correct mode.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Along the flow,
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}F_{\mathcal B}(z(t))
  =-\lVert\nabla_zF_{\mathcal B}(z(t))\rVert_2^2\le0.
\]
Since $P(t)\le1$ and $\Psi$ is increasing,
\[
  R_{\mathcal B}(z(t))
  \le R_{\mathcal B}(z(T))
     +\frac{\Psi(1)-\Psi(P(T))}{\rho}
  =C_T.
\]
Every $-\log p_b$ is nonnegative and their sum is
$kR_{\mathcal B}$. Therefore
$-\log p_b(t)\le kC_T$ for every banked mode, which is exactly
Equation~\eqref{eq:replay-retention-bound}.
\end{proof}

The bound can be conservative when $\rho$ is small, but its qualitative content
is sharp: any positive recurrent replay dose creates a log barrier against
extinction. The numerical dose controls finite-time strength, not which
distribution the categorical objective targets.

\begin{corollary}[x-Mode GRPO no-collapse guarantee after verified coverage]
\label{cor:replay-no-collapse}
Assume the conditions of Theorem~\ref{thm:replay-retention} and that by time
$T$ the bank covers the full correct support, $\mathcal B=\mathcal C$. Let
$u_c=1/m$ on $\mathcal C$. Then the categorical mean flow converges to
\[
  P(t)\longrightarrow1,
  \qquad q(t)\longrightarrow u.
\]
Consequently
\[
  \min_{c\in\mathcal C}q_c(t)\longrightarrow\frac1m,
  \qquad
  \mathcal H(q(t))\longrightarrow\log m,
\]
and conditional correct-mode collapse is impossible.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
When $\mathcal B=\mathcal C$,
\begin{equation}
  R_{\mathcal C}
  =\log m-\log P+\mathrm{KL}(u\|q).
  \label{eq:replay-mass-uniformity-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Thus one uniform verified-likelihood loss already contains both a verified-mass
term $-\log P$ and a within-support uniformity term $\mathrm{KL}(u\|q)$. The
potential~\eqref{eq:replay-potential} becomes
\[
  F_{\mathcal C}
  =\rho\log m+\rho\mathrm{KL}(u\|q)
   -\rho\log P-\Psi(P).
\]
For fixed $P$, its unique minimizer is $q=u$. Its derivative in $P$ is
$-\rho/P-c_G(P)<0$, so its unique limiting distribution is $P=1,q=u$.

For completeness, Theorem~\ref{thm:replay-retention} keeps every correct
coordinate away from zero. The decreasing potential gives
$\int_T^\infty\lVert\nabla F_{\mathcal C}\rVert_2^2dt<\infty$; bounded
softmax derivatives imply $\nabla F_{\mathcal C}\to0$. At any limiting
distribution the correct-coordinate stationarity equations are
\[
  0=\rho(p_c-1/m)-c_G(P)p_c(1-P),
\]
so all $p_c$ are equal. Substituting $p_c=P/m$ gives
$0=-(1-P)(\rho+c_G(P)P)/m$, hence $P=1$. Each incorrect coordinate satisfies
$0=p_a(\rho+c_G(P)P)$ and therefore has $p_a=0$. The limiting distribution is
unique, proving convergence.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Exact scope of the x-Mode GRPO guarantee]
The theorem is a conditional post-discovery retention result, not an end-to-end
discovery theorem. Without full coverage it protects every mode in
$\mathcal B$ but says nothing about unobserved modes in
$\mathcal C\setminus\mathcal B$; capacity 16 can also truncate the protected
support. The categorical proof treats each stored exemplar as its execution
category and uses $\log p_b$, whereas the language-model implementation uses a
length-normalized teacher-forced exemplar score. Cross-prompt parameter
interference, stochastic finite steps, and nonconvex representation are outside
the mean-flow result and must be tested empirically. These limitations motivate
the matched five-domain \xmode{} comparison rather than a stronger neural
network claim.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A fixed coefficient does not deliver a fixed dose}
\label{app:realized-dose}

Section~\ref{sec:semantic-maxent} fixes $\eta=.10$. The \emph{realized}
pressure, measured as the ratio of applied semantic-advantage RMS to task
advantage RMS over all 3{,}072 updates of seed 43 in the semantic-MaxEnt arm, is not fixed at all:
Countdown 3.71\%, Graph coloring 1.64\%, Python factors 1.88\%, MathIR 1.02\%.

Equation~\ref{eq:semantic-advantage} depends on how many verified modes the
prompt's bank happens to hold. A coefficient is therefore not a dose.

A registered follow-up adapts $\eta$ per run to hold this ratio at a fixed
target. Its first cohort establishes a constraint worth recording: with $\eta$
bounded above by $.40$ --- the largest value that still leaves a verified
response a $.60$ advantage margin under a unit reward gap, and so the largest
that preserves the correctness ordering of
Appendix~\ref{app:semantic-maxent} --- a $5\%$ target is unreachable in MathIR
and Python factors, whose controllers sat at that ceiling for 97--98\% of
updates while realizing only $2.8\%$ and $1.7\%$. Equalizing semantic pressure
across domains is bounded by the safety constraint, not by the controller.

A second cohort re-derives the target from what those saturated cells sustained
rather than from the bound: the lowest ratio any ceiling-pinned cell held was
$.0170$, so $\rho=.015$ is registered with $\eta$'s bound and every other
setting unchanged. Its mechanism gate --- read before any outcome, as
registered --- is met on the four domains so far in progress. Realized ratio is
$.0131$--$.0164$ against the $.0150$ target, inside $13\%$ where the registered
criterion allowed a factor of two; $\eta$ sits at a bound on $0$--$5.8\%$ of
applied updates against a $20\%$ allowance; no domain is frozen above $57\%$
against an $80\%$ threshold; and mean response length is within $5.4\%$ of the
fixed arm with no-EOS at zero in every cell. The coefficients that hold one
common ratio span $.033$--$.144$, which is this section's own point from the
other side: equal realized pressure requires unequal coefficients. Python
factors, whose $.0170$ set the target, has not yet started, so reachability is
established on four domains of five, and the arm's registered outcome --- its
paired difference against the fixed arm at pass 8 --- is not yet readable.

\section{Semantic MaxEnt: Estimator and Properties}
\label{app:semantic-maxent}

Section~\ref{sec:semantic-maxent} states the entropy-gradient identity and the
applied advantage. This appendix gives the predictor it is evaluated under and
the properties that follow.

\noindent\textbf{Open-set prompt-local predictor.} For prompt $x$, let the
explicit support be the union of previously verified canonical outcomes and the
leave-one-out verified peers of the current group, with $K$ distinct keys whose
counts sum to $N$. With Laplace pseudocount $\alpha=1$ and one reserved
\textsc{unseen} bucket,
\begin{equation}
  \hat p(a)=\frac{n(a)+\alpha}{N+\alpha(K+1)},
  \qquad
  \hat p(\textsc{unseen})=\frac{\alpha}{N+\alpha(K+1)} .
  \label{eq:open-set-predictor}
\end{equation}
The reserved bucket is what makes the estimator open-set. A closed-set
estimator over $K$ observed keys assigns zero mass to everything unobserved,
its surprisal diverges, and the gradient toward genuinely new outcomes is
undefined. Equation~\ref{eq:open-set-predictor} keeps that mass finite and
positive, so discovery pressure is well defined before a second mode exists.

\noindent\textbf{Exact centering.} The expectation in
Equation~\ref{eq:semantic-advantage} is taken under the same detached $\hat p$
that scores the row, so $\E_{\hat p}[A^{\mathrm{sem}}]=0$ identically rather
than approximately. The term therefore redistributes advantage among verified
rows without shifting the objective level, and adds no net drift to the update.

\noindent\textbf{Bounded pressure.} The centered clipped surprisal lies in
$[-1,1]$, hence $|A^{\mathrm{sem}}|\le\eta$. Every task in this paper uses a
unit correct/incorrect reward gap, so at $\eta=.10$ a verified response retains
at least a $.90$ advantage margin over an incorrect one. Semantic MaxEnt can
reorder verified outcomes among themselves; it cannot make an incorrect output
preferred.

\noindent\textbf{The clip is part of the scale.} Replacing $s$ by
$\min(s,C)$ makes this a truncated-entropy surrogate rather than exact entropy
ascent, and $C$ and $\eta$ act jointly: at $C=5$ an outcome never seen after
$200$ prior verified rows saturates the clip and earns nearly the full $\eta$,
while at $C=50$ the same event earns roughly a tenth of that. Reported
coefficients are therefore meaningful only alongside their clip.

\noindent\textbf{Eligibility.} Only active, parseable, validator-positive rows
are eligible, and ineligible rows never enter $\hat p$'s support. This is what
prevents the term from rewarding diverse failure: novelty among wrong answers
is invisible to the estimator. It also means the term is exactly inert on any
task whose canonical outcome key the estimator cannot parse, which is a
property to verify per domain rather than assume.

\section{External Python Verification Contract}
\label{app:python}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix expands the Python Factors
contract in Section~\ref{sec:modebench} and its limitation in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

A Python response is parsed in expression mode and must be a lambda with one
argument named $n$. Accepted nodes are integer constants, $n$, bounded
arithmetic, comparisons, Boolean operators, unary operators, and conditional
expressions. Calls, imports, attributes, comprehensions, containers,
subscripts, assignment, loops, and every other identifier are rejected before
execution. Candidate length and AST size are bounded.

The parent sends restricted source and trusted JSON task data to a persistent
external interpreter over JSON lines. The worker reparses, removes builtins,
evaluates the lambda, and invokes it on all frozen inputs under a per-request
timer. It returns only validity, the integer vector, and its key. Timeout,
broken pipe, malformed output, non-integer values, or divisor failure returns
no key. The parent can restart a failed worker.

For inputs $[6,10,15]$,
\[
\texttt{lambda n: 2 if n \% 2 == 0 else 3}
\]
returns $(2,2,3)$, while
\[
\texttt{lambda n: 3 if n \% 3 == 0 else 5}
\]
returns $(3,5,3)$. Both verify and receive different behavior keys.

\section{Algorithmic Details}
\label{app:algorithm}

\noindent\emph{Main-body link.} This appendix formalizes the state transition
summarized by Figure~\ref{fig:verified-replay-mechanism} and
Section~\ref{sec:method}.
For each prompt $x$, let $\mathcal B_x$ be the capacity-$M$ verified-exemplar
bank and let $\tau$ be the recurrent replay cursor.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{One verified-replay Dr.GRPO optimizer update}
  \label{alg:verified-replay-update}
  \small
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Policy $\pi_\theta$; prompt $x$ and verifier specification
      $s_x$; group size $G$; replay weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{replay}}$;
      states $(\mathcal B,\tau)$
    \ENSURE Atomically updated $(\theta',\mathcal B',\tau')$
    \STATE Draw the on-policy group
      $\mathcal G=\{y_i\sim\pi_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\}_{i=1}^G$
    \FOR{$i=1,\ldots,G$}
      \STATE Execute $\widetilde c_i\gets V(y_i,s_x)$ and observe task
        reward $r_i$
      \STATE Set $c_i\gets\widetilde c_i$ iff
        $r_i>0\wedge\widetilde c_i\ne\bot$; otherwise set $c_i\gets\bot$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Compute centered Dr.GRPO task advantages
      $A_i^{\mathrm{task}}$ from $\{r_j\}_{j=1}^G$
    \STATE After the full group is validated, retain the first policy exemplar
      for each new $c_i\ne\bot$ in $\mathcal B_x$, up to capacity $M$
    \STATE $(z,\tau')\gets\textsc{NextBank}
      (\{\mathcal B_u\}_u,\tau)$ by a fixed round-robin cursor
    \IF{a replay bank $\mathcal B_z$ exists}
      \STATE For each retained exemplar $e_c$, compute
        $s_c=|e_c|^{-1}\log\pi_\theta(e_c\mid z)$
      \STATE $L_{\mathrm{replay}}\gets-|\mathcal B_z|^{-1}
        \sum_{c\in\mathcal B_z}s_c$
    \ELSE
      \STATE $L_{\mathrm{replay}}\gets0$
    \ENDIF
    \STATE $w_{\mathrm{rep}}\gets(G-1)/G^2$ for one replay
      pseudo-group per $G$ on-policy rows
    \STATE $\theta'\gets\textsc{Update}
      (\theta,L_{\mathrm{PPO}}(A^{\mathrm{task}})
      +w_{\mathrm{rep}}\lambda_{\mathrm{replay}}L_{\mathrm{replay}})$
    \STATE Checkpoint $(\theta',\mathcal B',\tau')$ as one atomic state
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The compute-matched control executes the same bank update, scheduling,
teacher-forced forward pass, and backward traversal, but multiplies the applied
replay derivative by exact zero. The treatment changes only that derivative
from zero to live.

\section{Decoding Control: Does Any Sampler Setting Repair the Collapse?}
\label{app:decoding}

\noindent\emph{Scope.} This appendix records a sampler control for a superseded
12-pass cohort. It is not evidence for \xmode{}.

Every headline cell samples at temperature one. A reader may reasonably ask
whether matched Dr.GRPO's missing modes are merely improbable rather than
absent: if a modestly hotter sampler recovered them, the finding would concern
sharpening at a fixed decoding setting, not loss of support. That is a different
scientific claim, so we test it directly.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol.} We re-measure all $50$ frozen terminal checkpoints
(five domains $\times$ two arms $\times$ seeds 43--47) at six sampling
temperatures $T\in\{0.5,0.7,1.0,1.3,1.6,2.0\}$, giving $300$ terminal-checkpoint
cells plus $60$ hardware-matched pre-RL controls;
at a four-fold larger sample budget, $K=32$, over $T\in\{1.0,1.3,1.6\}$, for a
further $150$ terminal-checkpoint cells plus $30$ pre-RL controls; and under
nucleus truncation, top-$p=0.95$, at $T\in\{1.0,1.6\}$, for a final $100$
terminal-checkpoint cells plus $20$ pre-RL controls. The three knobs a practitioner
actually turns are therefore each measured against the same frozen checkpoints,
$660$ cells in all.
Each cell loads one checkpoint and runs the \emph{same} evaluation path used
during training---the same validator, prompts, split, response budget, registered
$K$, and four fixed draw seeds---while performing no rollout, optimizer step, or
export, so a cell is a pure function of the checkpoint and its decoding setting.
Two controls make the sweep admissible. First, every cell at $T=1$ must
reproduce its run's published terminal value within
$\max(3\,\mathrm{SE},.02)$ for rates and $\max(3\,\mathrm{SE},.05)$ for mode
counts; all $240$ such checks pass, the great majority exactly. Second, each cell
runs on the GPU model that trained its checkpoint, because floating-point
reduction order differs between GPU models and changes which tokens are sampled
from identical weights under identical draw seeds. That effect is visible in the
frozen pass-0 rows, which are identical within each GPU group and differ between
them. It also bears on the headline cohort: twenty-two of the twenty-five paired
seeds ran both arms on one GPU model and three pairs are split, so the sweep
below pins placement where the headline design does not.

\noindent\textbf{Estimand.} For arm $a$ and domain $d$ define the
\emph{temperature-repair index}
\[
  \rho_{a,d}
  =\frac{\max_T\ \texttt{distinct@8}(a,d,T)}
        {\texttt{distinct@8}(\historicalt,d,1.0)}.
\]
The numerator maximizes over the whole sweep \emph{with no accuracy
constraint}, so the baseline may spend arbitrary correctness to buy breadth.
A value below one means that no decoding setting recovers the treatment's
temperature-one breadth.

The baseline does not convert budget into breadth in any domain: the largest

additional draws per prompt surface no verified execution the first eight did

the missing modes are not rare, they are absent, and no amount of sampling from
this policy will produce one. \Historicalt{} spends the same budget very differently,
nearly doubling its breadth on Graph coloring and more than doubling it on
PantryPlan, which is what a policy that has retained its support looks like
under a wider draw.

The hardware-matched pre-RL controls make the support loss literal in the two
domains where initialization already has broad verified support. At $K=32$, the
two hardware-specific references yield $1.590$--$1.723$ Graph-coloring modes and
$7.129$--$7.258$ PantryPlan plans. Terminal matched Dr.GRPO yields only $.327$
and $.638$, respectively, while \historicalt{} yields $4.238$ and $4.728$. Matched
Dr.GRPO therefore does not merely fail to discover alternatives: it removes
verified support already observable before RL. On PantryPlan, \historicalt{} mitigates
but does not fully restore the pre-RL breadth.

\noindent\textbf{Nor does truncating the tail.} The third knob cuts the other
way: nucleus sampling deletes low-probability mass, so if the baseline's missing
modes were merely improbable, truncation should visibly cost it breadth. At the
reported operating point it costs it nothing. Moving to top-$p=0.95$ changes
matched Dr.GRPO's \texttt{distinct@8} by $+.000$ on Graph coloring and Python
factors, $-.001$ on MathIR and PantryPlan, and $-.021$ on Countdown. \Historicalt{}, by
contrast, does lose breadth to the same operation---$-.126$ on PantryPlan and
$-.020$ on Graph coloring---because it has tail mass to lose. A policy whose
support has collapsed is indifferent to how the tail is cut, and that
indifference is itself the measurement.

The practitioner's usual recipe for diverse-but-valid sampling is to raise
temperature and truncate together. At $T=1.6$ with top-$p=0.95$ that recipe
recovers real breadth for the treatment, $+.472$ on Python factors and $+.256$
on Graph coloring over untruncated sampling at the same temperature, because
truncation removes the malformed text that heating produced. For the baseline
the same combination returns $+.000$ on Graph coloring, Python factors, and
MathIR. There is nothing in the tail to keep.

We therefore read the failure as loss of support rather than sharpening: the
absent modes are not waiting at low probability for a hotter sampler, a wider
one, or a narrower one. This control covers decoding of the trained policies
only. It says nothing about the alternative training objectives listed in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.

\section{Cross-Family Replication on a Second Base Model}
\label{app:cross-family}

\noindent\emph{Scope.} This appendix records a historical second-base-model
replication of a superseded multi-component method. It is not evidence for \xmode{}.

\noindent\textbf{Why.} Every headline cell is produced by one base model. A
reader may reasonably ask whether the collapse of matched Dr.GRPO's metric band,
and its reopening under \historicalt{}, is a property of the objective or of that one
model family's tokenizer and instruction tuning. The registered replication
answers that directly by re-running the whole five-domain design on
Falcon3-1B-Instruct at a pinned revision: an independent line in tokenizer,
pretraining corpus, and instruction tuning, and roughly three times the
parameter count. The parameter count is deliberately not matched, because no
comparison in the study is between the two base models; every comparison is
within a domain, between the two arms, at a matched seed.

\noindent\textbf{What is held fixed.} The five domains, both arms, seeds
43--47, 12 epochs over 384 training prompts with a fixed 128-prompt evaluation
split, group size 16, $\beta_{\mathrm{KL}}=0$, rollout temperature 1, the
evaluation protocol, the reporting rule, and every \historicalt{} coefficient at its
value in this paper. No coefficient is retuned for the new model.

\noindent\textbf{What necessarily changes.} Three things, each forced by the
base model rather than chosen, and each applied identically to both arms.
First, the \emph{chat surface}: Falcon3 was instruction-tuned on different role
markers, so each domain uses a twin of its prompt contract that shares the
system instruction and the canonical answer rewrite verbatim and differs only in
those markers; the rendering reproduces the model's own published chat template
byte for byte. Second, \emph{optimizer-state placement}: at 1.67B parameters the
weights, master copy, and Adam moments do not fit a 24\,GB device alongside a
collocated inference engine, so optimizer state is offloaded to host memory,
which changes where state lives and not the update computed. Third, two
\emph{response budgets}. Falcon3 is uniformly more verbose than
\qwenmark{}2.5-0.5B, and budgets calibrated to the latter's terseness truncate it:
measured against the paired runs before any outcome was read, the manuscript's
budget bound 44\% of Falcon rollouts on Python factors and 14\% on MathIR while
binding the \qwenmark{} cohort on 20 of 4,609 and 0 of 4,609 responses respectively.
Those two budgets were raised until truncation was negligible. Graph coloring
(6\%) and Countdown (2\%) were left at the manuscript values, and PantryPlan is
unaffected because its canonical sampler emits a fixed six-token action.

\noindent\textbf{What transfers without modification.} The canonical action
spaces resolve to the same horizon, sequence count, and maximum sequence
entropy under the new tokenizer as under the original: $3/27$ for Graph
coloring, $3/108$ for Countdown, and $6/64$ for PantryPlan. The objective is
therefore defined over the same action geometry in both families, and the
reward and validator paths are surface-independent.

\noindent\textbf{Registered prediction.} Stated before launch and deliberately
qualitative: matched Dr.GRPO's four metrics will again coincide, with
modes-per-success near one, in at least four of the five domains; \historicalt{} will
raise \texttt{distinct@8} in at least four of the five; and \historicalt{} will not
reduce \texttt{pass@8} below the matched baseline in any domain. Absolute values
are \emph{not} predicted to reproduce this paper's numbers, and no threshold on
their agreement is registered. A different base model has different task
competence; what is under test is the direction and the presence of the band
collapse, not the magnitude.

\noindent\textbf{Claim boundary.} A negative or mixed result is reportable and
will be reported. If the effect does not reproduce, the correct conclusion is
that the result as stated is contingent on the base model, and this paper's
claim will be narrowed accordingly rather than the cohort excluded. Because two
response budgets differ from the manuscript's, the replication's Python factors
and MathIR rows are not comparable to a run of the same model at the original
budgets, and the reported cohort uses the raised-budget runs throughout.

\noindent\textbf{Terminal result.} The two method-direction clauses registered
before launch are met with room to spare. \Historicalt{} raises \texttt{distinct@8} by
$+.923$, $+.475$, $+1.255$, $+.846$, and $+1.084$ modes on Graph, Countdown,
Python, MathIR, and PantryPlan, and raises \texttt{pass@8} by $+.276$, $+.300$,
$+.964$, $+.792$, and $+.145$. Both differences are positive in every paired
seed: 25 of 25 for breadth and 25 of 25 for success. PantryPlan reproduces the
central accuracy--support dissociation particularly sharply: baseline greedy
accuracy is higher ($.633$ versus $.577$), yet \historicalt{} returns $1.72$ versus
$0.63$ modes and raises \texttt{pass@8} from $.633$ to $.778$.

The baseline-collapse clause needs a narrower verdict. Countdown, MathIR, and
PantryPlan end at modes-per-success $1.06$, $1.00$, and $1.00$; Graph is the
exception at $1.42$. Python's four baseline metrics all equal zero, so its
modes-per-success is undefined. The protocol specified neither a numerical
tolerance for ``near one'' nor a convention for zero-success endpoints. Thus
the four-metric band closes qualitatively in four domains, but a strict
four-of-five verdict on the compound ratio clause is not licensed. We report
the two directional clauses as confirmed and the baseline-collapse clause as
partially unresolved, rather than choosing a post-hoc zero convention.

Per-seed rows and paired win counts are bound in
\path{var/artifacts/e73_falcon_cross_family_summary_v2.json}, which reads the
distinct-mode count \texttt{sampled\_distinct\_correct\_at\_8} and aborts if
any cell violates Lemma~\ref{lem:sample-ordering}. Absolute agreement with Qwen was not
predicted, and these fixed-recipe results are robustness evidence rather than a
tuned estimate for Falcon.

\noindent\textbf{What the trajectories add.} Two things the endpoint table
cannot show. First, the baseline's breadth is not merely lower, it is
\emph{non-monotone}: on Graph coloring it peaks near $1.2$ modes at pass four
and falls by a third thereafter, and on MathIR and PantryPlan it turns down
before the terminal endpoint. A method that had simply never found the
modes would produce a flat line, not a hump. Second, the two arms separate early
and stay separated: there is no depth at which the baseline is catching up, so
the gap at pass twelve is not an artifact of stopping there.

Python factors is the limiting rescue: the baseline remains at zero while
\Historicalt{} reaches $.897$ greedy accuracy, $.964$ \texttt{pass@8}, and $1.26$
modes. MathIR supplies the other limiting trajectory, with all baseline metrics
falling to $.070$ as the treatment continues upward. These are terminal shapes,
not an early-checkpoint selection.

and the shared frozen weak warm start.
