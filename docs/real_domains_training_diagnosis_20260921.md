# Training and evaluation diagnosis, September 21, 2026

The larger coding and Reuters studies are **on hold**. Their frozen protocols and original pilot evidence are preserved. Reserved test questions have not been used for model training, model evaluation or cohort tuning. The user authorized additional GPU time, with correctness before expansion.

## What the audit found

1. **A real coding scoring defect.** The pilot recomputed old-policy probabilities with every response padded to the group’s longest sequence; the production learner trimmed each microbatch. BF16 inference therefore produced different probabilities before any parameter update. A zero-learning-rate GPU test on the same checkpoint and a mixed coding group found differences on 644/1,263 response tokens, mean absolute log-probability difference 0.00302005 and maximum +0.159584. Matching the scoring widths made every response-token difference exactly zero. This warrants correction, but does not establish the cause of Re:Max’s diversity deficit. The v1 probe’s FP32 gradient-angle reduction returned an impossible cosine above one; those gradient comparison fields are invalid and superseded by a separate float64 probe. Independent blockwise float64 recomputation of its saved vectors found a 3.2262% full-versus-trimmed gradient difference and 0.4785% variation between repeated trimmed runs; this is one group/checkpoint, and the latter variation remains unexplained. Its saved token-score comparison is unaffected.

2. **No replay sign or accumulation-scaling failure.** An independent literal-gradient reference passed 29 cases at group size 16, spanning microbatch sizes, empty and nonempty banks, mixed and constant reward groups, clipping, and multi-step AdamW. Maximum pre-clipping gradient discrepancy was 1.57e-9. Replay’s effective coefficient is 0.1×15/256=0.005859375 as documented; no accidental extra division was found.

3. **The original Reuters pilot could not test reward learning.** All 32 groups had constant rewards: 30 all-correct and two all-incorrect. MaxRL’s fresh gradient was zero throughout and its adapter stayed identical to initialization. More training on precisely that saturated sample is not an informative MaxRL/Re:Max comparison.

4. **Coding was short and coverage-limited.** Thirty-two updates meant four visits per question. Fifteen replay steps used singleton banks, with no within-bank diversity to balance; only 17 used multiple modes. The untrained development questions never produced more than one accepted mode per question in either arm. Most of the untrained ED@32 difference came from one question with two versus one accepted samples out of 128.

5. **Train/eval numerics matter at this effect size.** Checkpoint seals, adapter loading/scaling, EOS, vocabulary masks and metric formulas passed the independent audit. Nevertheless, HF training uses FP32 LoRA matrices whereas the endpoint loads BF16 matrices, and residual engine differences remain after matching adapter dtype. On identical coding bank tokens, cross-engine treatment-contrast MAE was 0.001605 nats/token versus a signed native-HF contrast of +0.000862; for Reuters it was 0.043378 versus +0.010911. The corrected development comparison uses the training engine. These observations do not prove that backend differences caused the sampled diversity result.

## What replay actually changed

On the fixed union of both arms’ observed coding bank exemplars, Re:Max increased average exemplar likelihood more than MaxRL. Equal weighting by prompt, then canonical key, then exemplar gives a step-32 advantage of +0.00110008 nats/token, positive on six of seven banked prompts. This verifies an active mechanism; exemplar likelihood is not the total probability of the corresponding canonical mode.

A separate same-HF Reuters diagnostic enumerated all six single-letter answers and both generation EOS tokens on all 48 original endpoint questions. It retained unenumerated output probability as an explicit residual. On the 16 training questions, the resulting full-output ED@32 difference bound was negative: Re:Max−MaxRL between −0.006269 and −0.004046. On the 32 development questions, the bound was −0.009455 to +0.030872. These bounds retain missing output mass for the fixed HF teacher-forced scoring implementation. They do not enclose every cache/batch/kernel variant and are not confidence intervals over training seeds. They do not establish a positive diversity effect.

The old Reuters decrease has a concrete discovery limitation. Decomposing its mean enumerated ED@32 change (−0.00523496) gives +0.00000557 from banked modes and −0.00524053 from valid modes absent from the bank. For example, one bank retained “wheat” but not “grain”; grain’s scored probability fell from 0.00522011 to 0.00407012. Five multimode-bank controls had nearly flat ED@32, while ten singleton-bank questions accounted for the decrease. Rehearsing a discovered answer can reduce undiscovered valid alternatives. The new eight-question pilot deliberately tests retention after discovery; it is not an unbiased estimate of benefit over all Reuters questions.

An additional population-PCMD calculation on the same old probabilities gives a different, compatible result: the training Re:Max advantage is bounded by +0.011507 to +0.011689, while development is unresolved (−0.000013 to +0.003316). PCMD measures balance conditional on correctness; ED@32 also detects rare valid answers. The old pilot therefore showed improved common-answer balance alongside worse rare-answer coverage. This additional diagnostic retains the original success and ED metrics and does not replace them.

## Completed correction checks

A separate versioned trainer now matches old/live scoring widths, with the original trainer and frozen runs unchanged. Nine CPU regressions pass, including rejection of old-schema checkpoints. The first mixed coding GPU update passes in both arms: exact-zero score differences and clipping, nonzero fresh gradients, and replay applied only to Re:Max. The full eight-update coding pair subsequently passed independent raw-token/verifier/bank/checkpoint auditing: both had seven mixed groups and one all-wrong group, exact-zero old/live differences and clipping on all updates, matched initialization, and correctly isolated replay gradients. Both jobs completed successfully; the pair cost 0.35 GPU-hours. This was an execution check, not an efficacy experiment. The corrected Reuters development pilot keeps native annotations and selects eight training questions by treatment-independent baseline criteria: all three baseline-mixed questions in the existing 128-question training screen, plus all five baseline-multimode controls from the original 16. The 128-update pair completed after the corrected-path gate passed (jobs 31440351 and 31440352), costing 0.76222 GPU-hours. Its independent audit passed all 256 updates: exact-zero old/live score differences and clipping, matched initialization, verified source labels and raw responses, and correct fresh/replay gradients. Both arms discovered 16 modes across eight questions by update 16; each mode received 16 replay exposures. Both arms were scored at updates 32, 64 and 128 on the same 40 questions (eight training and 32 development), with update 128 declared the primary diagnostic endpoint. The fixed 32-question development split and reserved test split remain unchanged.

The next efficacy question is whether longer, active reward learning produces concentration that replay can prevent. The completed Reuters pilot still approached the reward ceiling quickly: only four MaxRL groups and five Re:Max groups had mixed rewards out of 128 per arm. Its predeclared requirement that every baseline-mixed training question actually produce mixed groups failed: the grain/wheat question was always all-correct. This limitation cannot be erased by a favorable diversity result. All 128 replay updates used two discovered modes from the same prompt as the fresh group; this enriched cohort removes the original singleton/discovery regime. Cohort, duration and corrected scoring changed together, so comparison with the original pilot cannot isolate any one cause. MaxRL had four updates with nonzero fresh gradients, although optimizer momentum can still move parameters on subsequent zero-gradient steps. A favorable result is a hypothesis, not a filter for retaining runs. All null and negative outcomes remain part of the record. A wider study and paper inclusion remain deferred.

## Corrected 128-update Reuters result

The fixed final checkpoint gives a positive retention result in this one paired seed. Independent recomputation passed all 3,360 score records, native labels and prompts, checkpoint seals, four declared cohorts and all checkpoint contrasts, using a separate PCMD bound calculation. All 40 predeclared questions and all seven policies (base and both arms at 32, 64, 128) are included. These values come from native-HF teacher-forced probabilities of all six letters and both EOS endings, retaining unenumerated output probability explicitly.

| Fixed cohort at update 128 | MaxRL population PCMD | Re:Max population PCMD | MaxRL ED@32 | Re:Max ED@32 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Eight training questions | 0.237225–0.237267 | 0.494200–0.494202 | 1.813943–1.814708 | 1.999999992–2.000000000 |
| 32 untrained development questions | 0.067776–0.067865 | 0.086028–0.086046 | 1.320864–1.322439 | 1.340775–1.341089 |

PCMD is the probability that two independently sampled correct answers have different native topics; ED@32 is the expected number of distinct correct topics in 32 unconditional samples. Larger is better for both. The intervals are bounds for unresolved output probability under this fixed numerical scoring implementation, **not training-seed confidence intervals**, and do not enclose all cache/batch/kernel variants. Both final policies have mean correctness lower bounds above 99.99% in both cohorts. The tiny correctness contrast remains unresolved; no accuracy advantage or exact equivalence is claimed.

The final Re:Max−MaxRL ED@32 difference is bounded by +0.185292 to +0.186057 on training and +0.018335 to +0.020225 on development. Population-PCMD differences are +0.256933 to +0.256978 and +0.018163 to +0.018271, respectively. Both diversity contrasts are positive at all three fixed checkpoints; the largest earlier checkpoint is not substituted for the declared endpoint. In the predeclared secondary split, the three baseline-mixed training questions contribute ED@32 +0.486066 to +0.487904, and the five retention controls +0.004828 to +0.004949. The failed fresh-learning-activity gate described above still applies.

The native baseline training ED@32 bound was 1.998931–2.000000. All eight training questions have exactly two native gold topics, and both banks discovered all 16 topics by update 16. Thus the result supports **preserving and balancing already discovered valid answers as MaxRL concentrates**, rather than discovering new answers. On development, Re:Max improves over MaxRL, but its ED@32 change from the base model is unresolved (−0.019111 to +0.002042) because baseline unenumerated output mass is larger. Population PCMD improves over both MaxRL and the base model in this fixed development cohort.

The separate sampled development check agrees in direction: all 1,024 final responses per arm were correct; mean distinct correct topics in 32 samples were 1.3125 for MaxRL and 1.40625 for Re:Max. These finite samples are noisier than the probability diagnostic and their larger observed contrast is not used to replace it. One seed, baseline-enriched training selection, rapid reward saturation, fully discovered training support, and reused development questions limit the result. It is a credible mechanism pilot on native human labels, not yet a generalization claim for Reuters or an appendix-ready multi-seed comparison.

## Coding128 execution stopped for a second correctness defect

The user authorized the same eight hardened training questions and13 existing development questions for128 updates per arm. The native-HF evaluator and corrected training path passed CPU and tiny GPU validation, and the fixed protocol was frozen. The first larger baseline shard then exposed an execution-contract defect before any coding efficacy result was available: a valid randomized program can produce different accepted witness vectors on repeated execution, violating the stable canonical-key requirement.

On `1454_A`, preserved baseline samples232 and264 use `random.shuffle` until they construct a derangement. Independent CPU replay reproduced the hard failure three times for each program: both executions passed all15 tests, but their canonical keys differed. A deterministic accepted control retained its key in all three repetitions. The sandbox never seeded the candidate program's `random` module. Its intended `PYTHONHASHSEED=0` was also ineffective because Python was launched with `-I`, which ignores Python environment variables. Model sampling seeds do not seed the separate candidate interpreter.

The controller correctly stopped new submissions on the verifier violation. The remaining four GPU jobs were stopped on2026-09-22 at02:21UTC, preserving all raw samples, training records, the available32-update checkpoint, original manifests and failed allocation evidence. This campaign supplies no completed efficacy comparison. Its eight allocations, including validation, the earlier startup preemption, the failed baseline shard and canceled jobs, used2.26778 GPU-hours; cumulative prior-plus-current usage is7.47806 GPU-hours. See the [terminal accounting](../var/artifacts/real_domains_pilot_20260921/code_corrected128_execution_20260922/aborted_terminal_accounting.json) and [reproduction receipt](../var/artifacts/real_domains_pilot_20260921/code_corrected128_execution_20260922/nondeterministic_program_diagnosis_20260922_v1/summary.json).

The correction in progress uses a separately versioned candidate launcher with an effective fixed hash seed and an arm-independent standard-library random seed. It preserves the candidate's source, kernel isolation and resource limits. It does not promise to control explicit OS entropy, self-reseeding or every possible source of nondeterminism; the independent full-suite stability check remains required. Before a fresh comparison, the exact same questions, checkers and input suites must pass their source-program controls under the new execution contract, followed by corrected-path GPU validation. Fresh replay banks and a distinct suite identity prevent mixing old and new canonical labels. No failed question or sampled program is silently dropped or replaced.

The revised source audit has passed all 504 labeled reference programs across the same 21 questions. The separate production-verifier audit has also passed: all 252 positive references remained accepted with stable keys across two full executions each, and all 512 preserved baseline draws were regraded without hard violations or exclusions (75 accepted, 437 ordinary rejections). Samples 232 and 264 now have stable keys. These regraded draws are diagnostic evidence, not fresh pilot endpoints. The first seeded launcher also exposed a compatibility issue with Python `exit`/`quit`; that CPU-only failure is preserved, and the revised launcher restores the pinned standard site builtins without enabling site initialization.

The user subsequently requested `node105` in the dedicated `mltheory` partition. Slurm reports ten idle A5000 GPUs there; a CPU-only allocation verified about 3 TB free in `/tmp` and 2 TB free in shared project storage. All unsubmitted A6000 preparations are retained and superseded by new node105 requests. Slurm routes short validation jobs through `all` despite the requested `mltheory` partition, while keeping the explicit `node105` constraint; the full jobs use `mltheory`. Earlier training peaks were close to the A5000 memory budget, so a separate bounded hardware check and the unchanged eight-update validation must establish fit before the full pilot. This changes the requested allocation, not the question set, loss, model, sample budget or endpoint rule.

The target diagnostic remains fixed at128 updates, with512 samples per training question and128 per development question for the shared base and final endpoints;32/64 are descriptive checks. Broader studies remain on hold. The original27-GPU-hour projection and47-hour allocation envelope were planning values for the interrupted protocol; new operational accounting will explicitly retain its cost.

After this fixed diagnostic, freeze a replication protocol with additional paired seeds before widening the question pool or evaluating reserved test questions. Report correctness, conditional diversity and rare-answer coverage together; preserve null and negative runs. The current QA pilot establishes a useful retention mechanism and the coding pilot should determine whether that benefit survives sustained reward learning.
